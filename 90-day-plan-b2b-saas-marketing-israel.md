# The 90-Day Plan: Landing Your First Performance Marketing Role in Israeli B2B SaaS
### Ops spike edition — built on first principles, mental models, and the three books

---

## 0. First principles: what actually gets a junior hired

Strip hiring down to its atoms. A hiring manager at an Israeli SaaS company says yes to a junior when three beliefs form in their head:

1. **"This person can operate our stack with minimal hand-holding"** → proven by *work*, not certificates alone
2. **"This is a low-risk hire"** → proven by *visible evidence* and *someone vouching*
3. **"They'll make my life easier next week"** → proven by *specificity* (you speak their language: MQLs, attribution, CPL)

Israeli tech hiring runs disproportionately on referrals — most companies literally pay employees "חבר מביא חבר" bonuses because internal referrals beat every other channel. Cold applications into an ATS are the worst-odds channel, yet it's where most juniors spend 90% of their effort.

**Inversion (Munger):** ask why juniors *fail* to get hired — generic CV, no portfolio, "aspiring marketer" positioning, spread thin across five specialties, applying cold. The plan is simply the systematic opposite.

**Law 23 — Concentrate Your Forces:** "You gain more by finding a rich mine and mining it deeper than by flitting from one shallow mine to another — intensity defeats extensity every time." Your rich mine: **marketing ops, B2B SaaS, Israel, companies of ~20–300 employees.** One arrow, one target, for 90 days.

**Why ops is the right spike (validating your choice):** every Israeli SaaS company runs HubSpot or Salesforce/Marketo and drowns in messy data; juniors chase "creative" and "strategy," so ops is chronically under-supplied; ops output is measurable, which makes proof-of-work easy to demonstrate; and it's the highest-leverage seat from which to grow into demand gen or growth roles. Supply–demand asymmetry is your edge.

So the entire 90 days builds exactly three assets:

| Asset | Why it matters | Built in |
|---|---|---|
| **Proof of work** (projects) | Evidence replaces pedigree | Month 2 |
| **Distribution** (LinkedIn) | So the proof reaches the right ~200 people | Month 1, compounds all 90 days |
| **Trust** (communities & relationships) | So applications become referrals | Month 1 onward |

## 1. The operating system (the Atomic Habits layer)

**Identity first.** Clear's core argument: every action is a vote for the type of person you wish to become, and behavior incongruent with identity doesn't last. So stop "trying to get a job" — from today you *are a marketing ops professional who isn't hired yet.* Decisions simplify: "What would a marketing ops person do today?" (They'd check a dashboard. They'd read a teardown. They wouldn't doomscroll job boards.)

**Systems over goals.** Winners and losers have the same goals — every candidate wants the job. The system is what differentiates. Your daily system (~1.5–2 hrs, adjust to your reality):

- **Skill block (45–60 min):** courses and tool practice. *Habit-stack it:* "After my morning coffee, I open HubSpot Academy."
- **Build block (30 min):** move the current project forward, however small.
- **Network block (15 min):** 3 thoughtful comments on Israeli SaaS marketers' posts + 1 DM or follow-up.

Weekly: 2 LinkedIn posts (e.g., Mon/Thu), 1 coffee chat booked, and a 20-minute Friday review of your metrics (below).

**Environment design:** put LinkedIn and HubSpot Academy on your phone's home screen; unfollow noise and follow ~30 Israeli SaaS marketing leaders so your feed becomes your classroom. **Make it satisfying:** keep a visible tracker and don't break the chain. **Two-minute rule** for bad days: minimum viable day = one lesson + one comment. Never zero.

**Expect the valley of disappointment.** Weeks 3–7 will feel like nothing is happening — no replies, no interviews. That's the plateau of latent potential: the plan deliberately front-loads invisible work (skills, relationships) that pays out in month 3. Trust the system, review the leading indicators, keep voting.

## 2. Month 1 (Weeks 1–4): Build the spike

### 2.1 Skills and certifications (all free)

- **HubSpot Academy:** Marketing Software certification, then the lessons on lifecycle stages, lead scoring, and email nurturing. **Create a free HubSpot portal — this becomes your sandbox and later your portfolio.**
- **Google Skillshop:** GA4 certification + Google Ads Search certification. Explore the GA4 demo account (real e-commerce data to practice on).
- **LinkedIn Marketing Labs:** fundamentals + reporting courses — LinkedIn Ads is *the* B2B SaaS paid channel; knowing Campaign Manager is a differentiator.
- **Spreadsheets & dashboards:** pivot tables, XLOOKUP, funnel math in Sheets; build one dashboard in Looker Studio.
- **Automation:** Zapier or Make — build 2–3 simple automations (form → CRM → Slack notification).

**Concepts to be fluent in** (one page of notes each): the MQL→SQL→Opportunity→Closed-Won funnel; CAC, LTV, payback period; attribution models (first-touch, last-touch, multi-touch — and why everyone argues about them); UTM taxonomy and hygiene; lead scoring; CRM lifecycle stages.

**End-of-month deliverable:** certs done + a one-page "How a B2B SaaS funnel actually works" explainer written in your own words. If you can't explain it simply, you don't know it yet (Feynman test).

### 2.2 The LinkedIn transformation (do it in Week 1–2, don't perfect it)

Recruiters find you through **keyword search**, not adjectives. "Aspiring marketer seeking opportunities" is invisible; tool names are magnets.

- **Headline formula:** *[Role you're becoming] | [Stack] | [Value you create].* Example: **"Marketing Operations & Performance Marketing | HubSpot · GA4 · LinkedIn Ads | Building measurable B2B SaaS funnels."** This is Law 25 — Re-Create Yourself: forge the identity deliberately instead of letting your past define you.
- **About (3 short first-person paragraphs):** (1) the pivot story — why marketing ops, what pulled you in; (2) what you're building right now — learning in public, the projects; (3) exactly what you want — a junior ops/performance role at an Israeli B2B SaaS. Specific beats polished.
- **Featured section:** will hold your three projects (empty now, full by Week 10 — that's fine).
- **Experience:** reframe every past role through a data/process/ownership lens. Every job contains funnel thinking — service work is retention, sales is conversion, admin is ops. Numbers wherever honest.
- **Skills:** pin Marketing Operations, HubSpot, Google Analytics, Paid Social. Add certifications as you earn them.
- **Settings:** location = your actual district in Israel; industry = Software Development; "Open to Work" visible to recruiters only.
- **Language:** post in English (Israeli tech is English-first online and it widens your reach); engage in Hebrew where it's natural.
- **Content cadence (2 posts/week, from Week 3):** document, don't lecture. "What I learned building my first lead-scoring model," "3 things the GA4 demo account taught me about attribution." Learning-in-public from a junior is *likable* — it's Law 6 (court attention) without pretending to be a guru, and it makes you familiar before you're introduced.

### 2.3 Communities (join Week 1, give before you take)

- **מנהלי שיווק מצייצים** (Facebook) — the biggest Israeli marketing community; jobs, questions, and visibility all flow through it.
- **דיגיטל וזהו** (Facebook) — practitioner-level digital marketing talk.
- **Digital Marketing Israel** (Facebook, English-speaking) — useful if your Hebrew is still building.
- **Startup for Startup** (Monday.com's community + podcast) — the Israeli SaaS operator brain-trust; make the marketing/growth episodes your commute listening.
- **Secret Tel Aviv + Israeli startup jobs groups** — for postings and market awareness.
- **Startup Nation Finder** (Startup Nation Central's free database) — not a community, but your tool for building the target-company list in Month 3.
- **Meetups:** search Meetup, Eventbrite, and LinkedIn Events for TLV marketing/growth/SaaS gatherings; attend at least one per month. WhatsApp groups are where the real-time action is — invite links circulate inside the Facebook groups; ask once you're a familiar name.

*(Community names and invite links shift — verify inside the groups themselves.)*

**The Carnegie protocol** — the entire networking strategy is his line: *"You can make more friends in two months by becoming genuinely interested in other people than you can in two years by trying to get other people interested in you."* Operationally:

1. **Weeks 1–2: only give.** Answer what you can, ask good specific questions, appreciate people's posts *genuinely and specifically* (Carnegie is emphatic that appreciation is not flattery — one is sincere, one is transparent).
2. **Talk in terms of the other person's interests.** Ask marketers about *their* stack, *their* attribution headaches. Be interested, not interesting.
3. **Remember names and details.** Follow up the next day referencing something specific they said. In a noisy community, the person who remembers is unforgettable.
4. **Be a good listener.** In coffee chats, aim to talk 30% of the time. People walk away from conversations where *they* talked rating *you* brilliant.

## 3. Month 2 (Weeks 5–8): Proof of work — the projects

**Law 9 — Win through your actions, never through argument.** A junior *saying* "I'm analytical" is noise. A junior *showing* a funnel they built is signal. Four projects, escalating in publicness.

### Project 1 — Build a complete demand-gen engine (Weeks 5–6)
Invent a fictional SaaS (or better — use a friend's real business; real beats fictional). In your free HubSpot portal, build the full chain: **landing page → form → lead scoring model → lifecycle stages → 4-email nurture sequence → Looker Studio dashboard** wired to the funnel data.

- **Proves:** you can operate the exact stack in the job description.
- **How to learn it:** HubSpot Academy walks through every piece. This is *just-in-time learning* — pick the project first, learn only what the next step demands. Courses are reference material, not the path. Stuck >30 minutes → search → still stuck → ask in a community. (Specific, real questions build relationships — Carnegie again.)

### Project 2 — Spend real money (Weeks 6–7)
₪400–800 total. Promote something real: a friend's business, a nonprofit, or your own lead magnet — e.g., *"The Israeli SaaS Marketing Stack: 20 tools compared"* (meta-move: the lead magnet's audience **is your future hiring managers**). Run Google Search + one LinkedIn or Meta campaign. Document hypotheses → results → iterations: CTR, CPC, CPL, what you killed and why.

- **Proves:** *skin in the game.* Every interviewer can smell the difference between "I did the cert" and "I burned real shekels learning that broad match eats budgets."

### Project 3 — Public teardowns (Weeks 7–8) — highest-leverage project
Pick 3 Israeli B2B SaaS companies (Series A–C — big enough to have real funnels, small enough that your post reaches their actual team). For each: sign up for the trial/demo, capture the **ad → landing page → email flow** (the LinkedIn Ad Library and Meta Ad Library show their live ads), audit UTM hygiene, form friction, nurture cadence, and message match. Publish on LinkedIn: *"I analyzed [Company]'s demand-gen funnel — 3 things I loved and 3 tests I'd run."*

- **Tone is everything.** Law 1 (Never Outshine the Master) + Carnegie (don't criticize, condemn or complain) → frame as **admiration + curiosity + "tests I'd run,"** never "what they're doing wrong." Companies reshare flattering-but-smart teardowns; their marketers DM you. This exact play has gotten juniors hired more than any other content format.
- Tag people thoughtfully; never spam.

### Project 4 — The meta-project (starts Week 8, runs through Month 3)
**Run your job search inside your HubSpot portal.** Target companies as deals; pipeline stages (identified → contacted → conversation → interview → offer); email sequences; a dashboard on top. Then post about it. *"I'm running my job search as a HubSpot pipeline"* is irresistible proof-of-craft to exactly the people you want noticing — and it forces you to actually use the tool like an operator.

**Package all of it** in a simple Notion portfolio; link it in your LinkedIn Featured section. Each project = 1–2 LinkedIn posts. By Week 9 your profile shows a body of work most *employed* juniors don't have.

## 4. Month 3 (Weeks 9–12): Run the search like a performance campaign

You are now the product. Apply the discipline you've been learning:

**ICP:** Israeli B2B SaaS, ~20–300 employees (big enough to hire juniors, small enough that ops people wear many hats). Target titles: *Marketing Operations Specialist, Demand Gen Associate, PPC/Performance Marketing Specialist, Growth Analyst, Marketing Associate.* Build a 40–60 company list via Startup Nation Finder, LinkedIn, and funding announcements (Geektime, Calcalist/CTech).

**Channel mix (deliberately inverted from what most juniors do):**
- **70% — referrals & warm outreach** through the relationships and visibility you've built
- **20% — direct-to-hiring-manager** (find the marketing lead, not HR)
- **10% — job boards/ATS** (apply, but never *only* apply)

**Outreach protocol** — Law 13: *when asking for help, appeal to people's self-interest, never to their mercy or gratitude.* Nobody owes you a job and "I'm looking for opportunities" triggers nothing. Instead:

1. Open with **them**: a specific comment on their post, campaign, or talk — or a genuinely useful observation from your teardown work.
2. Ask for **15 minutes of advice, not a job.** People love giving advice; it flatters and invests them in your success. Then Carnegie's "let the other person feel the idea is theirs" does the heavy lifting — the referral becomes *their* idea, which makes it ten times stronger.
3. Volume: **5 personalized touches/day, 2–3 coffee chats/week.** Track reply rates like CPL and iterate your messaging weekly like ad copy.

**Interviews:**
- Have a 10-minute portfolio walkthrough ready: *problem → what I built → what I learned → the numbers.*
- Israeli companies love take-home assignments — your projects **are** pre-built take-homes; reference them.
- **Law 4 — Always say less than necessary:** answer, show, stop. Don't fill silence with qualifiers that shrink you.
- **Carnegie — you can't win an argument:** never fight an interviewer's framing. "Interesting — here's how I'd test that" wins every time that "actually, I disagree" loses.

**Optional accelerator — Law 28, Enter Action with Boldness:** offer one target company a free two-week ops sprint (UTM cleanup, a dashboard build, a lead-scoring audit). It's the cheapest possible way for them to discover they need you — and boldness is admired while timidity is invisible.

## 5. The weekly scoreboard (Friday, 20 minutes)

| Metric | Healthy signal | If it's broken |
|---|---|---|
| Learning blocks completed | 5/week | Shrink the block (two-minute rule), don't skip it |
| Posts published | 2/week | Lower the bar — document, don't perfect |
| Meaningful comments/DMs | 15+/week | Schedule the 15-min block; it's the first thing dropped |
| Outreach reply rate (Month 3) | >15% | Message problem — rewrite like failing ad copy |
| Coffee chats | 2–3/week (Month 3) | Ask problem — advice, not jobs |
| Chats → interviews | Some conversion by Week 11 | Positioning problem — sharpen the spike |

Diagnose it like a funnel, because it is one.
