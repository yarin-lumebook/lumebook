01 — SOURCING

Where to find them

One move beats every funding database: prospect from companies already running ads. The budget is approved and flowing, and your opener is a critique of ads they're paying for right now — the most concrete cold message that exists. You're not selling the channel. You're showing them their own ads and what's wrong.

Start here every time

Meta Ad Library (facebook.com/ads/library) and Google Ads Transparency Center (adstransparency.google.com). Both free, both public, no login. They show exactly who is spending, on what creative, right now. Every other source tells you a company might need marketing; these two tell you it's already buying the category.

Israel, honestly

Israel is your priority market — proximity, referral density, direct relevance to where you want to be hired — so aim for ~half your pipeline there. But Israeli seed skews cyber/enterprise/defence, exactly where the CPA math fails. Expect the local market to produce your relationships and the US/remote PLG market to produce your results. A US PLG case study presented to an Israeli hiring manager transfers fully; a stalled Israeli enterprise account doesn't.

03 — DISQUALIFY FAST

The four hard gates

Binary checks. A company that fails any one is disqualified regardless of how attractive it looks. Run these before you spend a minute on personalisation — it's the cheapest filter you have.

GATE 1

Live conversion event

Something a stranger can complete today with no sales call: signup, trial, install, or booked demo. No exceptions.

GATE 2

≥ $2,000/mo spend capacity

Below this, no channel produces interpretable data. One client at $8k beats four at $1k.

GATE 3

Founder / CMO reachable directly

If the decision-maker sits behind a gatekeeper, the deal cycle exceeds your pilot window.

GATE 4

No in-house paid specialist

If they already have a performance marketer, you're a threat, not a gift. Check LinkedIn People first.

Why free attracts the wrong companies

Adverse selection: the founders who say yes fastest to free work from an unproven operator are systematically the ones who can't produce a result — no money, no product-market fit, no tracking, or already turned down by people who charge. Ease of yes is inversely correlated with portfolio value. The gates and the score exist to fight this pull. Having criteria and declining bad fits is also what raises your status from "asking for a chance" to "a professional doing selective work."

04 — THE ARITHMETIC TEST

Will the math even work?

The single most decisive "worth my time" check. Meta needs roughly 50 optimisation events per ad set per week to exit the learning phase; below that, delivery is unstable and results are statistically uninterpretable — no case study, only noise. So the conversion event's friction, not the company's prestige, decides everything.

Minimum daily budget per ad set = (Target CPA × 50) ÷ 7

Read the verdict column, then decide

This isn't an argument against cyber or fintech as sectors — it's about conversion-event friction. A fintech with a self-serve free tier is an excellent target; a fintech selling a $60k contract through a demo form is not, no matter how recently it raised. If the event they'd have you optimise sits in the top two rows and their budget is startup-sized, walk away regardless of everything else.

The stacking rule — the one to actually memorise

Individual signals are weak predictors. Stacked signals are strong ones. A company that (a) added a free tier 30 days ago, (b) runs two stale Meta creatives, and (c) has had a Growth Marketer role open 50 days isn't three weak leads — it's one near-certain conversation. Require a minimum of three signals, at least one from Group A, before a company enters your list. This single rule does more for your reply rate than any copywriting.

The override rule

Two gates beat the total score every time. If conversion-event friction scores 0–2, drop the company — no conversion event means no case study, and no weighting changes that. If budget scores 0–2, drop it too. A 78-point company with a $500/mo budget eats the same 40 hours as a 78-point company with $10k and produces nothing you can show anyone. Weighted averages hide binary failures; these two gates catch them.

What you're actually collecting

You're not building an agency. You're accumulating three artefacts: a live ad account you can screenshot, a founder who'll vouch for you on a call, and a written explanation of a decision you made with someone else's money. Everything here is machinery for producing those three. If a company can't produce them, the score already told you — move on without guilt.
