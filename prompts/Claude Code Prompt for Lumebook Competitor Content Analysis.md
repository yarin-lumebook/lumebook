You are acting as a senior DTC content strategist and growth marketer.

I am the SMM/content manager for Lumebook, a B2C/DTC-first brand.

Your task is to help me build a rigorous competitor analysis focused specifically on competitors’ CONTENT SYSTEMS, not just their branding, follower counts, or individual viral posts.

The goal is to understand:

1. What content competitors repeatedly create
2. Which content patterns appear to perform best
3. Which audiences/personas they speak to
4. Which hooks, angles, formats, creators, and proof mechanisms they use
5. How their organic content supports awareness, consideration, conversion, and retention
6. How they use UGC, creator content, customer proof, demos, education, entertainment, and product-led content
7. What competitors are overusing
8. What they are underusing
9. Where Lumebook has an opportunity to differentiate
10. Which content hypotheses Lumebook should actually test

I do NOT want a shallow social media audit.

Do not give me observations like:
“Competitor X posts 4 times per week”
unless the observation leads to a strategic implication.

Think in terms of:
Research → positioning → personas → content pillars → angles → formats → production → distribution → measurement → iteration.

## Step 1: Competitor structure

Help me classify competitors into:

- Direct competitors: same or highly similar product/problem
- Adjacent competitors: different product, similar customer or use case
- Best-in-class DTC/content brands: not necessarily direct competitors, but useful benchmarks for content systems

Recommend an appropriate number of brands to analyze deeply. Prioritize depth over quantity.

## Step 4: Performance normalization

Do not compare posts based only on raw views.

Create useful normalized metrics, including where appropriate:

View efficiency = views / followers

Engagement rate by views = (likes + comments) / views

Also suggest better qualitative indicators for content that drives business outcomes, such as:

- buying-intent comments
- product questions
- price questions
- friend tagging
- profile visits
- link clicks
- saves
- shares
- branded search
- creator code usage
- conversion data if available

Explain the limitations of public social data and do not pretend we can infer sales from engagement alone.

Create four buckets:

## Step 10: Build testable hypotheses

Turn the strongest findings into experiments.

Each experiment should include:

- hypothesis
- customer insight
- content pillar
- persona
- angle
- format
- hook
- creator type
- CTA
- number of pieces to produce
- testing period
- primary metric
- secondary metrics
- what result would support the hypothesis
- what result would reject it
- what to test next

Example structure:

Hypothesis:
Raw creator-led demonstrations will outperform polished branded product videos for cold audiences.

Reason:
Competitor analysis shows stronger relative engagement and higher-intent comments on creator-led demonstration content.

Test:
6 creator-led Reels vs 6 polished product-led Reels.

Primary metric:
Qualified profile visits or landing-page clicks.

Secondary metrics:
3-second hold rate, completion rate, saves, shares, buying-intent comments.

## Working principles

Be analytical and skeptical.

Do not confuse:
- correlation with causation
- virality with business performance
- engagement with conversion
- format with strategy
- aesthetics with effectiveness

If data is missing, say so.

If you cannot support a conclusion, label it as a hypothesis rather than a fact.

Whenever possible, compare multiple examples before drawing a conclusion.

The final output should help me answer:

“What content system should Lumebook build, why should it build it, and what should we test first?”

Start by proposing the exact research workflow and database structure before performing any deeper analysis.
