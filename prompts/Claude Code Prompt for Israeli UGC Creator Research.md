You are acting as a senior creator partnerships researcher and DTC growth strategist.

I am building a UGC/creator pipeline for Lumebook, a B2C/DTC-first brand targeting parents and families in Israel.

Your task is to research and identify ONLY Israeli creators with approximately 1,000 to 50,000 followers who could produce credible UGC or creator-led content for Lumebook.

Do not include celebrities, large influencers, generic lifestyle influencers with no clear relevance, or accounts above 50,000 followers unless they are an exceptional edge case and clearly labeled separately.

The objective is not simply to find influencers with reach.

The objective is to find creators who possess the trust, context, expertise, audience fit, lived experience, or professional authority that could make Lumebook believable and useful to Israeli parents.

## Geographic and audience constraints

Creators must be based in Israel or clearly create primarily for an Israeli audience.

Prioritize:
- Hebrew-speaking creators
- Israeli parent audiences
- Israeli education/child-development communities
- local relevance

Secondary consideration:
- creators producing in English, Russian, Arabic, or other languages if they have a meaningful Israeli parent audience

Follower range:
- minimum: approximately 1,000
- maximum: approximately 50,000

Prefer micro-creators between approximately 2,000 and 25,000 when audience quality and niche relevance are strong.

## Platforms

Research primarily:
- Instagram
- TikTok

Secondary:
- YouTube
- relevant public Facebook creator pages

Do not use follower count alone as a quality signal.

## Scoring

Score each creator from 1–5 on:

1. Audience fit
2. Creator-brand fit
3. Parent trust
4. Content quality
5. Authenticity
6. UGC potential
7. Educational/professional credibility
8. On-camera ability
9. Commercial usability
10. Likelihood of being affordable for a small DTC brand

Then calculate an overall weighted score.

Do NOT over-weight follower count.

Follower count should be a secondary consideration.

Audience relevance and content fit matter more.

## Estimate collaboration practicality

Where possible, classify creators roughly as:

- likely gifted-only
- gifted + affiliate
- low-cost paid micro creator
- standard paid micro creator
- potentially expensive relative to size
- unknown

Do not fabricate rates.

Only estimate category-level practicality based on observed creator size, professionalism, sponsorship history, and niche.

Clearly label estimates as estimates.

## Competitor / brand-collaboration research

For each promising creator, check whether they have already worked with:
- children's products
- educational products
- parenting brands
- toy brands
- books
- learning products
- family apps
- DTC family products

Identify obvious conflicts or category saturation.

Do not automatically reject creators who have worked with competitors.

Instead classify:
- no apparent conflict
- adjacent collaboration history
- potential competitor conflict
- unclear

## Content concept generation

For every shortlisted creator, propose one specific Lumebook content concept tailored to that creator.

Do not give everyone the same script.

Examples:

Parent:
“Show the moment you normally struggle to keep your child meaningfully engaged, then naturally introduce Lumebook.”

Kindergarten teacher:
“Show three ways you would use Lumebook with children in an educational setting.”

Speech therapist:
“Explain one language-development principle and demonstrate how a parent could turn Lumebook into a conversation activity.”

Screen-free creator:
“Include Lumebook in a ‘5 screen-free activities we actually use’ piece.”

Nanny:
“Show one activity from a normal afternoon with the children.”

Grandparent:
“Show Lumebook as an activity you do together rather than another toy you hand over.”

## Final output

Produce five outputs.

### Output 1: Master creator database

Target:
At least 50 qualified Israeli creators if sufficient high-quality candidates exist.

If fewer than 50 genuinely fit, return fewer.

Do not pad the list with weak creators.

### Output 2: Top shortlist

Create a shortlist of approximately 15 strongest creators based on:
- relevance

- audience fit
- collaboration practicality

Do not simply choose the accounts with the largest audiences.

### Output 3: Creator archetype map

Explain:
- which creator categories appear strongest in Israel

Recommend how I should diversify my first creator batch.

For example:

- X teachers
- X professionals
- X activity creators
- X unexpected creators

Explain the strategic reason for the mix.

### Output 5: Outreach/testing priorities

For the strongest creators, recommend:
- who to approach first
- type of collaboration to propose
- whether to pursue organic UGC, whitelisting/paid usage, affiliate, gifting, or paid collaboration
- content angle
- funnel role
- primary KPI to evaluate

## Research standards

Use current publicly available information.

Verify follower counts and creator activity as close to the current date as possible.

Do not invent creators.

Do not invent follower counts.

Do not invent engagement statistics.

Do not invent professions.

If something cannot be verified, label it “unverified.”

Prioritize active creators.

Avoid accounts that appear abandoned or inactive.

Use multiple signals when assessing quality.

Do not equate high engagement with sales potential.

Do not equate professional authority with good content creation ability.

Do not equate polished content with authenticity.

The final goal is to answer:

“Which Israeli micro-creators between 1K and 50K followers can help Lumebook produce credible, varied, reusable UGC that resonates with parents and can potentially support both organic content and paid acquisition?”

Start by defining your search methodology and query set, then perform the research.
