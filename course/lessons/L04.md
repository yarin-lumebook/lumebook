# This week's numbers: five numbers, and where each one lives

## Do this now
On Sunday, fill in the five boxes on the Numbers tab, each one from the right place, and write one sentence about what they don't prove.

## The lesson
The Numbers tab asks for five boxes. Each one comes from a different place:

1. **Tagged clicks.** Today you have no source that counts clicks on the links in your DMs. Until you do, tick "Not available yet".
2. **DMs sent with a tagged link.** From the link log here, which fills up from every link you made in the link maker and sent.
3. **Comment words received.** From the comments on this week's Instagram posts.
4. **Tagged orders.** From what Yossi sends. If he hasn't sent anything, tick "Not available yet".
5. **Best post: saves and shares out of reach.** From the post's insights on Instagram.

**One rule before everything else: rate before count.** A number on its own says almost nothing. Are 6 DMs a lot or a little? It depends on out of how many. If 8 comment words came in, 6 DMs are 6 out of 8, which is 75%: three out of every four parents got a link. If next week there are 9 DMs out of 20 comments, there are more DMs, but the rate dropped to 45%: more than half of the parents who asked didn't get a link. The count went up, the work got worse.

The tab does the math and shows it. Your job: check that the denominator is right, meaning that the "out of how many" really counts what it should.

**Box 5, how to pick the best post.** Not by the most saves. Add saves and shares together, and divide by reach, meaning the number of people the post reached. That way you can compare a post that reached many people with a post that reached few.

**Two corrections before you write:**
- You like and save every one of your own posts. Subtract 1 from the saves (`research/carousel-audit/creative-audit.md`, line 5).
- A post that hasn't had 7 days yet is still too new to judge (same line).

**"Not available yet" is a correct answer.** In boxes 1 and 4 you don't guess. A guess looks like data, and then decisions get made based on it. "Not available yet" shows exactly what's missing, and what to ask Yossi for (lesson L06).

**The sentence at the end.** The tab won't save without one sentence about what these numbers don't prove. It's not a ritual. It's the habit the whole course is built on.

## Example
Made-up numbers for practice, not LumeBook's:

- Post A: 40 saves and shares, reach 2,000. That's 40 out of 2,000, which is 2%.
- Post B: 30 saves and shares, reach 600. That's 30 out of 600, which is 5%.

Post A has more saves, but Post B worked better for each person who saw it. Post B goes in box 5. And if you saved both, first subtract 1 from each.

## Common mistake
"Views and likes are the result." They are what happens before stage 1. The result that pays you is a tagged order. That's why four of the five boxes measure the path to an order, and none of them counts likes.

## What the numbers can't prove
One week of three posts doesn't show a trend. A rise from 2 to 4 can be chance.

## Terms
- **Reach**: how many different people saw the post. It appears in the insights of every post.
- **Rate**: a part out of a whole, usually as a percentage. 6 out of 8 is 75%.
- **Denominator**: the "out of how many" of a rate. In the DM rate, the denominator is the comment words.

> Dividing by reach is exactly what lets you compare them.
