The five priorities

1. Show the delivered price and arrival date before the payment page

  • Why it matters: in the last 30 days of the admin readout, 202 orders reached payment and 85 were paid; about half leave at the step where the home shipping fee (₪30) first appears. The site's own checkout summary says "המשלוח מתווסף בקארדקום. הסכום כאן הוא עבור הספר בלבד." The book page and the preview button show ₪119 alone, and the struck-through ₪149 is exactly the one-book total with home shipping. On CardCom the first shipping option, free pickup in Jerusalem, is preselected under a "משלוח עד הבית" label, so the page shows ₪119 until the buyer opens the menu, although buyers come from all over the country (audit.md A25).
  • Evidence: observed on the site and in its code (audit.md A2, A3); admin numbers (docs/lumebook-sales-data-decisions-2026-10-12.md). In Baymard's US survey, extra costs are the top abandonment reason (40%) and slow delivery the second (20%) S-baymard-reasons; in a StubHub field experiment people who met fees only at checkout were almost 45% less likely to buy at that step, while a cinema field experiment found hiding a surcharge changed nothing overall, so showing it early costs little S-price. Israeli consumer law requires prices to be total prices including delivery, and a discount to state a true earlier price (sources.md L-consumer).
  • Confidence: high that the information arrives late; medium on how much fixing it adds.
  • Action: "₪119 + ₪30 משלוח עד הבית · או איסוף חינם בירושלים" and an estimated arrival date on the book page, the preview and the purchase button; until then, home delivery preselected on CardCom instead of Jerusalem pickup; a business and legal decision on the ₪149 reference price. Measure with a fixed before/after window (experiments.md X1).

2. Say exactly what the free preview includes, and back it with real pages and one likeness promise

  • Why it matters: every preview is an order record, and 527 were created in 30 days against 202 that reached payment, so the biggest leak is between preview and payment. The preview shows only the personalised cover; inner pages are locked ("את האיור הזה ניצור עבורכם במיוחד לאחר הרכישה") while the home page says "קוראים את הסיפור בחינם · מזמינים רק אם מתאהבים". The guarantee is stated three different ways ("100% שביעות רצון מובטחת" on book pages, "לא ניתנים לביטול" in the FAQ, "ללא התחייבות לשביעות רצון" in the terms).
  • Evidence: observed in the editor and policies (audit.md A4, A5); the store already has a likeness redraw that keeps the free try (code); MeBook, Magical Book and Wonderbly each state one defect remedy (audit.md, competitors); research on self-made products supports the preview as a selling step only when it meets expectations (S-diy).
  • Confidence: high on the gap; medium on the effect.
  • Action: one line before the photo step on what is free, two or three real inner pages from consented customer books on the preview, one sentence of likeness and defect policy near the button (experiments.md X2). Later, test one illustrated inner page only for people who left (X3), judged on contribution.

3. Make marketing consent separate before adding any message

  • Why it matters: for Israeli numbers the store's code sends marketingConsent: true whenever the required terms box is ticked, and hides the separate promotions box that foreign numbers see; the privacy policy promises a separate explicit choice. The cookie banner is notice-only and every analytics and ad tag loads before any choice. Every lifecycle flow in this plan depends on a list that was gathered lawfully, and on the WhatsApp number staying in good standing.
  • Evidence: code and policy text (audit.md A6, A19). The Communications Law's section 30A requires explicit prior written consent for advertising messages, "פרסומת" at the start of each, and a way to refuse, with up to ₪1,000 per message without proof of damage; its existing-customer exception requires that the person was told at collection and offered a refusal (law text, sources.md L-spam). No ruling applying it to WhatsApp was found, but WhatsApp fits the statute's definition of an electronic message. Meta classes the store's reminders, coupons and birthday messages as marketing templates and requires opt-in (sources.md W-categories, W-optin). Law-firm summaries of the Privacy Protection Authority's Feb 2026 consent opinion say opt-in is expected for direct marketing (sources.md L-privacy-consent).
  • Confidence: high on the facts; the legal consequences need a lawyer (this is not legal advice; 14 questions are in crm-flows.md section 7.5).
  • Action: show the existing unticked marketing box to Israeli numbers, keep the required box for terms and service messages, align the terms, and get counsel's view on the cookie banner before changing it (switching to opt-in needs Google's Consent Mode, the setting that lets tags respect the visitor's choice and model the missing data, in place first, or reporting collapses).

4. Get paid books approved and delivered on time, starting with Hanukkah

  • Why it matters: after payment the buyer still edits and approves the book, and printing starts only then ("אחרי האישור הספר מועבר אוטומטית להדפסה"); home delivery is up to 7 business days. The new birthday reminders go out a week before the date, which mostly leaves a digital gift. Hanukkah starts on 4 Dec 2026; the order deadline in shared/facts.json (22 Nov) is unconfirmed, and none of the Israeli competitors reviewed shows a dated cut-off.
  • Evidence: tutorials and reminder page (audit.md A7, steps 10 and 11); competitor review (audit.md); key dates (shared/facts.json).
  • Confidence: high on the mismatch; timings are hypotheses.
  • Action: an approval nudge at 24 and 72 hours after payment with a deadline line (crm-flows.md F6); reminders about 21 days ahead plus a last-print-day message (F11); a dated "order by" line on book pages, preview and payment from about 10 Nov once the business confirms the date (X6).

5. Make results visible: fix the funnel events, attribution and links

  • Why it matters: GA4 (Google Analytics) receives page views and purchases only, because the tag manager listens for event names the site no longer sends; first-touch attribution (the record of which link first brought a visitor) is kept per browser tab and lost when it closes; the visitor ID is no longer sent with orders; ad events carry ₪50 for a ₪119 book; links use three UTM formats (UTMs are the labels added to a link that tell the store which post, creator or message sent the visitor), and before 24 Oct no social link had a UTM at all. Without this, nobody can tell whether priorities 1 to 4, a creator, or a message sold anything.
  • Evidence: code (audit.md A16, A20, A21); research/carousel-audit/README.md; GA4 documentation (measurement.md).
  • Confidence: high.
  • Action: the event map, purchase with a non-empty transaction_id sent once from the server, CardCom as an unwanted referral, private paths redacted, attribution kept 30 days, one UTM taxonomy, and holdouts on every marketing flow (a random 10 to 20% of eligible people who get no message, so the extra sales a message causes can be told apart from sales that would have happened anyway) (measurement.md).

A sixth item belongs on the same developer list because it is small and sits upstream of everything: show the book cover immediately on book pages (8.7 s and 16.2 s waits measured for a returning visitor; first-time visitors also wait for a 3D viewer, audit.md A1).

Every point gained between preview and payment now lowers cost per order without more budget. Keep the budget flat until one Control-versus-Challenger ad test has run (the admin's own recommendation, and the one place with enough volume for a real A/B test).
  2. What the research changed (assumptions challenged)

  1. "We need to set up phone-based lead capture and a CRM." The store already does both. It verifies the phone (and requires an e-mail) before any preview, sends WhatsApp and SMS through Twilio, keeps a left-alone comparison group, and has reminders, a referral program, a reaction-video offer, a sibling offer and a marketing preferences page. The work is to fix consent, sequence and measure what exists, with the developer, not to add a new system.
  2. Phone first is right for service and recovery, but not alone. WhatsApp is where reminders and help already happen, and the phone is the customer's identity. But marketing consent is currently bundled; Meta caps how many marketing templates one person receives, changed its rate cards four times in 2026 and resumed charging for utility messages inside the service window on 1 Oct (crm-flows.md section 7.1); and depending on one Meta-owned channel concentrates risk. In Baymard's survey 14% of shoppers refuse to give a phone number at all S-phone, so the store's clear reason for asking ("כדי שנשמור לכם את הספר") matters. The store already collects e-mail; use it for order and approval messages and as a second marketing channel with its own consent (crm-flows.md section 4).
  3. The free greeting card is already live, so the question is whether it sells books. /holiday-cards offers a personalised Hanukkah card or a five-page colouring booklet, then a ₪99 book code, but it is not in the sitemap and trackers are blocked on it. A free offer attracts people who value the zero price (S-zero), costs generation money and adds a step. Keep the direct book link as the default social CTA and test the card against it in the DM funnel, judged on paid books and cost per paid book (experiments.md X9).
  4. Tool access does not mean tracking works. GA4 is nearly blind past the landing page (priority 5).
  5. ManyChat is an entry point, not the CRM. Use it to answer comments and DMs with the right book link and one question ("למי הספר?"); let the store's own phone step identify people, so consent and identity stay in one place.
  6. Discounts are not the main recovery lever. In the admin's own comparison the third coupon added +1.6 sales per 100 against +5.4 for the first (with a comparison group the dashboard calls probably tiny); on 25 Oct the plain reminder added +9.5, the first coupon +7.6 and the third +2, and the best linked message is still the ₪79 second book (62 purchases within 24 hours of 234 received), not a coupon. Fix clarity first; at most one coupon, never in the first message, judged on contribution (crm-flows.md section 6).
  7. The grandparent buyer is still a hypothesis, but a stronger one. It rests on Meta's attribution by age (since 25 Oct: women 45 and over get 43 of 102 credited purchases, women 55 to 64 the cheapest at ₪81 each), not on verified buyers. Ask "למי הספר?" after purchase now, and in the DM router, for four weeks before building anything for gift givers (experiments.md X8).
  8. Faster recovery messages are not better. In field experiments with 40,500 and 23,900 customers, cart reminders sent 30 to 60 minutes after abandonment made people less likely to buy than no message, while reminders 1 to 3 days later increased purchases S-timing. Every recovery flow here waits about a day (crm-flows.md, section 3).
  9. A/B tests are mostly out of reach. About 200 people reach payment a month; detecting a 10-point change there needs about 780. Fix observed defects, use fixed before/after windows, usability sessions and non-buyer interviews, and keep holdouts running for a quarter (experiments.md section 1).

  2. "You approve before we print" plus a named defect remedy. MeBook, Magical Book and Wonderbly state the remedy; LumeBook already has the approval step, so it needs only the clear remedy (A5).
  3. A cheap extra copy aimed at grandparents and siblings, joined to the same parcel. MeBook's "ומה עם סבתא?" is the direct comparison to LumeBook's ₪79 offer (X10).
  4. Show the delivered total. Local single-book prices sit within about ₪20 of each other, so how shipping is framed is likely what a comparing parent notices; LumeBook's ₪119 + ₪30 is ₪149 delivered against MeBook's ₪139 framed as including shipping (A2, X1). Avoid MeBook's two different shipping messages on one page.
  5. A gift path for buyers without the child's photo: a gift card or a parent upload link (B2, X8).
  6. A dated holiday cut-off, which no Israeli competitor shows (B5, X6). With LumeBook's shorter stated delivery time, this is a real difference, if the date is honest.
  7. Concrete photo do and don't instructions and a face crop (Booqli, Magical Book, WonderWraps).
  8. WhatsApp support with stated hours (Booqli, TinyHeroStories, Magical Book).
  9. An order-status page with a timeline (MeBook, Magical Book): answers "where is my book" without a person.
  10. Save and resume by one-time code, which LumeBook already has through the phone step and "הספרים שלי".

Patterns that depend on a different market, model or scale

  • Wonderbly's "never AI" and publisher backing: a different product (character builder, not photo likeness); LumeBook's audience-copy rule keeps AI out in either direction.
  • Trustpilot-scale review counts: at LumeBook's scale, dated reviews, printed-book photos and creator videos are more realistic proof.
  • MeBook's pay-first model with paid credits and narration: depends on brand recognition and an editing app; for LumeBook the free preview is the main trust signal.
  • Global shipping estimators, Klarna and customs notes: not relevant for Israel-only sales.
  • Kindergarten bulk pricing (₪50 to ₪77 per book at competitors): a separate B2B motion, already run by LumeBook's /kindergarten flow.
  • Permanent struck-through prices and restarting countdown timers (seen at MeBook and TinyHeroStories): a consumer-protection risk, not a pattern to copy.

What remains unverified

Seen only in code, not on screen: the first-visit cookie banner, the phone and e-mail step, payment failure messages, the order confirmation page and the order status page. Not verified at all: whether Apple Pay, Google Pay and Bit show when the payment page is opened inside Facebook's or Instagram's in-app browser (in Safari all four show); whether the server's purchase event carries a transaction ID; the post-purchase editor and print approval; shipping notifications; the content and timing of the live WhatsApp templates; how the free Hanukkah card performs; real page timings and Lighthouse accessibility scores.

1. Can LumeBook run A/B tests at all?

An A/B test (a randomised controlled experiment: visitors are split by chance between the current version and a change, and only the change differs) needs enough people to tell a real effect from noise. The numbers below come from the 12 Oct admin readout (docs/lumebook-sales-data-decisions-2026-10-12.md) and were computed with the standard two-proportion sample-size formula at 95% confidence and 80% power (close to the 16σ²/δ² rule of thumb in S-exp). Kohavi and co-authors put the practical floor for useful A/B tests at "at least, thousands of active users, preferably tens of thousands", and warn that underpowered tests that do reach significance exaggerate the effect S-exp.

What this means:

  • Only large effects are measurable by A/B test, and only at the payment step or for the whole start-to-purchase rate. A change that adds 2 or 3 points will not be distinguishable from noise this year.
  • CRM holdouts cannot produce a precise answer per message. They can still show direction when pooled over months and over several flows, and they protect against counting purchases that would have happened anyway.
  • The store already splits visitors into flow arms (localStorage.lumebook_flow_arm = "wizard", book_flow_v1), so randomisation infrastructure exists. Use it for the few tests below that can reach a decision; use the methods in section 2 for the rest.
  • Ads are the exception.   • Peeking: deciding as soon as a difference looks good inflates false positives S-exp. Fix the sample size or the end date before starting, and read results once.

3. Prioritised experiments and changes

Ordering: fix observed defects first (they need no test to justify), then the tests that can reach a decision at current volume, then the rest.

X0. Show the book cover immediately on book pages (defect A1)

  • Problem and evidence: main cover requested 8.7 s and 16.2 s after load in a returning profile; a loading mascot shows instead (audit A1). Book pages are where posts and creator links should land.
  • Hypothesis: if the stock cover renders at once, fewer visitors leave book pages before acting.
  • Exact change: render the catalogue cover immediately; load the personalised cover in the background with a short timeout; never block the page on it.
  • Audience and stage: everyone landing on /books/*. Landing.
  • Primary metric: share of /books/* landings that start the wizard (wizard step 1 viewed) in the same session.
  • Guardrails: errors reported to Sentry from the cover components; page load time.
  • Tracking: GA4 or Mixpanel landing page + wizard_step_viewed (see measurement.md).
  • Effort and dependencies: developer, small. First confirm on a clean phone whether new visitors also wait.
  • Decision rule: ship as a fix. Keep it unless the start rate falls.
  • Next action: Yarin tests on a clean phone and sends Yossi the timings.

X1. Total cost and arrival date before payment (defects A2, A3)

  • Problem and evidence: ₪119 appears alone on book pages and the preview CTA; home shipping (₪30) and the 7 business days appear only on the CardCom page, where free pickup in Jerusalem is preselected so the total reads ₪119 (A25); the struck-through ₪149 equals the one-book total with shipping (audit A2, A3). About half of orders that reach payment are not paid (12 Oct readout). In Baymard's US survey, "extra costs too high" is the top abandonment reason (40%) and 12% could not see the total cost up front S-baymard-reasons. In a StubHub field experiment, buyers who saw fees only at checkout were almost 45% less likely to buy at that step, although total revenue rose because more people reached it; an online cinema field experiment found hiding a surcharge changed nothing overall S-price. So showing the fee early is unlikely to cost sales and likely to move the drop earlier, where it is cheaper (no generation spent).
  • Hypothesis: showing the full cost and an arrival date earlier raises the share of previews that are paid, because fewer people meet a surprise on the payment page.
  • Exact change: on the book page, the preview screen and the CTA: "₪119 + ₪30 משלוח עד הבית · או איסוף חינם בירושלים"; an estimated arrival line computed from today ("אם מאשרים היום, מגיע בערך עד [יום ותאריך]"); the shipping choice made on the preview and passed to CardCom, home delivery preselected; decide whether the ₪149 struck price stays (legal question in crm-flows.md). If the preview part takes longer, preselect home delivery on CardCom at once (a settings change) and note its date, since it moves the same metric.
  • Audience and stage: everyone with a preview. Preview to payment.
  • Primary metric: paid orders ÷ previews ready (book level, within 7 days).
  • Guardrails: average order value; share choosing pickup; support questions about price.
  • Tracking: preview_ready, begin_checkout, purchase with transaction_id (see measurement.md).
  • Effort and dependencies: developer, small to medium (arrival date logic). Business decision on the reference price.
  • Decision rule: before/after over 4 full weeks each side (fixed in advance), no other pricing change in that window. Keep if paid ÷ preview does not fall and price questions drop. If the store's flow-arm split can be used, an A/B test reaching about 780 payment-step arrivals detects a 10-point change.
  • Next action: agree the exact wording with Yossi; record the start date in the publishing log.

X2. Say what the preview includes, and show real inner pages (defects A4, A5)

  • Problem and evidence: the free preview is the personalised cover only; inner pages are locked until after payment, while the home page promises "קוראים את הסיפור בחינם · מזמינים רק אם מתאהבים" (audit A4). Remedies for a poor likeness are stated in conflicting ways (A5). Research on self-designed products finds that people value products they helped create more, but much less when the result looks unattractive, and the IKEA effect disappears when the work is left unfinished S-diy. The closest evidence on reducing "what will I get" uncertainty is a set of randomised field experiments in which fit information raised conversion and order value at an online retailer S-diy. No study of free previews for personalised products was found.
  • Hypothesis: setting the expectation before the photo step, and showing two or three real inner pages from consented customer books with a clear likeness promise, raises paid ÷ preview and reduces likeness complaints.
  • Exact change: a line before the photo step ("בחינם רואים את הכריכה עם {{שם_הילד}}. את האיורים בפנים יוצרים אחרי הרכישה, ואפשר לתקן אותם עד שאתם מרוצים" only if that promise is true); on the preview, a strip of real inner pages; the likeness and fix policy in one sentence near the CTA.
  • Audience and stage: everyone at photo step and preview.
  • Primary metric: paid ÷ previews ready. Guardrails: wizard completion (photo step); likeness complaints; refunds.
  • Effort and dependencies: content (consented customer pages; the reaction-video offer collects consented media), developer small, business decision on the promise.
  • Decision rule: before/after, 4 weeks each side, or A/B with the flow-arm split. Keep if paid ÷ preview rises or holds while complaints fall.
  • Next action: ask Yossi what likenessRecoveryEnabled does and what the current remedy is.

X3. One illustrated inner page before payment, for people who left

  • Problem and evidence: as X2. Generating inner illustrations for everyone costs money per started book, and only about 1 in 5 starters pays (12 Oct readout).
  • Hypothesis: generating one inner page only for people whose preview is ready and who did not pay within 24 hours (a much smaller group) lifts their purchase rate by more than it costs.
  • Exact change: in the preview-not-purchased flow (crm-flows.md, F3), the second message links to a preview where page 1 is already illustrated.
  • Audience: previews ready, not paid after 24 hours, marketing consent not required if the message is a service reminder (legal question).
  • Primary metric: paid within 7 days of the message ÷ people messaged, against a randomly held-out group that gets the standard reminder.
  • Guardrails: generation cost per extra sale; opt-outs.
  • Tracking: message sends with flow and variant IDs; orders joined by order ID.
  • Effort and dependencies: developer medium (generate on demand). Needs the cost of one illustration from Yossi.
  • Decision rule (contribution, not purchases): continue if (extra purchases × contribution per order) is greater than (illustrations generated × cost per illustration) after enough people for a +6-point difference (about 900 people across both groups); otherwise stop.
  • Next action: get the generation cost and the contribution per order.

X4. Payment-step reminder without a discount

  • Problem and evidence: about half of orders that reach payment are not paid; the 12 Oct readout recommends a message for people who reached payment and did not pay; the current coupon ladder exists but a plain reminder for this step was not listed.
  • Hypothesis: a service-style reminder with a help offer, about a day after leaving CardCom, recovers some orders without giving away margin. Field experiments found reminders within the first hour reduced purchases and reminders after 1 to 3 days increased them S-timing.
  • Exact change: flow F4 in crm-flows.md.
  • Audience: reached payment, no payment within 24 hours, not a test name, not already purchased.
  • Primary metric: paid within 72 hours ÷ eligible, treatment vs a 20% random holdout.
  • Guardrails: STOP replies, complaints, WhatsApp quality rating.
  • Decision rule: keep if the treated group is not worse than holdout after about 300 eligible people and complaints stay low; read the size of the effect after a full quarter.
  • Next action: Yossi confirms whether a "reached payment" event exists server-side (CardCom notifications).

X5. Rationalise the coupon ladder

  • Problem and evidence: in the admin's comparison with a left-alone group, the first coupon added +5.4 sales per 100 and the third +1.6 (12 Oct readout); on 25 Oct the plain reminder added +9.5, the first coupon +7.6 and the third +2 O-admin-oct25. The dashboard's own notes flag that the left-alone group is probably tiny and not random. In 70 randomised discount e-mail tests, about 90% of the extra spending came from people who never redeemed the code, so the reminder did most of the work; deeper discounts lowered later buying by established customers in catalogue field tests; and a minority of buyers (5% to 19% in airline data) wait strategically for deals S-wait.
  • Hypothesis: dropping the third coupon, and making the first reminder a no-discount help message, keeps most recovered sales and raises contribution per recovered sale.
  • Exact change: reminder 1 without coupon; coupon only at step 2 for people still eligible; no third coupon.
  • Primary metric: contribution per eligible person (revenue minus discount, print, shipping and message costs), not purchases.
  • Guardrails: paid ÷ eligible; complaints.
  • Decision rule: compare a quarter with the new ladder to the holdout. Keep the cheaper ladder unless contribution per eligible person is lower.
  • Next action: get the ladder's current timing and coupon values from the admin.

X6. Order-by dates and earlier reminders (A7, B5, B6)

  • Problem and evidence: birthday reminders go a week before the date, while printed home delivery takes up to 7 business days after approval (A7). Hanukkah starts 4 Dec 2026; the order deadline in shared/facts.json (22 Nov) is unconfirmed.
  • Hypothesis: dated order-by messages and earlier reminders increase on-time printed orders and reduce late, digital-only gifts.
  • Exact change: first reminder about 21 days before (hypothesis), a "last day for a printed book" reminder, and the digital copy as the late answer; a dated Hanukkah line on book pages from 10 Nov.
  • Primary metric: printed orders paid ÷ reminders sent; share of reminder-driven orders delivered before the date.
  • Guardrails: opt-outs from reminders.
  • Decision rule: compare reminder cohorts by send timing over the season. The store's reminder service started on 4 Oct 2026, so there is no baseline yet; record from day one.
  • Next action: business confirms the Hanukkah order-by date and print lead time.

X7. Deep links from social to the book in the post (B1)

  • Problem and evidence: the bio link goes to the home page; the visitor must choose among three creation modes and about 66 books (audit steps 4 and 5).
  • Hypothesis: a link to the matching book page (or the wizard with the book preselected) raises books started per click.
  • Exact change: every post, story and creator link uses the book URL with UTMs (measurement.md); ask Yossi whether the wizard accepts a book parameter.
  • Primary metric: books started ÷ sessions, by link type (home vs book).
  • Decision rule: sequential by post; judge after 20 posts of each type. Directional only.
  • Effort: none for Yarin beyond link discipline.

X8. Gift giver photo request (B2)

  • Problem and evidence: ad delivery skews to women 55 and over, read as grandmothers but not verified (12 Oct readout); on 25 Oct Meta credited 43 of the 102 purchases in its age table to women 45 and over, women 55 to 64 the cheapest at ₪81 each O-admin-oct25, still Meta's attribution and not buyers. A gift giver may not have a good photo. First step, now: one optional question on the thank-you page, "למי הספר?" (לילד שלי, לנכד או לנכדה, לאחיין או לאחיינית, מתנה לחברים), for four weeks, read against payment per preview by answer (journey demo step 8, note 5). The group-order flow already sends parents an upload link.
  • Hypothesis: letting a gift buyer send the parent an upload link increases gift orders.
  • Step 1 (no development): ask "למי הספר?" in the Instagram DM router (crm-flows.md, F1) and in the post-purchase message, for four weeks, to size the gift-giver share.
  • Decision rule: build the request link only if gift givers are a meaningful share (business sets the bar before reading the answers).

X9. Free greeting card first vs straight to the book, in the DM funnel

  • Problem and evidence: a free Hanukkah greeting card or coloring booklet is live at /holiday-cards, followed by a ₪99 book code (audit step 12), but nothing shows whether it sells books. A free offer attracts people for whom the price is zero, not necessarily buyers S-zero; generation and messages cost money.
  • Hypothesis to test, not assume: for cold audiences around Hanukkah, a free card first produces more paid books per 100 DM conversations within 30 days than a direct book link.
  • Exact change: in ManyChat, randomly assign people who comment or DM the keyword to (a) direct book link or (b) free card, then a book link after delivery. Use ManyChat's randomiser step if available (see sources.md), or alternate by week.
  • Primary metric: paid books within 30 days per 100 DM conversations, joined by UTM utm_content per arm.
  • Guardrails: cost per paid book including generation; opt-outs; share of card requesters who start a book.
  • Decision rule: keep (b) only if paid books per 100 are higher and cost per paid book is lower than (a). Otherwise use the card only as a post-purchase gift.
  • Dependencies: trackers are blocked on /holiday-cards, so the arm must be read from the ₪99 code redemptions and from orders whose phone matches a card request (in the store, not in analytics); consent design per crm-flows.md.

X10. Second book offer framed for siblings and family

  • Problem and evidence: the post-purchase offer is the strongest recorded message (48 purchases after 209 sends in 90 days, $0.16 message cost per purchase, 12 Oct; 62 purchases within 24 hours of 234 received, $0.15, 25 Oct O-admin-oct25; not shown to be caused by the message) and buyers average 1.41 books.
  • Hypothesis: a sibling and cousin framing ("עוד ילד או ילדה בבית?") raises second-book take-up versus the current wording.
  • Primary metric: second book within the ₪79 window ÷ first purchases, by variant, with a holdout that gets no offer.
  • Decision rule: sizeable effects only (volume is about 70 sends a month); run for a quarter.

X11. Reminder timing for unfinished books and previews

  • Problem and evidence: the timing of the store's current reminders is not visible to Yarin. Field experiments (other stores) found that cart reminders sent 30 to 60 minutes after abandonment reduced purchases versus no message, while 1 to 3 days later increased them, by e-mail, app and SMS S-timing.
  • Hypothesis: moving any reminder that now goes out within an hour to about 24 hours raises paid ÷ eligible.
  • Primary metric: paid within 7 days ÷ eligible, by timing arm, against the holdout.
  • Decision rule: if the current timing is already a day or more, skip the test. Otherwise alternate timing by week for 8 weeks (switchback), because monthly volume (about 150 people in the unfinished-book flow) cannot support a per-person split with a holdout.
  • Next action: Yossi reports the current send delays.

4. What not to test now

  • A progress bar or step counter as a lift: a meta-analysis of 32 randomised web-survey experiments found constant progress indicators did not reduce drop-off S-progress. Fix the 4-to-5 step counter (audit A10) because it is inaccurate, not because it will sell books.
  • Button colours, headline wording on the home page and other small copy changes: the volume cannot detect their effects.
  • New discount levels: they change margin and teach waiting S-wait before the basics (X0 to X2) are fixed.
  • A new CRM or messaging tool: the store already sends WhatsApp and SMS (Twilio) with consent records and a holdout (see roadmap.md).

3. Rules that apply to every flow

Priority when flows overlap (higher wins; a lower flow pauses while a higher one is active):

  1. Service and transactional: F5 payment failed, F6 approval, F7 order and delivery, OTP codes.
  2. F4 reached payment.
  3. F3 preview ready.
  4. F2 unfinished book.
  5. F8 help after delivery, F9 review and video.
  6. F10 second book.
  7. F11 reminders the customer asked for.
  8. F12 referral invitation.
  9. F13 seasonal and win-back.

Frequency caps (hypotheses, set in the message system):

  • At most one marketing message per person per day, and at most 2 per rolling 7 days and 4 per 30 days, across all flows. Service messages do not count but are never bundled with offers.
  • No marketing between 21:00 and 08:00, on Shabbat or on Jewish holidays (Friday from 14:00 to Saturday night). Service messages that the customer is waiting for (OTP, payment failure) may go any time.

Deduplication: one active flow instance per person per flow; a person with two drafts gets one message about the newest, naming both only if both are ready.

Suppression (never send marketing): STOP or preference off; open support conversation in the last 48 hours; complaint or refund request in the last 30 days; test names or team phones; group-order parents without their own opt-in; anyone whose WhatsApp marketing message failed delivery twice in a row (switch to SMS or e-mail only with consent for that channel).

Exit conditions: purchase exits F2, F3, F4 and F5 immediately (checked at send time, not at schedule time); approval exits F6; a reply exits automation and opens a human conversation.

Human handoff: any free-text reply goes to the support inbox; replies with "לא דומה", "בעיה", "החזר", "ביטול", "לא הגיע" are tagged urgent. Response hours are stated in the first automated reply (for example "עונים בימים א׳ עד ה׳ בין {{שעות}}"; set real hours).

Marketing labelling: every message classed as marketing (F2, F3, F4, F10, F11, F12, F13 and F0's second message, see section 7) starts with the word "פרסומת", names לומבוק, and ends with "להפסקת הודעות שיווקיות השיבו STOP". The drafts below show this in full for F10 and F13; the message system should add the same first and last line to the others. Service messages (F5 to F8) carry no offer, so they stay service.

Holdout: each flow keeps a random 10 to 20% of eligible people unmessaged for a quarter (measurement.md, section 8). Service flows (F5 to F7) have no holdout.

Timing rule for recovery messages: nothing within the first hour after someone leaves; the first reminder about a day later. Early messages annoyed people and reduced purchases in field experiments; later ones helped S-timing.

Delivery failures: WhatsApp may refuse to deliver marketing templates to a person who has had too many from businesses recently (error 131049, per Meta's per-user limits W-caps). Log these failures and leave those people out of the flow's results; do not retry within 24 hours.

4. Channel choice: phone-first, with e-mail as the second channel

Yarin's preference for phone-based capture is already how the store works: the phone is verified before the preview, and WhatsApp is the reminder channel. The evidence supports keeping it for service and recovery messages, with three changes:

  1. Consent must be separate. The terms treat giving a phone number as consent to marketing; the privacy policy says marketing needs a separate explicit choice (audit A6). Keep the phone and OTP for the book and service messages, and ask for marketing consent with its own unticked choice at the preview or after payment.
  2. Use the e-mail the store already collects as the second channel. The phone step already requires an e-mail ("אימייל לעותק הדיגיטלי"), and CardCom asks for an invoice e-mail. Use it for order confirmations and long content (the full order summary, the approval link, the digital copy), and as a fallback when WhatsApp marketing delivery fails or Meta changes its rules or prices. Separate marketing consent applies. Consider making the e-mail optional at the phone step and asking for it at payment instead (audit B7), since two required contact fields before any preview is a lot on a phone.
  3. ManyChat stays at the Instagram edge. It is good at turning a comment or DM into a link with UTMs and a first question ("למי הספר?"). Do not copy phone numbers from ManyChat into the store's lists; let the store's own OTP identify the person, so consent and identity stay in one system.

Costs, delivery rules and legal points per channel are in section 7. In short: WhatsApp marketing messages cost about $0.035 each in Israel and utility messages about $0.005 (third-party tables checked against Meta's card); SMS through Twilio costs about $0.26 per 70-character Hebrew segment; e-mail is close to free at this size.

6. Discounts: when they help and when they hurt

  • Likely to raise contribution: the ₪79 second book in the 24-hour window (no new acquisition cost, shared shipping, illustrations partly reusable); a time-limited seasonal price with a real end date.
  • Seen on 25 Oct O-admin-oct25: against the left-alone group, the plain "finish your book" reminder added +9.5 purchases per 100, the first coupon +7.6 and the third +2; the one list-wide broadcast (back to school, 813 sent) was followed by one purchase within 24 hours. No list-wide marketing message until consent is separate, and after that only messages with a trigger.
  • Likely to lower it: a coupon in the first recovery message (it goes to people who would have bought anyway), a fixed three-coupon ladder, and permanent "limited time" prices. In 70 randomised e-mail tests at a ticketing site, about 90% of the extra spending from discount e-mails came from people who never used the code, so most of the effect was the reminder itself S-wait. Deeper discounts raised later buying by first-time customers but lowered it for established customers in three catalogue field experiments S-wait, so a coupon to past buyers can pull purchases forward and raise deal-seeking. The admin's own comparison shows the third coupon adding much less than the first (+1.6 vs +5.4 extra sales per 100, a probably tiny comparison group).
  • Rule of thumb for LumeBook: non-price help first (clarity, a deadline, free pickup, help with the photo); at most one coupon per person per 90 days, never in the first message; judge every discount by contribution per eligible person against a holdout, not by purchases.

7. Channel rules, costs and Israeli legal points

This section summarises official documentation, law texts and law-firm commentary read on 24 Oct 2026. It is not legal advice; the open questions at the end are for an Israeli lawyer. Sources are listed in sources.md, sections 6 and 7.

7.1 WhatsApp (Meta)

  • Pricing is per delivered message since 1 July 2025, by template category: marketing, utility, authentication, plus free-form service replies inside a 24-hour customer service window W-pricing.
  • Since 1 October 2026 utility templates sent inside an open service window are charged again, and service replies are free only for the first 1,000 a month per number W-pricing. The assumption that order updates inside the window are free no longer holds.
  • Israel rates (USD, effective 1 Oct 2026): marketing about $0.0353, utility and authentication about $0.0053 per delivered message. These come from two third-party tables that say they were checked against Meta's rate card; Meta's own rate-card file could not be opened, so confirm in WhatsApp Manager or with Twilio before budgeting W-rates. The store's own estimate ($0.036 per message, 12 Oct readout) matches the marketing rate.
  • Categories: Meta lists abandoned-cart reminders, discount or coupon offers and birthday messages as marketing; order confirmations and shipping updates with order details as utility; mixed content defaults to marketing, and Meta re-categorises templates W-categories. In this plan, F2, F3, F4, F10, F11, F12, F13 and F0's second message are marketing; F5, F6, F7 and F8 are utility if they contain no offer; F9's review request is utility only when tied to a specific order, and its reaction-video offer is marketing.
  • Opt-in: Meta requires a clear opt-in that names the business, and prompt escalation to a human from any automation; opt-outs must be respected on or off WhatsApp W-optin. Israeli law is stricter (7.4).
  • Per-user marketing limits: WhatsApp caps how many marketing templates one person receives from businesses, adapting to that person's engagement; blocked sends fail with error 131049 and are not charged. Israel is not exempt W-caps. A coupon ladder sent in quick succession may silently lose its later steps.
  • Messaging limits and quality: business-initiated messages are limited per portfolio (250, then 2,000, 10,000 unique people per rolling 24 hours); blocks and "not interested" feedback lower template quality and can pause templates W-limits. A whole-list broadcast risks both.
  • Marketing Messages API: Meta's separate API for marketing templates is generally available since 19 Nov 2025. Accounts created from 13 Apr 2027 cannot send marketing through the Cloud API, and from 13 Oct 2027 marketing sends through the Cloud API fail; utility and service stay on the Cloud API W-mm. The store sends through Twilio, so Yossi should ask Twilio how and when it migrates.
  • Click-to-WhatsApp ads open a free entry point window of up to 7 days in which Meta does not charge for messages W-pricing. Relevant only if paid ads are used again.

7.2 Instagram DMs (Meta, through ManyChat)

  • An automated comment-to-DM gets one private reply per comment, within 7 days; further messages only after the person replies, within 24 hours of that reply I-private. Quick-reply buttons (F1) are how the conversation continues.
  • Automated promotional messages are allowed only inside the 24-hour window (up to 7 days after a click-to-Direct ad); a human agent tag allows manual replies for 7 days I-window. Recurring "marketing messages" on Messenger were deprecated on 10 Feb 2026, and no current Instagram equivalent was found I-window. Instagram is an entry point, not a re-engagement channel.
  • Rate limit: 750 private replies per hour per account I-limits. Not a constraint at LumeBook's volume.

7.3 SMS and e-mail

  • SMS through Twilio to Israel: $0.2575 per segment; Hebrew uses UCS-2, so 70 characters per segment (67 when concatenated), about $0.77 for a 140-character message; a branded sender ID needs domestic registration (about a week) SMS-twilio. Local providers publish far lower unit prices (for example 019 SMS ₪20 + VAT per 1,000 verification messages; ActiveTrail about ₪0.111 per message) SMS-local. Use SMS only as a fallback for people without WhatsApp, with the same consent and labelling.
  • E-mail: Gmail and Yahoo require SPF, DKIM and DMARC for bulk senders, a one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) on marketing mail, and unsubscribes honoured within about two days; Microsoft rejects non-compliant high-volume mail E-auth. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on lumebook.com regardless of volume. The store already has a one-click unsubscribe endpoint (code).

7.4 Israeli law: what it means for these flows

  • Spam law (Communications Law, section 30A): an advertising message ("דבר פרסומת", a commercial message meant to encourage buying) by e-mail, SMS, automatic dialling or electronic message needs the recipient's explicit prior consent in writing (electronic counts). Each must start with the word "פרסומת" (in the e-mail subject), name the advertiser and give a simple way to refuse. Courts may award up to ₪1,000 per message without proof of damage L-spam. The Supreme Court held in 2014 that an unsubscribe link does not reduce the compensation L-spam-court.
  • Existing-customer exception (30A(c)): consent is not needed where the person gave their details during a purchase or during negotiation for a purchase, was told the details would be used for advertising, was offered a chance to refuse and did not, and the advertising is for similar products L-spam. A parent who created a book may fall in "negotiation for a purchase", but only if the phone step told them about offers and let them refuse. Today the Israeli phone step bundles marketing into the required box (audit A6), and a magistrates' court certification decision (as reported by a vendor blog, not opened) held that a clause buried in general terms is not explicit consent L-spam-court.
  • WhatsApp: no ruling applying 30A to WhatsApp was found, but a WhatsApp message travels over the internet and fits the statute's definition of an electronic message; Israeli law-firm commentary advises treating it like SMS and e-mail L-spam-wa. Plan as if marketing WhatsApp templates are covered.
  • Which messages are advertising: order, production and shipping updates are service. A reminder whose purpose is to get the unfinished book bought fits the statutory definition of advertising, as do coupons, the second-book offer, birthday reminders that link to a book, and broadcasts (assessment for the lawyer, not a ruling).
  • What to do until a lawyer answers: (1) show the separate unticked marketing box to Israeli numbers and store consent text, version, time and source per person; (2) send F2, F3, F4, F10 to F13 only to people with that consent; (3) start every marketing template with "פרסומת", name לומבוק, and end with the STOP line, which must stop marketing on WhatsApp, SMS and e-mail together; (4) keep service messages free of offers so they stay service.
  • Privacy Protection Law, Amendment 13 (in force 14 Aug 2025): the notice at the point of collection (section 11) must state whether giving the data is a legal duty or depends on consent and the consequence of refusing, the purpose, the controller's name and contact, recipients, and access and correction rights; administrative sanctions start at ₪50 per person asked with a ₪30,000 floor L-privacy. Most private databases no longer need registration L-privacy. A photo counts as data of special sensitivity only as a biometric identifier used to identify a person digitally; whether LumeBook's likeness pipeline does that is a lawyer question L-privacy. Per law-firm summaries of the Privacy Protection Authority's Feb 2026 consent opinion, opt-in is expected for direct marketing, and services aimed at minors must adapt consent L-privacy-consent.
  • Cookies and pixels: the Authority recommended opt-in for cookies in 2020 (not binding), and the Feb 2026 opinion questions implied consent for third-party advertising tools L-privacy-consent. The store's default-on, notice-only banner (audit A19) is therefore a live question.
  • Consumer Protection Law: goods made specially for the consumer are excepted from the 14-day distance-sale cancellation right, but the disclosure duties still apply; "price" includes delivery; prices must be total prices in shekels including VAT; a sale must state the price before the sale, and the "regular or previously charged price" is a material term for misleading-conduct purposes L-consumer. A Consumer Protection Authority enforcement note on custom-made goods (2018) was read only partly (scrambled PDF) S-cancel. The ₪149 struck price, "limited time" wording and every "instead of ₪X" coupon need a true earlier price.
  • Accessibility: service websites must meet Israeli Standard 5568 (identical to WCAG 2.0) at level AA, with an accessibility statement; exemption only for VAT-exempt dealers or turnover up to ₪100,000 a year; damages up to ₪50,000 without proof of damage (indexed) L-access. The store has a statement page; conformance was not tested.

6. Attribution windows and limits

  • GA4 key events default to a 90-day lookback (30 days for first visit), and the first click, linear, time decay and position-based models were retired in November 2023 (https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10597962). GA4's default model is data-driven, which at LumeBook's volume behaves close to last click; read it as a hint, not a measure.
  • Ambassador credit: 7 days, printed books only (store settings, 14 Oct). QR ₪99 offer: 7 days, same browser (/offers/g10-99).
  • CRM windows (proposed, for reporting only): payment-step reminder 72 hours; preview reminder 7 days; win-back 14 days. Incrementality comes from the holdout, not from the window (section 8).
  • Known blind spots: visitors who decline analytics cookies (no UTMs in GA4 or Mixpanel; the store's own attribution may also be consent-gated), Instagram's in-app browser and device switches, gift buyers who return weeks later, and organic spill-over from posts seen but not clicked.

8. Incrementality: how to count what CRM really adds

  • A purchase that follows a message is not necessarily caused by it. The admin's "purchases after a message" table (12 Oct readout) counts both.
  • Per-flow holdout: a random share (10 to 20%) of eligible people get no message from that flow. Extra sales = (rate in treated − rate in holdout) × treated people.
  • Global holdout: a random 10% of people get no marketing flows at all for a quarter (service messages continue). This is the only way to see CRM's total effect, including overlap between flows.
  • Low volume: with about 150 eligible people a month in a flow, per-flow results stay uncertain for many months (experiments.md, section 1). Report the difference with its range, pool flows that share a goal, and decide on direction plus cost, not on a single month.
  • Discounts: count the discount given to people who would have bought anyway as a cost. A coupon flow can raise purchases and still lower contribution.
