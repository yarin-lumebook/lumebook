# Buying journey audit: lumebook.com/IL/he

Internal. Part of the CRO and CRM strategy in `research/cro-crm/` (start at `report.md`). Holds internal sales figures from `docs/lumebook-sales-data-decisions-2026-10-12.md`; keep it off public pages.

Walked on 24 Oct 2026 in the Claude desktop app's built-in browser at phone size (375 x 812) and desktop size. Supporting code read: `research/cro-crm/sources.md` section 2 (C-code). Findings marked "from code" come from that read, not from the screen.

## How this audit was done, and its limits

- **Profile.** The browser profile was already phone-verified on lumebook.com as Yarin's own account and had accepted analytics cookies. A first-time visitor's experience (cookie banner, phone verification step, first-visit book pages) was therefore **not seen directly**. Where code shows what a new visitor gets, that is marked "from code".
- **Nothing was bought, uploaded or generated.** The test child name was `בדיקה`, which `shared/facts.json` already lists as a test name. Step 1 of the wizard sent `POST /api/order`, which answered `{"success":true,"message":"Rate limit check passed","testOnly":true}`, so no book record was created by the walk-through.
- **Checkout.** A deliberate click on "רכישת ספר מודפס" was blocked by the session's permission rules. Later, a click inside the preview's page list landed on the locked page's "לרכישת הספר" link and opened the CardCom payment page for one of Yarin's existing test books. Nothing was typed or submitted; the page text already on screen was read and the tab was moved away at once. This may show up in the store's data as a checkout visit on Yarin's test account and could trigger the store's own reminder to Yarin's phone.
- **Payment page on a phone.** Later on 24 Oct (22:28) Yarin sent two iPhone screenshots of the CardCom page in Safari: the full page and the open shipping picker (`media/projects/2026-10-24-cro-crm-actions/cardcom-*.jpg`, not in git). They show the payment methods and the preselected shipping option (step 9, A25).
- **Not verified at all:** card entry, whether the wallets show inside Facebook's and Instagram's in-app browsers, payment failure, the order confirmation page, the post-purchase editor, print approval, shipping notifications, delivery and the WhatsApp messages themselves.

Confidence labels: **high** = seen directly, repeatable; **medium** = seen once, or depends on the profile; **low** = inferred.

Customer counts on these sites (MeBook 65,000+, TinyHeroStories 5,437+, Wonderbly 10 million readers) are the brands' own claims. A practice used by a bigger brand is not evidence that the practice caused its sales.

**Patterns that transfer to LumeBook**

1. **One dated photo-retention sentence at the upload button, matching the policy.** Magical Book's is the clearest; MeBook and WonderWraps contradict their own policies. LumeBook already promises 60 days in its policy but says nothing at upload (B3).
2. **"You approve before we print" plus a named defect remedy.** MeBook, Magical Book and Wonderbly state the remedy; LumeBook already has the approval step, so it needs only the clear remedy (A5).
3. **A cheap extra copy aimed at grandparents and siblings, joined to the same parcel.** MeBook's "ומה עם סבתא?" is the direct comparison to LumeBook's ₪79 offer (X10).
4. **Show the delivered total.** Local single-book prices sit within about ₪20 of each other, so how shipping is framed is likely what a comparing parent notices; LumeBook's ₪119 + ₪30 is ₪149 delivered against MeBook's ₪139 framed as including shipping (A2, X1). Avoid MeBook's two different shipping messages on one page.
5. **A gift path for buyers without the child's photo**: a gift card or a parent upload link (B2, X8).
6. **A dated holiday cut-off, which no Israeli competitor shows** (B5, X6). With LumeBook's shorter stated delivery time, this is a real difference, if the date is honest.
7. **Concrete photo do and don't instructions and a face crop** (Booqli, Magical Book, WonderWraps).
8. **WhatsApp support with stated hours** (Booqli, TinyHeroStories, Magical Book).
9. **An order-status page with a timeline** (MeBook, Magical Book): answers "where is my book" without a person.
10. **Save and resume by one-time code**, which LumeBook already has through the phone step and "הספרים שלי".

**Patterns that depend on a different market, model or scale**

- Wonderbly's "never AI" and publisher backing: a different product (character builder, not photo likeness); LumeBook's audience-copy rule keeps AI out in either direction.
- Trustpilot-scale review counts: at LumeBook's scale, dated reviews, printed-book photos and creator videos are more realistic proof.
- MeBook's pay-first model with paid credits and narration: depends on brand recognition and an editing app; for LumeBook the free preview is the main trust signal.
- Global shipping estimators, Klarna and customs notes: not relevant for Israel-only sales.
- Kindergarten bulk pricing (₪50 to ₪77 per book at competitors): a separate B2B motion, already run by LumeBook's `/kindergarten` flow.
- Permanent struck-through prices and restarting countdown timers (seen at MeBook and TinyHeroStories): a consumer-protection risk, not a pattern to copy.
