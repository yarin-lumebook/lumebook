Research of 15 Oct 2026 for LumeBook's own filming. Each Reel below teaches a different way to film a product with a phone. Two are personalised children's books, six are children's products or parenting products whose filming technique carries over to a book.

## How to read this

- **Seen** = visible in the frames (one frame per second, timestamps accurate to about half a second). **Said** = in the machine transcript (local Whisper, Hebrew). It mishears words, so I quote the creator's burned-in subtitles when they exist and mark transcript quotes as machine transcript. I cannot hear the audio myself: music and natural sound are only reported when the transcript or the frames show them. **My read** = my interpretation.
- **Counts** are public plays, likes and comments read on 15 Oct 2026. They show visible response, nothing about reach, watch time, saves, shares, sales, or whether a Reel was boosted as an ad. Where I call something effective I say which kind: filmmaking, clear product communication, or visible audience response. None of these Reels has a verified business result.
- **Buying talk** comes from the earlier comment study in `research/creator-content-conversion/` (14 Oct 2026, up to 30 comments per post in Instagram's ranked order). I did not re-read comments for this research.
- Annotated frame strips live in `media/projects/2026-10-15-israeli-product-reels/annotated/` (not in git). Frames are the creators' work, kept for internal study only.

![Annotated frames, DTugYwmDNKa](../../media/projects/2026-10-15-israeli-product-reels/annotated/DTugYwmDNKa.jpg)

**1. First three seconds.** Seen: 0:00 eye-level selfie, a small dark object at her lips (I can't tell what it is). At 0:01 a full-screen cut to the cover picture of her daughter in a garden with animals, back to her face at 0:03. White subtitles mid-frame. Said and subtitled: "אם גם אתם פוחדים מהגמילה מחיתולים" (if you are also afraid of potty training), then "עשיתי משהו שלא חשבתי שיעבוד" (I did something I didn't think would work). My read: a named fear plus an unfinished promise. The product appears before the first sentence ends, so nobody wonders what the video is about.

**2. Shot sequence.**

| Time | Shot | Seen / said | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:03 | Selfie, medium close-up, handheld, cut to the cover picture at 0:01 | Problem hook | Names the viewer's worry |
| 0:04-0:08 | Top-down close-up, her hand holding printed pages (girl and monkey, monkey on a potty) | "I also thought potty training would be really hard", "then I heard about the personal book" | Makes the topic concrete |
| 0:09-0:10 | Over-the-shoulder, the child holds the cover "טרה נפרדת מהחיתול" | "At first I was sceptical" | The book is the child's own |
| 0:11-0:17 | Wide, static (phone propped or on a tripod): mother and child reading on a grey sofa. A screen recording of the ordering site sits bottom-left as a picture-in-picture | "I uploaded one photo of her and that's it", "the system did everything" | Shows how it is made inside the story, no separate tutorial |
| 0:13 | Insert, the child's face close to the lens, smiling | | Emotion beat |
| 0:18-0:21 | Mom holds the cover up to the standing child, then the child flips pages | "I was in shock", "it's really her", "like they drew her one to one" | Likeness claim with the child in frame |
| 0:22-0:27 | Wide sofa again, picture-in-picture of the page editor; the child laughs at 0:27 | "Lots of potty books, but nothing compares to the moment she saw herself in the story" | Emotional claim |
| 0:28-0:33 | Over-the-shoulder close-up of the child holding the open book | "I'm sure you're asking yourself: if I start potty training in a week, will it arrive in time?" | A buyer's real objection, said out loud |
| 0:34-0:47 | Top-down page flips by her hand | "It arrived really fast", "I checked other options before", "the print quality", "the price is really worth it", "you can add family members and even a pet" | Product details as a list of her reasons |
| 0:48-0:53 | Child reading, then her hand on the pages | "I'm not saying it's magic, but it gave her motivation and took away a lot of pressure" | Honest close |

About 16 shots in 53 s, three cuts in the first four seconds, then 3 to 7 s per shot.

**3. When the product appears and when its value is clear.** Seen at 0:01 (cover). The value is understandable by about 0:15 (one photo becomes a book) and complete at 0:21, when the child holds a book with herself in it. It is shown three ways: the printed pages, the ordering flow, and the child with the book.

**4. Demonstration, proof, emotion, story, selling.** Demonstration: the ordering flow (0:11-0:27) and page flips. Proof: the viewer can compare the child's face at 0:13 with her picture on the cover; the rest is her word. Emotion: the child's smile and laugh, the parent's relief. Story: sceptic, tried it, surprised, objection, honest caveat. Selling lines (said): "המחיר ממש שווה", "רק פה זה באמת היה דומה לה". The coupon code is only in the caption.

**5. Sound, captions, rhythm.** Voiceover nearly wall to wall (29 transcript segments in 53 s); she is on camera only in the opening seconds. Music: unknown. Every line is subtitled in small white text that sometimes runs into the screen-recording overlay.

**6. Ending and CTA.** No on-screen CTA. The video ends on the caveat and the benefit. The caption carries the code, a question ("ספרו לי בתגובות מה הסיפור שהכי מתאים לילד.ה שלכם?") and the UGC disclosure.

**7. Convincing and weak.** My read. Convincing: the hedges ("I was sceptical", "not magic") make the praise believable; the delivery-time question is exactly a buyer's worry; the footage is a real living room; the process is shown inside the story instead of as a tutorial. Weak: the caption sells a birthday gift while the video is about potty training; subtitles and overlay crowd the frame from 0:11 to 0:27 and the site text is unreadable; there is no on-screen CTA; the object at her mouth in the first frame distracts. Counts: 23,532 plays is about 18 times her follower count. I can't tell whether LumeBook ran it as an ad. Yarin can check in Meta Ads Manager before reading anything into it.

**8. How Yarin can recreate it with a phone.** Keep the skeleton, change the speaker's claims to what Yarin can say honestly:
1. A milestone worry in one line, with the book on screen within the first second.
2. A small screen recording of the ordering flow over a table or sofa shot (CapCut: overlay, scale to about 35 %, bottom corner, never over the subtitles).
3. Top-down page flips while the voiceover answers one objection with verified facts (printed book up to 7 business days, digital copy by email in minutes, free preview: `src/product-facts-2026-10-15.md`).
4. An honest close and one on-screen CTA.
Without a child: replace the child shots with page flips and a photo-beside-page comparison (concept 2 in `playbook.md`). For barter creators, this Reel is the best brief LumeBook already owns: ask them to keep it, fix the caption-topic mismatch, add an on-screen CTA and keep subtitles clear of overlays.

**8. How Yarin can recreate it with a phone.** Yarin is not a clinician, so he should not copy expert advice. The format works for facts he owns: "3 things to know before you order" in one locked-off take with the book in hand, numbered headings and one CTA (concept 4 in `playbook.md`). Phone on a tripod at eye level, about 1 m away, window light in front of him, a clip-on mic (or the phone close and the room quiet). For expert content, invite a speech clinician instead: topaz_meir_slp was already "in process" in the creator map on 14 Oct (`research/creator-content-conversion/report.md`, first test T1).
