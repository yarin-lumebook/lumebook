# Principle index for coders (built by src/build_principles.py; full version: principles.md)

ID, rule, strength, and what to look for. Cite IDs in the table's whys.

## Q1. The hook (first 3 seconds)

- **H1. Pass the 3-second skip test** Rule: Within the first 3 seconds the Reel gives a concrete reason to keep watching (a spoken line, an on-screen headline or a striking visual of the product or a reaction), with no intro, greeting, logo card or slow setup before it. Strength: strong. Look for: `hook_0_3s.opening_image` shows the product, a reaction or a person mid-action (not a logo or blank set); `hook_0_3s.spoken` starts with content, not a greeting or name; `hook_0_3s.on_screen_text` is present and readable; first cut or visual change in `shots` before 3 s. Tag "slow open" when 0 to 3 s is a greeting, intro or static establishing shot. (Overlaps E1, T3.)
- **H2. Make the value clear early (result or promise first)** Rule: By about 3 to 5 seconds a viewer can tell what the product is and what it gives the child or parent (the result shown, or the promise stated). Strength: moderate. Look for: `product_first_seen_s` at or under 3; `value_understandable_s` at or under 5; `hook_0_3s.on_screen_text` or `spoken` states a benefit or outcome ("he saw his own name" type) rather than a teaser with no subject.
- **H3. Show the product early, but keep the brand light and repeated (pulse, do not plaster)** Rule: The product appears early and more than once inside the action (in hands, on the page, in the child's lap); the logo or brand name is brief and recurring, not a static central card, and on screen for a small part of the Reel. Strength: contested. Look for: count product appearances across `shots` (2 or more = repeated); `brand` visible on the product vs as a separate logo card or watermark; logo centred and held for more than 2 s = "plastered"; estimate share of duration with a logo or brand card on screen. (Overlaps S1, K5, C7.)
- **H4. Open with a person, ideally a face and a voice** Rule: A person appears and speaks (to camera or in voiceover) within the first 3 seconds. Strength: moderate. Look for: `face_on_camera` at 0 to 3 s; `audio.speech` = direct-to-camera or voiceover with `hook_0_3s.spoken` starting before 3 s; tag "music-only open" when the transcript is empty or lyrics in 0 to 3 s. (Overlaps K8, S4, F6.)
- **H5. Open a curiosity gap and close it** Rule: The opening poses a specific question or an unresolved situation (what will the child do when they see it? how is the face made?) and the Reel visibly answers it later. Strength: moderate. Look for: `hook_0_3s.spoken` or `on_screen_text` contains a question, a "wait for it" or an unfinished situation; mark the timestamp where it is answered (payoff seen in `shots`); tag "unpaid loop" if the question is never answered. (Overlaps K2, R4.)
- **H6. Deliver the hook in sound and on screen** Rule: The Reel has audio (speech and or music, never muted), and the hook is both said or heard and shown (on-screen text or a clear visual), so it works with sound on and off. Strength: moderate. Look for: `audio.speech` and `audio.music`; `captions_on_screen` = burned-in subtitles or headline; hook present in both `hook_0_3s.spoken` and `hook_0_3s.on_screen_text`; tag "mostly text" when text covers most of the frame for most of the Reel. (Overlaps E4, T1, T2.)
- **H7. Make the first frame work as a cover: vertical, clear, native** Rule: The Reel is full-screen vertical (9:16), the first frame and chosen cover show the subject large and readable, the headline sits inside the safe zone, and there are no borders, watermarks or other-app logos. Strength: moderate. Look for: aspect ratio from frames; black or blurred borders; watermark or another app's logo; the first frame in `sheet_01` vs the grid cover; text overlapping the bottom caption area or right-side button column. (Overlaps E7, F1, F2, F3, T4.)
- **H8. Look like a creator, not an ad** Rule: The Reel looks phone-made and personal (handheld or tripod at home, natural sound, real moments, direct address), not like a polished TV commercial. Strength: moderate for "original, native" (official); weak for "lo-fi beats polished" as a measured effect (case studies and platform claims only).. Look for: `shots[].movement` handheld; home setting; `format` (talking-head, hands-only demo, unboxing, reaction); `relationship` = brand_repost vs creator_posted; studio lighting, logo animations or TV-style voiceover tagged as "ad look". (Overlaps E6, F7.)
## Q2. Every few seconds: retention and interaction

- **R1. Earn watch time relative to length: cut every second that does not move the story** Rule: No dead air or repeated setup in the middle; the Reel ends soon after the payoff, so most viewers reach the end. Strength: strong. Look for: duration vs time of payoff (seconds after payoff before the end); shots with no new information in `shots[].purpose`; long static holds (one shot over about 6 s with nothing new) in `editing_rhythm`. (Overlaps R2, E8.)
- **R2. Choose length by content, stay well under 3 minutes** Rule: Use about 7 to 15 s for a single visual moment or loop, about 30 to 60 s for a demo or story, and never more than 3 minutes. Strength: moderate for the 3-minute ceiling (official); weak for any exact optimum (correlational, brand accounts, and better accounts may simply make longer Reels).. Look for: duration from `index.json`; bucket it (0 to 15, 15 to 30, 30 to 60, 60 to 90, 90+); compare with `format`. (Overlaps R1, E8.)
- **R3. Keep the middle moving: a new beat every few seconds, information inside entertainment** Rule: Each shot in the middle adds something new (a new page, a reaction, a step, a change of angle), and product facts are delivered inside an emotional or entertaining moment rather than as a dry list. Strength: moderate. Look for: `n_shots_estimate` and average shot length from `index.json` cuts (soft cuts may be missed); count new-information beats per 10 s; tag segments where spoken product facts play over a static shot with no reaction. (Overlaps E2, F8.)
- **R4. Put the payoff near the end, and make short Reels loop** Rule: The strongest moment (the reveal, the child's reaction) comes in the last seconds, and the last frame leads back into the first; for Reels under about 10 s, edit a seamless loop. Strength: weak to moderate. Look for: timestamp of the payoff vs duration; last frame vs first frame similarity (same framing or object); `ending_and_cta` shows payoff vs a logo card or long talking outro. (Overlaps H5, K2.)
- **R5. Give viewers someone to send it to** Rule: The Reel contains a moment a parent or grandparent would send to a specific person: a gift idea, a relatable parenting moment, a strong emotion (awe, delight, surprise) or a useful tip. Strength: strong that sends matter (official); moderate on what drives them (peer-reviewed but news articles by email).. Look for: `emotion` (awe, delight, surprise vs calm or sad); presence of a gift framing or "send this to" in caption or text; a practical tip (age, occasion, how to order) on screen; `cta_type` includes save_or_share. (Overlaps C4, K1.)
- **R6. Ask for comments with a real, specific question, and answer them; avoid bait** Rule: The video or caption asks one concrete question viewers can answer from their own life (or offers a comment word that delivers something real), the account replies within the first days, and there is no "tag 3 friends" or "comment YES" for its own sake. Strength: weak to moderate. Look for: question in `on_screen_text`, transcript or caption; `cta_type` and `comment_word`; whether the comment word delivers a concrete thing; tag/share/vote demands in caption ("tag", "share with 3 friends"); creator replies visible in comments (if collected). (Overlaps C2, C5.)
- **R7. Earn saves with something worth returning to** Rule: When the goal is saves, the Reel includes reusable information (a list, an age or occasion guide, steps to order) held on screen long enough to read. Strength: weak. Look for: list or step structure in `on_screen_text`; text held on screen 2 s or more per item; `cta_type` includes save_or_share. (Overlaps K4.)
- **R8. Use Reels for reach to new people, and judge them on reach signals** Rule: Treat Reels as the format for reaching non-followers; judge each Reel on skip rate, average watch time, non-follower views and sends per reach, and test new hooks with Trial Reels before showing followers. Strength: moderate. Look for: not visible in frames; record per Reel from Insights (skip rate, average watch time, follower vs non-follower views, sends) when LumeBook's own Reels are coded. For the 173 creator Reels only public plays, likes and comments exist.

## Q3. The call to action

- **C1. One CTA per Reel** Rule: The Reel asks for one action. The same action may appear in voice, on screen and in the caption, but no second, different action is added. Strength: Moderate. Look for: Count the distinct requested actions across the transcript, the text cards and the caption (comment word, follow, save, send, tag, link in bio, buy). Code 0, 1, or 2 or more. Note whether repeats of the same action appear in several channels.
- **C2. Ask for engagement only when it has a purpose** Rule: Every engagement ask has a real purpose (get a link, answer a question, give a recommendation). There are no "like if", "comment yes", "tag 3 friends" or "share to win" asks whose only goal is the count. Strength: Strong that the policy exists (platform-official on both apps). Weak on how Instagram detects bait, since Instagram would not say which terms are penalised. Look for: Flag spoken or written asks of the form like / comment X / tag N / share IF or TO WIN with no stated benefit to the viewer. Separate them from asks that deliver something (a link, an answer, a recommendation). (Overlaps R6, C5.)
- **C3. Comment-keyword-to-DM is a legitimate conversion CTA within the API rules** Rule: When a Reel's goal is a sale, it can ask for one Hebrew keyword in the comments, and an automated DM then delivers the personal link. The DM goes out within 7 days of the comment, is the only unprompted message, and any follow-up comes only after the person replies. Strength: Strong on the mechanics (API documentation), weak on the effect size (vendor correlation only), contested on reach risk (Instagram reversed its own advice within days).. Look for: A keyword is named on screen, in voice or in the caption (record the word and where it appears). Check whether the creator's replies show an automated DM. Note whether the keyword is a brand word or a generic word.
- **C4. Give a reason to send, and prioritise sends over saves** Rule: When reach to non-followers is the goal, the Reel gives a specific person to send it to and a reason, tied to a real need. Save prompts are optional and secondary. Strength: Strong that sends matter for ranking (three platform-official sources). Weak that asking for a send produces sends on Reels: no direct study found, and a generic "share this" could read as share bait (C2).. Look for: A send ask is present (yes/no). Code whether the recipient is named ("לאמא", "לחברה שמחפשת מתנה") and whether a reason is given. Code a save ask separately. Note the format (list, tip, gift idea) that might earn a save without being asked. (Overlaps R5, K1.)
- **C5. Ask an open, specific question when comments are the goal** Rule: When comments are the goal, the Reel or caption ends with one open, specific question that a parent can answer from their own life, not a yes/no or one-word prompt. Strength: Moderate. Look for: A question is present at the end of the transcript, on the last text card or in the caption (code where). Classify it as open or closed (yes/no, one word, emoji), and note whether it is answerable from personal experience. (Overlaps R6, C2.)
- **C6. Say the CTA and show it, after the value and inside the safe zone** Rule: The CTA is both said and shown on screen, after the product or benefit has been shown, sits inside the safe zone, and is repeated in the first line of the caption. Strength: Moderate for ads (official but exploratory, purchase intent probably self-reported). Weak for organic Reels: no organic timing study was found. Look for: CTA timestamp (first 3 s, middle, last 3 s). CTA channel: voice only, text only, or both. Whether the CTA text box overlaps the bottom 35% or top 14%. Whether the first caption line repeats the CTA.
- **C7. Keep the sell light: little brand time, the product shown twice, a benefit named** Rule: The brand name or logo is on screen for no more than about a quarter of the Reel, the product appears at least twice, and at least one concrete benefit or feature is said or shown. Strength: Weak to moderate. Look for: Seconds with a logo or brand name visible, divided by total length. Count of separate product appearances. Whether a specific benefit or feature is stated. Whether the scene is an everyday routine (bedtime, birthday).

Method note for all C rules: Instagram's trial reels are shown to non-followers first ([Create engaging content](https://creators.instagram.com/create-engaging-content), Instagram for Creators, undated). They are a practical way to A/B test two CTA versions on the same footage. (Overlaps H3, S1, K5.)
## Q4. What is shown

- **S1. Show the product being used, within the first two seconds** Rule: In the first 2 seconds the book is on screen and being handled (opened, a page turned, a finger on the child's name), not a closed box, a logo card or a still. Strength: moderate. Look for: `product_first_seen_s` at or under 2; first shot's subject is the product being handled (`demonstration` not "none" in the opening); `opening_image` is not a logo card, closed box or text card; logo size and position in the first 3 s. (Overlaps H3, K5, C7.)
- **S2. Show the proof that settles the buyer's main doubt, in one steady shot** Rule: The Reel shows checkable proof of the claim that worries buyers most: for a personalised book, the child's real photo next to the illustrated page, and the child's name in the printed text, held still long enough to look (about 2 seconds or more). Strength: weak to moderate. Look for: `likeness_proof` = "photo beside illustration" or "name visible on page"; a shot with both photo and page in frame; that shot's length from `shots[]`; `proof` field not "none".
- **S3. If the price appears, show the whole price at once** Rule: When a price is in the video or caption, it is the full price in shekels, with delivery stated next to it (for example ₪119 and ₪30 home delivery, or free pickup), never a teaser price with fees appearing later. Strength: strong for the legal rule (if shown, the total); contested for whether showing a price raises sales. Look for: `price_on_screen`, `price_in_caption`; whether delivery or other fees appear in the same frame or line; "from" or "starting at" wording; price first appearing only inside a screen-recorded checkout (`screen_recording` = yes).
- **S4. Hands holding the product can carry the Reel; a face is optional** Rule: A Reel where an adult's hands hold, open and point at the book (no face) is a first-choice format; add a face only when someone needs to talk. Strength: moderate. Look for: `face_on_camera` = "no face (hands only)"; shots where a hand touches the product; ratio of buying questions to total comments (separate from likes). (Overlaps H4, K8, F6.)
- **S5. No identifiable child's face in LumeBook's own Reels; consent and no identifiers when a creator's child appears** Rule: LumeBook's own Reels show no identifiable child's face; a creator Reel with a child has the parent's written consent, never combines the child's face with name, gan, school or location, and shows nothing embarrassing. Strength: strong as a legal and policy risk rule (official Israeli guidance, Israeli privacy law, Meta policy). No study was found on whether child-free product Reels sell less. Look for: `child_on_camera`; child's face visible and identifiable (yes/no); child's name, age, gan or town in video, caption or hashtags together with the face; embarrassing moments (bath, potty, crying).
- **S6. Let real people vouch: creators, customers and visible customer proof** Rule: The Reel carries a real person's first-hand use or verdict (a parent or grandparent who owns the book), or visible real-customer proof (messages, reviews, counts), rather than brand adjectives. Strength: moderate. Look for: `creator_type`; `relationship`; the speaker owns and uses the book (first-person past tense in the transcript); on-screen customer message or review screenshot; `selling_language` dominated by brand adjectives versus first-hand detail.
- **S7. Disclose the partnership in words, at the start** Rule: A creator Reel says it is a partnership in words in the first caption line or on screen in the first seconds (for example "בשיתוף לומיבוק"), plus Instagram's paid-partnership label when money or goods changed hands. Strength: strong as an Israeli legal requirement. Look for: `disclosure_visible`; where it sits (first caption line, end of caption, on screen in 0 to 3 s, hashtag only); paid-partnership label present. (Overlaps T8.)
- **S8. Show the personalising and the child as the hero** Rule: The Reel shows the act of personalising (one photo, the name typed, the free preview) and its payoff (the child's own name and likeness inside the story), so a parent can picture their own child. Strength: moderate that customising raises the customer's own valuation (lab); weak that watching someone else personalise does the same (untested).. Look for: `screen_recording` = yes and shows upload or name entry; `likeness_proof` includes the name on a page; voice or text says the book was made for a named child.
## Q5. Editing

- **E1. Open with motion and the point inside 3 seconds** Rule: By second 3 the viewer has seen a change (a cut, movement or a face reacting) and knows what the Reel is about (spoken line, on-screen text or the book visible). Strength: moderate. Look for: `first_change_s` (time of first cut or large movement), `face_by_3s` (yes/no), `topic_clear_by_3s` (spoken line, text or product on screen by 3 s), `opening_type` (face reaction, product, question text, action, logo, static). (Overlaps H1, T3.)
- **E2. Cut to remove dead air, at a moderate pace, within one scene** Rule: Talking or demo sections use seamless jump cuts that remove pauses within the same scene, at roughly one cut every 2 to 5 seconds; the Reel avoids rapid unrelated scene changes, especially over emotional moments. Strength: moderate. Look for: `cuts_total` and `cuts_per_10s` (count shot changes from frames), `cut_type` per cut (jump cut same scene, scene change, transition effect), `longest_shot_s`, `cuts_during_peak_emotion` (count of cuts in the 3 s around the reaction). (Overlaps R3, F8.)
- **E3. Use speed changes on objects and movement, not on people "enjoying" the product** Rule: Slow motion, if used, is applied to pleasant, detailed movement (pages turning, a gift being unwrapped) and not to a person visibly enjoying the product; fast motion is reserved for energetic, exciting sequences. Strength: moderate. Look for: `speed_change` (none, slow, fast, ramp), `speed_subject` (object, hands, person face, crowd), `speed_moment` (reveal, transition, reaction).
- **E4. Build for sound on, and caption the speech in short synced chunks** Rule: The Reel carries its message in audio (voice or natural sound), is never silent, and shows short on-screen text or captions timed to the speech, inside the safe zone, never filling most of the frame. Strength: moderate. Look for: `audio_present` (yes/no), `audio_type` (speech to camera, voiceover, natural sound, music only, mixed), `captions_present`, `text_synced_to_speech` (text changes within 1 s of the line), `max_words_on_screen`, `text_area_share` (estimate of frame covered by text), `text_in_safe_zone`. (Overlaps H6, T1, T2.)
- **E5. Choose audio the account may use; treat trending audio as optional** Rule: Music in a LumeBook Reel comes from Meta's Sound Collection or is original audio, and a trending sound is used only if it is available to the account, fits the moment and is still early in its trend. Strength: strong for the licensing constraint (official), weak for any reach gain from trending audio (official but unquantified), moderate for music fit. Look for: `music_present`, `music_source` (licensed track named in the audio label, "Original audio", Sound Collection, unknown), `trend_audio` (yes/no, audio label used by many Reels), `music_fit` (mood matches action: yes/no), `voice_over_music` (speech audible above music).
- **E6. Look native, but technically clean** Rule: The Reel looks like a phone video from a real home (direct to camera, natural light, real setting), yet is sharp, well lit, full-screen 9:16 and uploaded in high resolution, with no borders or letterboxing. Strength: contested. Look for: `aspect_full_9x16` (yes/no), `borders_present`, `sharpness_ok` (subject in focus in key frames), `lighting_ok`, `setting` (home, studio, outdoor, store), `direct_address` (person speaks to lens), `production_style` (phone native, semi-produced, studio). (Overlaps H8, F7.)
- **E7. Every Reel is original: own footage, no foreign watermark, real added value** Rule: The Reel is LumeBook's own footage (or a creator's, posted as a Collab), carries no other app's watermark, and if it recreates a trend it adds its own scene, voice or commentary. Strength: strong. Look for: `foreign_watermark` (TikTok, CapCut or other logo visible), `reupload_suspected` (same video seen on another account), `collab_tag`, `trend_recreation` (yes/no), `added_value` (own footage, own voice, commentary, none). (Overlaps H7, F1, F2, F3, T4.)
- **E8. Stay under 3 minutes, end cleanly, and do not count on loops** Rule: The Reel is under 3 minutes (ideally as short as the story allows), and ends on its payoff or call to action without a dead tail; a seamless loop is optional, not a target. Strength: strong for the 3-minute cap. Look for: `duration_s`, `length_band` (0 to 15, 15 to 30, 30 to 60, 60 to 90, 90 to 180, over 180), `tail_after_payoff_s` (seconds after the last story beat), `end_type` (payoff, CTA card, logo card, loop to first frame, abrupt), `loop_seamless` (last frame matches first). (Overlaps R1, R2.)
## Q6. What the content is about

- **K1. Aim for a high-arousal positive feeling: surprise, joy, awe, warmth, amusement** Rule: The Reel builds to one clear moment of surprise, delight, awe, warmth or laughter, and does not rest on sadness or calm information. Strength: strong. Look for: `peak_emotion` (surprise, joy, awe, warmth, amusement, sadness, none), `peak_time_s`, `emotion_valence` (positive, negative, mixed), `arousal_level` (high, medium, low, judged from faces, voice and music). (Overlaps R5, C4.)
- **K2. Tell a mini story: a character, a want, a turn, a payoff** Rule: The Reel has a recognisable character (child, parent, grandparent), a setup, a turn or reveal, and a payoff, rather than a list of product features. Strength: strong for narrative persuasion in ads. Look for: `character` (child, parent, grandparent, expert, none), `arc_beats` (setup, turn, payoff present: count 0 to 3), `reveal_moment_s`, `format` (story, demo, list, tutorial, talking head opinion, trend recreation). (Overlaps H5, R4.)
- **K3. Use relatable, everyday humour** Rule: When the Reel is funny, the joke comes from a recognisable parenting or kid moment the viewer has lived, not from a gag unrelated to family life. Strength: strong. Look for: `humour_present`, `humour_source` (child behaviour, parent struggle, sibling, wordplay, unrelated gag), `relatable_parenting_moment` (yes/no), `expert_claim_in_joke` (a factual claim delivered as a joke).
- **K4. Give practical value, but wrap it in emotion or personality** Rule: Any useful tip or fact (reading at bedtime, why seeing their own name helps a child) is delivered inside a feeling, a story or a laugh, and any developmental claim is modest and checkable. Strength: contested in isolation (usefulness helps email sharing but hurts video-ad sharing), consistent when combined (information plus emotion beats either alone).. Look for: `info_units` (count of distinct facts or tips in speech and text), `info_with_emotion` (info delivered alongside a reaction, story or joke: yes/no), `price_or_deal_mentioned`, `developmental_claim` (text of the claim), `claim_strength` (modest, strong, medical-sounding). (Overlaps R7.)
- **K5. Make the product part of the story; keep brand talk short** Rule: The book is shown in use as part of the action (being opened, read, given), and explicit brand messaging (logo, name, offer) takes no more than about a quarter of the running time. Strength: contested. Look for: `product_first_s` (first time the book is on screen), `product_in_use` (opened, read, given: yes/no), `brand_mentions` (count of spoken or written brand name and logo), `brand_share_duration` (seconds with logo or brand text divided by duration), `giveaway` (yes/no), `cta_type`. (Overlaps H3, S1, C7.)
- **K6. Speak to a real parenting concern or make parents feel "not alone"** Rule: The Reel connects to something parents of 1 to 8 year olds actually deal with (bedtime, routines, emotions, a new sibling, starting kindergarten, reading) and leaves the viewer feeling understood or helped, without hype. Strength: moderate. Look for: `parent_concern` (sleep, routines, emotions, sibling, kindergarten, reading, screen time, none), `reassurance_line` (a "you're not alone" or "us too" moment: yes/no), `hype_words` (miracle, must-have, guaranteed), `speaker_role` (parent, expert, child, brand).
- **K7. Tie the Reel to a buying moment and to the person who buys** Rule: The Reel names or shows a gifting or life moment (birthday, new sibling, holiday, first day of kindergarten, grandparent visit) and makes clear who would buy it (parent or grandparent). Strength: weak to moderate. Look for: `occasion` (birthday, new sibling, holiday name, kindergarten start, grandparent visit, none), `buyer_shown` (parent, grandparent, other relative, none), `gift_moment` (wrapping, giving, unwrapping: yes/no), `occasion_in_caption`.
- **K8. Put a real face, ideally the child's reaction, on screen** Rule: A real person's face is visible for most of the Reel, with the child's reaction as the emotional centre, and a parent speaking to camera when there is talking. Strength: contested. Look for: `face_screen_share` (share of sampled frames with a face), `child_face_at_peak` (yes/no), `shot_scale_at_peak` (close-up, medium, wide), `direct_address` (yes/no).

## Q7. What is filmed and how

- **F1. Shoot vertical 9:16, full screen** Rule: Filmed upright at 9:16 and filling the screen; no letterboxed landscape clips, squares or borders. Strength: strong. Look for: width and height from `media_info` (9:16); black bars or borders in any frame; letterboxed inserts. (Overlaps H7, E7, F2, F3, T4.)
- **F2. Keep text, faces and the product inside the safe zone** Rule: Nothing important in the top 14%, the bottom 35% or the outer 6% of each side of the frame. Strength: strong as a platform spec for ads. Look for: position of text boxes, faces and the product against a 14 / 35 / 6 percent grid on the contact sheets; text or product cut by the caption or buttons. (Overlaps H7, E7, F1, F3, T4.)
- **F3. Original, sharp footage: no watermarks, borders, reposts or text-only slides** Rule: Footage shot for this Reel, sharp (1080 x 1920 or better, never below 720p), no other app's watermark, not mostly text, not already posted on Instagram, under 3 minutes. Strength: strong. Look for: watermark visible; borders; resolution in `media_info`; share of the runtime that is text cards; the same clip already posted elsewhere on Instagram. (Overlaps H7, E7, F1, F2, T4.)
- **F4. Fill the frame with what matters, close enough to read on a phone** Rule: In key shots the book page, the name or the speaker fills most of the frame, and any text on the product is readable at phone size; avoid wide shots where the book is small. Strength: weak. Look for: `shots[].framing` = close-up for product shots; share of shots where the product fills more than half the frame; whether on-page text is legible on the contact sheet at phone size.
- **F5. Hands touching the product, from the viewer's point of view** Rule: Main product shots are POV or top-down, with a hand opening, turning or pointing at the book, the book facing the viewer the way they would hold it. Strength: moderate. Look for: `shots[].framing` = POV or top-down; hand in contact with the product (yes/no); perspective first-person or third-person.
- **F6. When someone talks, they talk to the viewer** Rule: Talking segments have the speaker facing the camera, eyes on the lens, speaking to "you". Strength: moderate for the mechanism (direct address builds a felt connection.
