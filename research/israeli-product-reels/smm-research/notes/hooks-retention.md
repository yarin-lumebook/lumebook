# Hooks, retention and interaction: evidence notes

Scope: the first 0 to 3 seconds of a Reel (hooks), what keeps people watching through the middle, and what prompts comments, sends and saves. Built for LumeBook's product Reels playbook (personalised children's books, Israel, Hebrew audience, parents of children about 1 to 8 and grandparents buying gifts, filmed with a phone at home).

All sources accessed 2026-10-18. Only sources opened in this task are cited. Evidence types: **official** (Instagram, Meta, TikTok, YouTube or Google), **peer-reviewed**, **industry study** (sample stated), **practitioner** (opinion). Most platform numbers below come from paid ads, not organic Reels; each bullet says which.

Fields named in "How to spot it" refer to the existing coding brief (`src/CODING_BRIEF.md`): `hook_0_3s.*`, `product_first_seen_s`, `value_understandable_s`, `shots`, `n_shots_estimate`, `editing_rhythm`, `audio.*`, `captions_on_screen`, `ending_and_cta`, `cta_type`, `comment_word`, `face_on_camera`, `child_on_camera`, `brand`.

## 1. Summary

- **Instagram measures the 3-second skip directly and uses it in ranking.** Meta's Reels ranking system card lists a prediction of whether you will watch under three seconds of a reel, with skips within two seconds as an input, and Instagram's Insights now show a skip rate for the first 3 seconds. This is the strongest official support for treating 0 to 3 s as a test every Reel must pass.
- **The top ranking signals are watch time, likes and sends.** Mosseri (Jan 2025): likes weigh slightly more for followers (connected reach), sends slightly more for non-followers (unconnected reach). Meta's system card scores watch time against other Reels *of the same length*, so a shorter Reel that is watched further can beat a long one.
- **Say what it is early, but do not plaster the brand.** Platform ad research (TikTok 2020, Meta and Toluna 2025, Google) links early key message and early product to better outcomes. Peer-reviewed eye-tracking work (Teixeira, Wedel and Pieters 2010) finds prominent central branding increases ad avoidance, and brief repeated brand moments ("pulsing") reduce it. Meta's own 2025 Reels study points the same way: product shown more than once, brand on screen no more than 25% of the time.
- **A person speaking in the first 3 seconds helps early retention.** An industry study of 10,110 brand Facebook Reels (Emplifi, 2026) found speech in the first 3 s lifted 10-second retention by about 25% over music-only, and a person on screen lifted it by about 10%, though the person effect faded by 30 s and reach was slightly lower.
- **Curiosity that is resolved works, in lab research.** Loewenstein's information-gap theory and Ruan, Hsee and Lu (2018, seven studies) support opening a question and paying it off; adding an uncertainty-then-resolution to an ad improved attitudes and willingness to try. Instagram's own Reels guide suggests posing a question in text early and answering it a few seconds later.
- **Length: no magic number, but stay well under 3 minutes.** Instagram's creator FAQ says Reels up to 3 minutes are recommended to non-followers. Socialinsider (about 6 million brand Reels, Jan to Jun 2026) found 45 to 60 s had the best engagement and median views; very short videos have the best completion (TikTok data). All length data is correlational.
- **Reels are the reach format; carousels win engagement.** Buffer (4M+ posts): Reels reach 36% more people than carousels, carousels get 12% more engagement. Judge Reels on skip rate, watch time and sends per reach, not engagement rate alone.
- **Widely quoted stats are weak or myths.** The "8-second attention span" has no traceable data; "85% watch without sound" is 2016 publisher data on Facebook video, not Reels; "65% who watch 3 s watch 10 s" and "47% of value in 3 s" are 2016 Facebook ad figures with no published method. No Israel, Hebrew or RTL specific evidence was found.

## 2. Principles

### Hooks (0 to 3 seconds)

#### H1. Pass the 3-second skip test

- **Rule:** Within the first 3 seconds the Reel gives a concrete reason to keep watching (a spoken line, an on-screen headline or a striking visual of the product or a reaction), with no intro, greeting, logo card or slow setup before it.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Instagram Reels Chaining system card](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-reels-chaining/), Meta Transparency Center, last updated 11 Nov 2025, official. Among the predictions the Reels ranking model makes: "How likely you are to watch less than three seconds of a reel". Inputs to that prediction include how often a reel was skipped within two seconds of opening, whether sound was on, and how many users watched at least three seconds. No weights are published.
  - [Instagram Adds Retention Metrics for Reels](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/), Social Media Today (Andrew Hutchinson), 24 Aug 2025, reporting an Instagram @creators Threads post (official, secondhand). Instagram replaced "view rate" with "skip rate": the share of views where the viewer skipped during the first 3 seconds; it added a retention chart per Reel.
  - [Improving how reels are ranked and recommended](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/helping-creators-of-all-sizes-break-through), Instagram for Creators, 26 Feb 2025, official. Advises: "Start with a compelling hook in the first 3 seconds." Lists watch time, retention, shares, likes and comments as ranking signals; a new Reel is first shown to a small group of non-followers, and top performers are shown to larger groups.
  - [Instagram for Creators FAQ](https://creators.instagram.com/faq?locale=en_US), Instagram, undated (2026 footer), official. Advises making the first 3 seconds engaging so viewers do not scroll past.
  - [Instagram gives creators more insight into their reels' performance](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/29/instagram-gives-creators-more-insight-into-their-reels-performance/), TechCrunch (Aisha Malik), 29 Jan 2025, official statement reported. Instagram added "view rate" (share who keep watching after 3 s) and said the opening seconds matter most.
  - [Your guide to getting started with YouTube Shorts](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/your-guide-to-getting-started-with-youtube-shorts/), YouTube Team, 8 Apr 2025, official (YouTube Shorts). Advises capturing attention in the first few seconds to stop viewers scrolling.
  - [Capturing Attention in Feed: The Science Behind Effective Video Creative](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/insights/capturing-attention-feed-video-creative), Facebook IQ, 20 Apr 2016, official research (paid ads in Facebook feed; Fors Marsh Group tests plus internal ratings of 850+ ads). People recall mobile feed content at a significant rate after 0.25 s of exposure; average time on a piece of feed content was 1.7 s on mobile vs 2.5 s on desktop.
  - [Understanding attention on TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/mediascience-study-brands-memorable-tiktok), TikTok for Business with MediaScience, 22 Sep 2021, official commissioned lab study (343 US respondents aged 18 to 45, eye tracking, heart rate, skin response; paid ads). Ads on screen for 6 s or less still delivered 38% of the recall of ads viewed 20 s or more; engagement appeared in the first few seconds regardless of total view time.
- **Strength:** strong. Instagram officially measures the under-3-second skip and uses it as a ranking prediction; the exact weight is unpublished.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** Open on the finished book or the moment it lands (a child's face reacting, the name on the cover), not on "hi, today I want to show you". Read skip rate in Insights per Reel.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `hook_0_3s.opening_image` shows the product, a reaction or a person mid-action (not a logo or blank set); `hook_0_3s.spoken` starts with content, not a greeting or name; `hook_0_3s.on_screen_text` is present and readable; first cut or visual change in `shots` before 3 s. Tag "slow open" when 0 to 3 s is a greeting, intro or static establishing shot.

#### H2. Make the value clear early (result or promise first)

- **Rule:** By about 3 to 5 seconds a viewer can tell what the product is and what it gives the child or parent (the result shown, or the promise stated).
- **Evidence:**
  - [9 creative tips to drive auction ad performance](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance), TikTok for Business, 21 Oct 2020, official (paid ads, no sample or method given). Over 63% of the highest-CTR videos highlight their key message or product within the first 3 seconds.
  - [Deconstructing the Power of Reels](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/reels-creative-strategies), Meta for Business, 2 Dec 2025, official research with Toluna (paid Reels ads). Showing brand and main message within the first 5 s made a creative 1.7x more likely to rank in the top 20% for purchase intent. Method per [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-and-toluna-study-finds-emojis-lift-reels-purchase-intent-2-5x/) (Luis Rijo, 18 May 2026): 100 Instagram Reels ads, 65 creative variables, 100 participants per ad in a simulated Reels feed, Germany, UK, Italy, Turkey, research run in 2024. Correlational, forced exposure.
  - [The Science of the Hook](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-science-of-the-hook-how-to-supercharge-your-reels-performance), Meta for Business, 15 Dec 2025, official guidance (paid Reels). Names three text-hook types: value promise (show the viewer's benefit at once), statement of intent (say what they will see or learn), question or invitation. Case study for a kids' education brand (KiwiCo) opened on relatable moments such as discovery, unboxing and parent-child bonding rather than polished product shots.
  - [Capturing Attention in Feed](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/insights/capturing-attention-feed-video-creative), Facebook IQ, 20 Apr 2016, official (paid ads; 600+ ads matched to 200+ Nielsen Brand Effect studies). Ads that showed the product and delivered the message succinctly had the strongest ad recall.
- **Strength:** moderate. Consistent across TikTok, Meta and Google, but all from paid ads, all correlational, and none publishes full methods.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** Show the result first: the personalised cover with the child's name, or the child seeing themself in the book, then explain.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `product_first_seen_s` at or under 3; `value_understandable_s` at or under 5; `hook_0_3s.on_screen_text` or `spoken` states a benefit or outcome ("he saw his own name" type) rather than a teaser with no subject.

#### H3. Show the product early, but keep the brand light and repeated (pulse, do not plaster)

- **Rule:** The product appears early and more than once inside the action (in hands, on the page, in the child's lap); the logo or brand name is brief and recurring, not a static central card, and on screen for a small part of the Reel.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Moment-to-moment optimal branding in TV commercials: preventing avoidance by pulsing](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/moment-to-moment-optimal-branding-in-tv-commercials-preventing-av/), Teixeira, Wedel and Pieters, *Marketing Science* 29(5): 783 to 804, 2010, DOI 10.1287/mksc.1100.0567, peer-reviewed (TV ads; eye tracking and zapping, 31 commercials, nearly 2,000 participants). Central on-screen brand positions increased avoidance (brand size did not); pulsing brand exposure, with total exposure held constant, significantly reduced avoidance. [HBS Working Knowledge summary](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/improving-brand-recognition-in-tv-ads) (Julia Hanna, 7 Jun 2010): pulsing cut zapping by 8 to 10% on average, up to 25% for some ads; mystery ads that reveal the brand late fail if viewers zap first.
  - [Deconstructing the Power of Reels](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/reels-creative-strategies), Meta and Toluna, 2 Dec 2025, official (paid Reels, sample above). Brand shown more than once in different scenes: 1.8x likelier top-20% purchase intent; product shown more than once: 2.7x; brand featured for no more than 25% of the duration: 4.8x (direct-response ads).
  - [The First Five Seconds: Creating YouTube Ads That Break Through](https://business.google.com/aunz/think/marketing-strategies/creating-youtube-ads-that-break-through-in-a-skippable-world/), Think with Google (YouTube Insights Team), 16 Jun 2015, official industry study (thousands of skippable YouTube ads, 16 countries, 11 verticals, 170 coded attributes, Jan 2012 to Feb 2014; paid ads). Brand in the first 5 s was linked to higher recall and awareness but also more skipping; logos on the product beat floating logos.
  - [About the ABCDs of effective video ads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551?hl=en), Google Ads Help, undated, official (paid YouTube incl. Shorts). Recommends showing the brand early and keeping it present, and for Shorts making branding organic to the story; cites a Google and Kantar study (Apr 2021) with up to 30% lift in short-term sales likelihood; sample not given on the page.
  - [On the Development and Strength of Category-Brand Associations in Memory: The Case of Mystery Ads](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=development+strength+category-brand+associations+memory+case+of+mystery+ads+Fazio), Fazio, Herr and Powell, *Journal of Consumer Psychology* 1(1): 1 to 13, 1992, peer-reviewed (forced-exposure experiment). Ads that identified the brand only at the end built stronger category-brand links than identical ads naming it early, most of all for unfamiliar brands. Also cited in Loewenstein (1994, footnote 1).
  - [Understanding Creative Fatigue, as summarised in Creativity pays dividends](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/creative-effectiveness), TikTok for Business with System1, 5 May 2025, official commissioned study (8,000 heavy TikTok users in US, UK, DE, FR, IT, ES; paid ads). Ads with brand recognition in the first 2 s generated 57% more happiness and 19% less attention decay.
- **Strength:** contested. Recall and brand-link research favours early or late branding depending on the measure; avoidance research says prominent central branding drives skipping. The reconciliation all sources allow: product early and woven into the story, brand marks brief and repeated.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** LumeBook is an unfamiliar brand, so the product (the personalised book) should be seen early and often, while the LumeBook logo or name stays small and occasional (on the cover, in the caption, one end card at most).
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** count product appearances across `shots` (2 or more = repeated); `brand` visible on the product vs as a separate logo card or watermark; logo centred and held for more than 2 s = "plastered"; estimate share of duration with a logo or brand card on screen.

#### H4. Open with a person, ideally a face and a voice

- **Rule:** A person appears and speaks (to camera or in voiceover) within the first 3 seconds.
- **Evidence:**
  - [What drives Facebook Reels performance](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/), Emplifi (Jitka Skalicka), 19 Mar 2026, industry study; sample per [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/human-speech-and-presence-help-drive-reels-engagement/815381/) (Andrew Hutchinson, 22 Mar 2026): 10,110 Facebook Reels from 704 brand Pages. Speech in the first 3 s vs music-only: 3-s retention +11.7%, 10-s retention +24.7%, 30-s +6.6%, sound-on 3-s views +8.8%, reach -3.3%. A person on screen for at least 1 s in the first 3 s: 3-s retention +3.6%, 10-s +10.1%, but 30-s -2.4% and reach -7.4%. 61.8% of brand Reels had no speech. Facebook Reels, brands only, correlational.
  - [9 creative tips to drive auction ad performance](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance), TikTok, 21 Oct 2020, official (paid ads, no sample). One in three highest-view-through ads address the viewer directly, often looking into the camera; creator-led videos made better-performing ads.
  - [The First Five Seconds](https://business.google.com/aunz/think/marketing-strategies/creating-youtube-ads-that-break-through-in-a-skippable-world/), Think with Google, 16 Jun 2015, official industry study (sample above; YouTube ads). A recognisable face and smiling characters were associated with more viewing and brand lift.
  - [Faces engage us: photos with faces attract more likes and comments on Instagram](https://www.news.gatech.edu/news/2014/03/20/face-it-instagram-pictures-faces-are-more-popular), Bakhshi, Shamma and Gilbert, CHI 2014, DOI 10.1145/2556288.2557403 ([metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145/2556288.2557403)), peer-reviewed (1.1 million Instagram photos, face detection; photos not Reels). Photos with faces were 38% more likely to get likes and 32% more likely to get comments; the number of faces, age (children vs adults) and gender made no difference.
  - [Deconstructing the Power of Reels](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/reels-creative-strategies), Meta and Toluna, 2 Dec 2025, official (paid Reels). People in everyday activities ("slice of life"): 1.5x likelier top-20% purchase intent.
- **Strength:** moderate. Several independent sources agree on early retention; the Emplifi data shows the person effect fades by 30 s and reach was lower, so it is not a free win.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** Yarin's face or voice in the first 3 s (a short spoken line works even if the child is not filmed); a child's face is not required for the hook, and Bakhshi found kids' faces no more engaging than adults' in photos.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `face_on_camera` at 0 to 3 s; `audio.speech` = direct-to-camera or voiceover with `hook_0_3s.spoken` starting before 3 s; tag "music-only open" when the transcript is empty or lyrics in 0 to 3 s.

#### H5. Open a curiosity gap and close it

- **Rule:** The opening poses a specific question or an unresolved situation (what will the child do when they see it? how is the face made?) and the Reel visibly answers it later.
- **Evidence:**
  - [The Psychology of Curiosity: A Review and Reinterpretation](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/PsychofCuriosity.pdf), Loewenstein, *Psychological Bulletin* 116(1): 75 to 98, 1994, DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75, peer-reviewed review. Curiosity is a form of cognitively induced deprivation arising from a perceived gap in knowledge; it is intense, transient, linked to impulsivity and tends to disappoint when satisfied.
  - [The Teasing Effect: An Underappreciated Benefit of Creating and Resolving an Uncertainty](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=The+teasing+effect+underappreciated+benefit+creating+resolving+uncertainty+Ruan+Hsee), Ruan, Hsee and Lu, *Journal of Marketing Research* 55: 556 to 570, 2018, DOI 10.1509/jmr.15.0346, peer-reviewed (seven studies). First creating then resolving an uncertainty gave a better experience than getting all information at once; adding this to an ad improved attitude to the product and willingness to try it.
  - [Emotion-Induced Engagement in Internet Video Advertisements](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1509/jmr.10.0207), Teixeira, Wedel and Pieters, *Journal of Marketing Research* 49(2): 144 to 159, 2012, DOI 10.1509/jmr.10.0207, peer-reviewed (controlled experiment, eye tracking, facial expression, zapping; internet video ads). Surprise and joy concentrate attention and retain viewers; the level of surprise matters for attention.
  - [How to make Instagram reels](https://about.instagram.com/blog/tips-and-tricks/how-to-make-instagram-reels), Instagram, undated (2026 guide), official. Suggests layering text overlays, for example posing a question early and answering it a few seconds later to build curiosity.
  - [The Science of the Hook](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-science-of-the-hook-how-to-supercharge-your-reels-performance), Meta, 15 Dec 2025, official (paid Reels). "Question or invitation" is one of three named hook types.
- **Strength:** moderate. Strong lab theory and official guidance; no Reels-specific causal data. The payoff must deliver, or curiosity turns to disappointment (Loewenstein).
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** "Watch his face when he finds his name" or "guess which page made her laugh", answered on screen within the Reel.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `hook_0_3s.spoken` or `on_screen_text` contains a question, a "wait for it" or an unfinished situation; mark the timestamp where it is answered (payoff seen in `shots`); tag "unpaid loop" if the question is never answered.

#### H6. Deliver the hook in sound and on screen

- **Rule:** The Reel has audio (speech and or music, never muted), and the hook is both said or heard and shown (on-screen text or a clear visual), so it works with sound on and off.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Deconstructing the Power of Reels](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/reels-creative-strategies), Meta and Toluna, 2 Dec 2025, official (paid Reels). Hook delivered with both visuals and sound: 1.5x likelier top-20% purchase intent; message through audio and visuals: 1.8x for brand interest; speech plus music: 2.0x (brand interest) and 2.1x (purchase intent).
  - [The Science of the Hook](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-science-of-the-hook-how-to-supercharge-your-reels-performance), Meta, 15 Dec 2025, official (paid Reels). Reels with music or voiceover showed up to 13% higher incremental conversions (footnote not shown).
  - [Instagram Ranking Explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained), Adam Mosseri, Instagram, 31 May 2023, official. Instagram tries to make muted Reels, low-resolution or watermarked Reels, Reels with borders and Reels that are mostly text less visible.
  - [What drives Facebook Reels performance](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/), Emplifi, 19 Mar 2026, industry study (Facebook brand Reels, sample above). Text overlays raised 3-s retention 5.1% and 10-s retention 7.6% overall, but on speech-led videos text lowered 10-s retention by 6%.
  - [Capture attention with updated features for video ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/updated-features-for-video-ads), Facebook for Business, undated (Feb 2016 per the [Spanish version](https://about.fb.com/es/news/2016/02/capta-la-atencion-con-nuevas-funcionalidades-para-anuncios-de-video/amp/)), official (Facebook feed video ads). Internal tests: captioned video ads increased average view time by 12%.
  - [How to make Instagram reels](https://about.instagram.com/blog/tips-and-tricks/how-to-make-instagram-reels), Instagram, undated, official. Recommends captions for spoken audio alongside text overlays.
  - [YouTube Shorts ads: asset specs and best practices](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16041697?hl=en), Google Ads Help, undated, official (Shorts ads). Sound (music, voiceover or both) said to increase conversions by over 20%, no study cited.
- **Strength:** moderate. Official on muted Reels being less visible; the lift figures are paid-ads and unmethoded.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** Speak the hook and put a short Hebrew headline on screen; add burned-in Hebrew captions for speech. Keep headline text short when someone is talking (Emplifi's speech-plus-text dip).
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `audio.speech` and `audio.music`; `captions_on_screen` = burned-in subtitles or headline; hook present in both `hook_0_3s.spoken` and `hook_0_3s.on_screen_text`; tag "mostly text" when text covers most of the frame for most of the Reel.

#### H7. Make the first frame work as a cover: vertical, clear, native

- **Rule:** The Reel is full-screen vertical (9:16), the first frame and chosen cover show the subject large and readable, the headline sits inside the safe zone, and there are no borders, watermarks or other-app logos.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Instagram Reels Chaining system card](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-reels-chaining/), Meta, 11 Nov 2025, official. One prediction is how likely you are to tap one of the tall rectangular Reels in Explore to open it full screen: in Explore, the cover image is what earns that tap.
  - [Instagram Ranking Explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained), Mosseri, 31 May 2023, official. Low-resolution, watermarked, bordered, mostly-text and already-posted Reels are made less visible.
  - [What drives Facebook Reels performance](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/), Emplifi, 19 Mar 2026, industry study (Facebook brand Reels). Vertical videos had 20.9% higher reach and 9.1% higher 3-s retention than other formats.
  - [9 creative tips](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance), TikTok, 2020, official (paid ads). Vertical TikToks had 25% higher 6-second watch-through (comparison group not named).
  - [Unlock the potential of Reels](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2024/11/04/unlock-potential-reels-best-practices-for-creative-success-using-adobe-creative-cloud), Meta byline on Adobe blog, 4 Nov 2024, official claim, promotional context (paid Reels; no sample). 9:16, sound on, key message in safe zones plus one extra element (person, text, voiceover, lo-fi or early hook): 20% lower cost per click, 56% higher CTR, 30% higher 3-s play rate.
- **Strength:** moderate. Official on what is demoted; no study isolates the cover frame itself.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** Film vertically on the phone, pick a cover that shows the open book or the child's reaction, keep Hebrew headline text away from the bottom and right-side buttons, never repost TikTok downloads with watermarks.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** aspect ratio from frames; black or blurred borders; watermark or another app's logo; the first frame in `sheet_01` vs the grid cover; text overlapping the bottom caption area or right-side button column.

#### H8. Look like a creator, not an ad

- **Rule:** The Reel looks phone-made and personal (handheld or tripod at home, natural sound, real moments, direct address), not like a polished TV commercial.
- **Evidence:**
  - [What is Instagram's Adam Mosseri really saying in his year-end memo](https://om.co/2026/01/01/what-is-instagrams-adam-mosseri-really-saying-in-his-year-end-memo/), Om Malik, 1 Jan 2026, reporting Mosseri's 20-slide Instagram post of 31 Dec 2025 (official statement, secondhand; Malik's interpretation separated). Mosseri: the polished feed is effectively over, most sharing now happens in DMs with blurry photos and shaky videos, imperfection is becoming a signal of realness, and Instagram plans to rank for originality.
  - [9 creative tips](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance), TikTok, 2020, official (paid ads). Creator-led videos produced better-performing ads; shoot natively; almost half of best performers tell their story in emotionally appealing ways.
  - [The Science of the Hook](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-science-of-the-hook-how-to-supercharge-your-reels-performance), Meta, 15 Dec 2025, official (paid). KiwiCo (kids' learning kits) used relatable discovery, unboxing and parent-child moments instead of polished product shots; combined Reels-style and standard creative gave 30% higher return on ad spend (case study, no method).
  - [Instagram for Creators FAQ](https://creators.instagram.com/faq?locale=en_US), official. Original content gets better reach; reposting others' work can limit distribution.
- **Strength:** moderate for "original, native" (official); weak for "lo-fi beats polished" as a measured effect (case studies and platform claims only).
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** This fits a solo SMM filming at home: real reading moments, real reactions, Yarin's own voice. Do not reuse other creators' clips.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `shots[].movement` handheld; home setting; `format` (talking-head, hands-only demo, unboxing, reaction); `relationship` = brand_repost vs creator_posted; studio lighting, logo animations or TV-style voiceover tagged as "ad look".

### Retention and interaction (3 seconds to the end)

#### R1. Earn watch time relative to length: cut every second that does not move the story

- **Rule:** No dead air or repeated setup in the middle; the Reel ends soon after the payoff, so most viewers reach the end.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Instagram Reels Chaining system card](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-reels-chaining/), Meta, 11 Nov 2025, official. A ranking prediction is how likely you are to watch more of a reel than 95% of people who watched reels of similar length; inputs include the reel's length and time spent viewing it. Watch time is judged against Reels of similar length.
  - [Instagram Shares Algorithm Insights To Inform Strategy](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/), Social Media Today (Andrew Hutchinson), 22 Jan 2025, reporting Mosseri's Instagram video (official, secondhand). Mosseri: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends." He advised watching average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach in Insights.
  - [Instagram Ranking Explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained), Mosseri, 31 May 2023, official. The most important Reels predictions: likelihood to reshare, watch all the way through, like, and visit the audio page.
  - [Instagram Adds Retention Metrics for Reels](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/), Social Media Today, 24 Aug 2025 (Instagram @creators, secondhand). Instagram on the retention chart: the line always slopes down, but a flatter line means a more engaged audience.
- **Strength:** strong. Multiple official statements; weights unpublished.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** Trim openings and endings hard; a 20-second Reel watched to the end is better placed than a 60-second one abandoned at 15.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** duration vs time of payoff (seconds after payoff before the end); shots with no new information in `shots[].purpose`; long static holds (one shot over about 6 s with nothing new) in `editing_rhythm`.

#### R2. Choose length by content, stay well under 3 minutes

- **Rule:** Use about 7 to 15 s for a single visual moment or loop, about 30 to 60 s for a demo or story, and never more than 3 minutes.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Instagram for Creators FAQ](https://creators.instagram.com/faq?locale=en_US), official, undated. Reels up to 3 minutes are recommended to audiences who do not follow you.
  - [How to make Instagram reels](https://about.instagram.com/blog/tips-and-tricks/how-to-make-instagram-reels), Instagram, undated, official. Length tiers: 3 to 15 s for quick transitions and trends, 15 to 60 s for tutorials and most formats, 60 s to 3 min for storytelling and detailed how-tos; suggests starting with 15 s. Reported by [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-reels-creation-tips-in-new-guide/831551/), 28 Sep 2026.
  - [What Is the Ideal Instagram Reels Length?](https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/), Socialinsider (Elena Cucu), 14 Jul 2026, industry study (about 6 million brand Reels, Jan to Jun 2026; account count not stated; correlational). 45 to 60 s had the highest engagement rate (0.35%) and median views (10,374); 0 to 30 s: 0.28% and 4,700; 60 to 90 s: 0.30% and 9,790; over 180 s: 0.15% and 4,428.
  - [How Long Should TikTok Videos Be?](https://www.fanpagekarma.com/insights/optimal-tiktok-video-length/), Fanpage Karma, undated, industry study (32,000 TikTok videos from one month; TikTok, not Reels). 0 to 10 s videos had by far the highest completion and highest reach per follower; 30 to 90 s had the highest interaction rates; reach fell sharply over 90 s.
  - [YouTube Shorts ads: asset specs and best practices](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16041697?hl=en), Google Ads Help, official (Shorts ads). Keep under 60 s; 10 to 30 s for action-oriented ads.
- **Strength:** moderate for the 3-minute ceiling (official); weak for any exact optimum (correlational, brand accounts, and better accounts may simply make longer Reels).
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** Reaction or reveal Reels at 7 to 20 s; "how it is made" or "what is inside the book" at 30 to 60 s. Test length with Trial Reels rather than trusting a benchmark.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** duration from `index.json`; bucket it (0 to 15, 15 to 30, 30 to 60, 60 to 90, 90+); compare with `format`.

#### R3. Keep the middle moving: a new beat every few seconds, information inside entertainment

- **Rule:** Each shot in the middle adds something new (a new page, a reaction, a step, a change of angle), and product facts are delivered inside an emotional or entertaining moment rather than as a dry list.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Why Do Consumers Stop Viewing Television Commercials?](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=Why+do+consumers+stop+viewing+television+commercials+Woltman+Elpers), Woltman Elpers, Wedel and Pieters, *Journal of Marketing Research* 40: 437 to 453, 2003, DOI 10.1509/jmkr.40.4.437.19393, peer-reviewed (two experiments, TV ads). Moment-to-moment entertainment raised the likelihood of continuing to watch; moment-to-moment information value lowered it; the two interact strongly (multiplicative effect on stopping).
  - [Emotion-Induced Engagement in Internet Video Advertisements](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1509/jmr.10.0207), Teixeira, Wedel and Pieters, *JMR* 2012, peer-reviewed (internet video ads). Joy and surprise retain viewers; for retention the *rate of change* of joy mattered more than its level, and gains from rising joy outweighed losses from falling joy.
  - [Creative Best Practices for TikTok Ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads), TikTok, undated, official, citing a Neuro-Insight "Global Information Density" study (2022). Faster scene changes typically draw viewers in early; no figures given.
  - [Unlock the potential of Reels](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2024/11/04/unlock-potential-reels-best-practices-for-creative-success-using-adobe-creative-cloud), Meta on Adobe blog, 4 Nov 2024, official (paid). Lists "digestible pacing" and relatable storytelling among Meta's Reels creative essentials.
- **Strength:** moderate. Peer-reviewed for TV and internet ads; no published data on cut rate for Reels. Rules like "cut every 2 to 3 seconds" are untraced (see Myths).
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** Alternate book detail with reaction (page, face, page, face); say the benefit ("this page has her dog in it") while the child is enjoying it, not as a feature list over a static cover.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `n_shots_estimate` and average shot length from `index.json` cuts (soft cuts may be missed); count new-information beats per 10 s; tag segments where spoken product facts play over a static shot with no reaction.

#### R4. Put the payoff near the end, and make short Reels loop

- **Rule:** The strongest moment (the reveal, the child's reaction) comes in the last seconds, and the last frame leads back into the first; for Reels under about 10 s, edit a seamless loop.
- **Evidence:**
  - [What drives Facebook Reels performance](https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/), Emplifi, 19 Mar 2026, industry study (Facebook brand Reels). Seamless loops in micro videos up to 7 s: replay rate +18.7%, reach +23.6%, engagement rate +16.1%; the benefit shrinks for longer videos because few viewers reach the loop point.
  - [Instagram Media Insights reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/reference/instagram-media/insights/), Meta for Developers, official. `ig_reels_video_view_total_time` counts total play time including replays; "views" is the number of times the media was played. Whether replays count toward ranking watch time is not stated.
  - [YouTube is changing how Shorts views are counted](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted), TechCrunch (Aisha Malik), 26 Mar 2025, official change reported (YouTube Shorts). Since 31 Mar 2025 a Shorts view counts each start or replay; the old metric is now "engaged views".
  - [The Teasing Effect](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=The+teasing+effect+underappreciated+benefit+creating+resolving+uncertainty+Ruan+Hsee), Ruan, Hsee and Lu, *JMR* 2018, peer-reviewed. The benefit comes from resolving the uncertainty; the resolution realises the curiosity built earlier.
- **Strength:** weak to moderate. One industry dataset on Facebook Reels; no official Instagram statement that replays raise ranking.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** End on the child's face at the reveal or on the closing of the book, cut so it flows back into the opening shot.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** timestamp of the payoff vs duration; last frame vs first frame similarity (same framing or object); `ending_and_cta` shows payoff vs a logo card or long talking outro.

#### R5. Give viewers someone to send it to

- **Rule:** The Reel contains a moment a parent or grandparent would send to a specific person: a gift idea, a relatable parenting moment, a strong emotion (awe, delight, surprise) or a useful tip.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Instagram Shares Algorithm Insights To Inform Strategy](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/), Social Media Today, 22 Jan 2025, reporting Mosseri (official, secondhand). Sends are one of the top three signals; sends are slightly more important for unconnected content (non-followers), likes slightly more for connected content.
  - [Instagram Reels Chaining system card](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-reels-chaining/), Meta, 11 Nov 2025, official. Separate predictions for resharing a reel and for sharing it off Instagram.
  - [Improving how reels are ranked and recommended](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/helping-creators-of-all-sizes-break-through), Instagram, 26 Feb 2025, official. People reshare Reels more than 4.5 billion times a day across Instagram and Facebook.
  - [Mosseri's year-end memo, via Om Malik](https://om.co/2026/01/01/what-is-instagrams-adam-mosseri-really-saying-in-his-year-end-memo/), 1 Jan 2026 (official, secondhand). Most sharing now happens in DMs.
  - [What Makes Online Content Viral?](https://jonahberger.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/ViralityB.pdf), Berger and Milkman, *Journal of Marketing Research* 49(2): 192 to 205, 2012, DOI 10.1509/jmr.10.0353, peer-reviewed (6,956 New York Times articles, 30 Aug to 30 Nov 2008, plus experiments; news articles, not video). Positive content was shared more than negative; high-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety) increased sharing and sadness decreased it; surprising, interesting and practically useful content were all positively linked to sharing.
- **Strength:** strong that sends matter (official); moderate on what drives them (peer-reviewed but news articles by email).
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** Grandparents and aunts are natural recipients: build Reels around "the gift that makes a child the hero" moments and reactions that trigger awe or delight, not sadness.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `emotion` (awe, delight, surprise vs calm or sad); presence of a gift framing or "send this to" in caption or text; a practical tip (age, occasion, how to order) on screen; `cta_type` includes save_or_share.

#### R6. Ask for comments with a real, specific question, and answer them; avoid bait

- **Rule:** The video or caption asks one concrete question viewers can answer from their own life (or offers a comment word that delivers something real), the account replies within the first days, and there is no "tag 3 friends" or "comment YES" for its own sake.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Instagram Reels Chaining system card](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-reels-chaining/), Meta, 11 Nov 2025, official. Includes a prediction of how likely you are to comment, using your past comments and the time viewers from your country spent on the reel.
  - [Popularity of Brand Posts on Brand Fan Pages](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/j.intmar.2012.01.003), de Vries, Gensler and Leeflang, *Journal of Interactive Marketing* 26(2): 83 to 91, 2012, DOI 10.1016/j.intmar.2012.01.003, peer-reviewed (355 posts from 11 brands on Facebook fan pages). Asking a question increased comments; likes and comments were driven by different post features.
  - [Instagram for Creators FAQ](https://creators.instagram.com/faq?locale=en_US), official. Liking or authentically replying to comments within the first 7 days can boost engagement and reach.
  - [Engagement Bait](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/content-distribution-guidelines/engagement-bait/), Meta Transparency Center, undated, official (the page refers to Facebook). Defines engagement bait as posts that explicitly ask for votes, shares, comments, tags, likes or reactions for purposes other than a specific call to action; the page does not state the penalty or whether it applies to Instagram.
  - [Instagram Organic Benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram), Socialinsider, 20 Feb 2026, industry study (35M posts, 447,613 pages, 2025). Reels drew the most comments per post at every account size.
  - [Instagram Shares Algorithm Insights](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/), 22 Jan 2025. Comments were not among Mosseri's top three signals.
- **Strength:** weak to moderate. Comments are an official ranking input but not a top signal; the question effect is from Facebook brand posts in 2012; no rigorous Instagram study on caption questions was found.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** Questions parents can answer from life ("what is the first word your child read?"), and comment-word offers that deliver something useful (sample page, price list) rather than bare "comment YES".
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** question in `on_screen_text`, transcript or caption; `cta_type` and `comment_word`; whether the comment word delivers a concrete thing; tag/share/vote demands in caption ("tag", "share with 3 friends"); creator replies visible in comments (if collected).

#### R7. Earn saves with something worth returning to

- **Rule:** When the goal is saves, the Reel includes reusable information (a list, an age or occasion guide, steps to order) held on screen long enough to read.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Instagram Ranking Explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained), Mosseri, 31 May 2023, official. Reels you have liked, saved, reshared or commented on feed the "user activity" signal.
  - [Instagram Reels Chaining system card](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-reels-chaining/), Meta, 11 Nov 2025, official. Saves are an input to the "Interested" prediction.
  - [Instagram Organic Benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram), Socialinsider, 20 Feb 2026, industry study (35M posts, 2025). Carousels led average saves at most account sizes.
  - [What Makes Online Content Viral?](https://jonahberger.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/ViralityB.pdf), Berger and Milkman, 2012, peer-reviewed. Practical usefulness was positively linked to sharing (sharing, not saving).
- **Strength:** weak. Saves are an official input, but no study was found on what drives saves of Reels.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** "Gift ideas by age" or "what to write in the dedication" Reels; or put the list in a carousel, which collects saves better.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** list or step structure in `on_screen_text`; text held on screen 2 s or more per item; `cta_type` includes save_or_share.

#### R8. Use Reels for reach to new people, and judge them on reach signals

- **Rule:** Treat Reels as the format for reaching non-followers; judge each Reel on skip rate, average watch time, non-follower views and sends per reach, and test new hooks with Trial Reels before showing followers.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Data Shows Instagram Reels are Best For Reach, But Not Engagement](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reach-engagement-analysis/), Buffer (Kirsti Lang; analysis Julian Winternheimer), 23 Oct 2024, industry study (4M+ posts, Jan 2022 to Oct 2024, medians). Reels reached 36% more people than carousels and 125% more than single images; carousels got 12% more engagement than Reels.
  - [Instagram Organic Benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram), Socialinsider, 20 Feb 2026, industry study (35M posts, 447,613 pages, 2025; engagement = likes plus comments per follower). Engagement per post: carousels 0.55%, Reels 0.52%, images 0.37%.
  - [Instagram Ranking Explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained), 31 May 2023, official. Most Reels people see come from accounts they do not follow.
  - [Instagram will let video creators make trial Reels for non-followers](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/instagram-will-let-video-creators-make-trial-reels-for-non-followers-160016725.html), Engadget (Karissa Bell), 10 Dec 2024, official feature reported. Trial Reels are shown to non-followers first, not on the grid or followers' feeds; after 24 hours creators see views, shares, likes and comments.
  - [The Science of the Hook](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/the-science-of-the-hook-how-to-supercharge-your-reels-performance), Meta, 15 Dec 2025, official (paid). Recommends A/B testing versions with different hooks and tracking watch time.
- **Strength:** moderate. Two large datasets agree on direction (Socialinsider's engagement gap is narrow); the non-follower role of Reels is official.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** Post the same footage with two different first 3 seconds as Trial Reels; keep the one with the lower skip rate.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** not visible in frames; record per Reel from Insights (skip rate, average watch time, follower vs non-follower views, sends) when LumeBook's own Reels are coded. For the 173 creator Reels only public plays, likes and comments exist.

## 3. Myths and contested claims

| Claim | What we found when tracing it | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "Human attention span is 8 seconds, shorter than a goldfish" | Comes from a 2015 Microsoft Canada consumer report, which cited the website Statistic Brain. The WSJ (Jo Craven McGinty, 17 Feb 2017) and BBC More or Less (Simon Maybin, 10 Mar 2017), read in a [University of Wisconsin course reprint](https://internet.psych.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/532-Master/532-UnitPages/Unit-09/Goldfish_MythBusting.pdf), found the listed sources (US National Library of Medicine, AP) had no such data and Statistic Brain gave none; attention researchers say "attention span" is task-dependent and has not been shown to shrink. | Myth. Do not use. |
| "85% of videos are watched without sound" | [Digiday, 17 May 2016](https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/): figures from publishers (LittleThings about 85%, Mic 85% of 30-s views, PopSugar 50 to 80%, an agency 85 to 90%) on Facebook video, no stated method. Instagram says it makes muted Reels less visible (Ranking Explained, 2023); PPC Land (May 2026) attributes to Meta a figure of over 80% of daily Instagram Reels plays being sound-on, with no data shown. | Outdated and misapplied to Reels. Design for both sound on and off (H6). |
| "65% of people who watch the first 3 seconds watch 10 seconds; 45% watch 30 seconds" | Traced to [Facebook for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/updated-features-for-video-ads) (Feb 2016): Facebook video ads, footnote not shown, no method. | Traceable but old, paid-ads, unmethoded. Not evidence about Reels. |
| "47% of a video campaign's value is in the first 3 seconds, 74% in 10" | Traced to Facebook with Nielsen, [Feb 2016 post (Spanish)](https://about.fb.com/es/news/2016/02/capta-la-atencion-con-nuevas-funcionalidades-para-anuncios-de-video/amp/): up to 47% and up to 74%; value refers to recall, awareness or sales; method not on the page. | Traceable, old, paid-ads, an upper-bound figure. Use only as historical context. |
| "The first 3 seconds decide 65% of..." and similar | Appears to be a garbling of the 2016 Facebook 65% figure above. No separate source found. | Myth as worded. |
| "63% of top ads show the key message in the first 3 seconds" | Real: [TikTok, 21 Oct 2020](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance), about the highest-CTR auction ads. No sample, date range or method; describes winners only (no comparison with losers). | Real but weak (paid TikTok ads, correlational). |
| "90% of ad recall happens in the first 6 seconds" / "50% of impact in the first 2 seconds" | TikTok's [creative best practices page](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads) cites the 90% six-second figure to its 2020 "Value of View" analysis without method. The "first 2 seconds, 50%" figure appeared only in search snippets; we did not find it on a TikTok page we opened. | 6-s figure: vendor claim without method. 2-s figure: not verified. |
| "Sends are worth 3 to 5 times a like" | Not in Mosseri's January 2025 statement as reported; he said sends weigh slightly more for non-followers. | Untraced. Do not use. |
| "Watch time is the number one signal" | Mosseri named watch time, likes and sends as the top three; Meta's system card lists predictions with no weights. | Overstated. Say "one of the top three". |
| "A healthy skip rate is 30 to 40%; above 50% Instagram cuts distribution" | Repeated by third-party guides surfaced in search, attributed to an Instagram breakdown we could not find. No official threshold located. | Unverified. Compare skip rate against LumeBook's own Reels instead. |
| "Reels over 3 minutes are not recommended to non-followers" | Instagram's [creator FAQ](https://creators.instagram.com/faq?locale=en_US) says Reels up to 3 minutes are recommended to non-followers; Socialinsider states the limit without a source. | Supported by official wording (stated positively). |
| "Cut every 2 to 3 seconds" / "pattern interrupt every 3 seconds" | No study with a cut-rate threshold for Reels found. TikTok cites a Neuro-Insight study saying faster scene changes help early, with no figures. Annie Lang's peer-reviewed work on edits and attention exists (Journal of Broadcasting and Electronic Media, 2000) but we could not open an abstract, so it is not cited. | Untraced as a number. R3 states the supported version: new information or emotion every few seconds. |
| "Hook within 0.5 seconds" | Seen only in third-party blog search results; no platform or study source. | Untraced. |
| "Captions ending with a question get 2x more comments" | Only marketing blogs in search results, no traceable original. The best evidence is de Vries et al. 2012 on Facebook brand posts (questions raise comments). | Untraced number; direction moderately supported. |
| "Brand in the first second" vs "reveal the brand at the end" | Both have evidence: early brand helps recall but raises skipping (Google 2015); late reveal builds brand links in forced viewing, especially for new brands (Fazio et al. 1992), but fails if people scroll first (Teixeira summary). | Contested. Use H3: product early and repeated, logo light. |

## 4. Israel, Hebrew, parents and children

- **Israel and Hebrew:** No study, platform statement or dataset specific to Israel, Hebrew or right-to-left on-screen text was found for hooks, retention or interaction. All datasets above are US, European, Turkish or global. Whether Instagram's auto-captions support Hebrew was not verified in this task.
- **RTL text:** No evidence found. Practical inference (not evidence): the safe-zone advice from Meta applies; Hebrew headlines start at the right edge, where Instagram's action buttons sit, so test the text position on the phone.
- **Age of viewer:** Meta's hook article says younger audiences consume content about 3 times faster than older audiences (footnote not visible). Inference, not tested: Reels aimed at grandparents may tolerate a slightly slower start than Reels for young parents, but the 3-second skip still applies.
- **Children on camera:** Bakhshi et al. (2014) found photos of children no more engaging than photos of adults. Meta (23 Jul 2025, updated 23 Oct 2025, [announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/07/expanding-teen-account-protections-child-safety-features/)) applies stricter message settings, Hidden Words and hidden comments from potentially suspicious adults to adult-run accounts that primarily feature children, and avoids recommending such accounts to those adults; it does not say their content is recommended less to others. Whether a brand account like LumeBook's would be classified this way is not stated.
- **Children's products:** The only kids' product example in official guidance found was KiwiCo in Meta's hook article (relatable discovery, unboxing and parent-child moments). Meta's Toluna study covered CPG and e-commerce ads, not children's products specifically.
