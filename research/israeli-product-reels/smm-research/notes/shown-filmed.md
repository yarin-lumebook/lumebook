# What is shown and what is filmed: evidence notes for the product Reels playbook

For the LumeBook product Reels playbook (`research/israeli-product-reels/playbook.md`). Area: what a Reel shows (product, people, proof, price, children, disclosure) and how it is filmed (format, framing, shot types, light, setting). Research done 18 Oct 2026 with web search and direct reads of primary pages, papers' abstracts (via the DOI record, OpenAlex or Crossref when the publisher blocked access) and Israeli legal and government texts. Every source below was opened in this task; nothing is cited from memory.

Evidence labels used below:

- **Platform**: Instagram, Meta or TikTok's own statements or research. Usually paid-ad metrics (ad recall, cost per result) measured by the platform itself.
- **Peer-reviewed**: journal or conference paper, with DOI. Where only the abstract could be read, the note says so.
- **Industry**: a study by a research firm or university centre with a stated method.
- **Law / Gov**: Israeli law text or official guidance. Not legal advice.
- **Practitioner**: opinion or secondary reporting, used sparingly.

Transfer tags: **[organic IG]** directly about organic Instagram; **[IG/FB ads]** Meta paid ads; **[TikTok]**; **[e-com]** e-commerce product pages or lab shopping tasks; **[photos]** still images, not video; **[TV]** television ads.

## 1. Summary

- **Show the product being handled in the first seconds, not a logo.** Lab work on e-commerce video finds a "product in use" video raises purchase intention more than a video of the product alone, and platform recall studies say most ad recall is won in the first 2 to 6 seconds. A big centred logo early is linked to more skipping (TV data).
- **A face-free, hands-holding-the-book Reel is evidence-backed.** In 250,000+ brand image posts, images of an unseen person holding the product drew more purchase-intent comments than selfies with faces, which drew more likes. Faces lift likes on Instagram photos in one large study and showed no effect in another.
- **Keep children's faces out of LumeBook's own Reels.** Israel's Privacy Protection Authority (Nov 2025) recommends not showing a young child's face in public posts and reminds parents that using a person's image for profit without consent is a privacy violation; Meta now limits how accounts that mainly feature children are recommended. No study shows child-free Reels sell less.
- **Real people vouching beats brand claims, especially people like the buyer.** TikTok's own 220-study analysis, Facebook purchase data, and a 96-study meta-analysis of word of mouth all point the same way; similarity between viewer and sender matters on social platforms. For baby and child products, trust mattered more than parasocial closeness in a 1,050-reader survey.
- **If a price is shown, show the whole price.** Israeli law forbids publishing a price that is not the total price; drip pricing (fees revealed later) leaves buyers less satisfied. Evidence that showing a price at all lifts sales is missing, and one large field experiment found upfront fees reduced purchases: this is contested.
- **Disclose partnerships in words at the start.** The Israeli Attorney General's position (2021) says tags and hashtags are not enough. A 61-paper meta-analysis finds disclosure costs some credibility, but field data on 64,438 sponsored Instagram posts shows they still draw engagement.
- **Film 9:16, original, sharp, inside the safe zone.** Instagram says low-resolution, watermarked, bordered, mostly-text and recycled Reels are made less visible. Meta's Reels ad spec keeps the top 14%, bottom 35% and 6% of each side free of key content, which is stricter than the playbook's current "bottom fifth" and "subtitles in the lower third".
- **"Lo-fi" means style, not low quality.** TikTok says avoid glossiness but shoot hi-res; peer-reviewed work finds high-quality images engage more and production polish raises perceived expertise, not trust. Home look, good light, steady phone.

## 2. Principles

### What is shown

#### S1. Show the product being used, within the first two seconds

- **Rule:** In the first 2 seconds the book is on screen and being handled (opened, a page turned, a finger on the child's name), not a closed box, a logo card or a still.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Effect of Product Presentation Videos on Consumers' Purchase Intention](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.812579), Cheng, Shao and Zhang, Frontiers in Psychology, 2022. Peer-reviewed (three pre-experiments and two experiments; sample sizes not in the abstract). A product usage video raised purchase intention more than a product appearance video, through perceived diagnosticity and mental imagery; the gap held only when the product rating was high. **[e-com]**
  - [The Impact of Dynamic Presentation Format on Consumer Preferences for Hedonic Products and Services](https://doi.org/10.1509/jm.13.0521), Roggeveen, Grewal, Townsend and Krishnan, Journal of Marketing, 2015. Peer-reviewed (multiple studies). Video rather than still presentation increased preference and willingness to pay for hedonic options; choice of the hedonically superior option rose by more than 79%. A gift book is a hedonic product. **[e-com]**
  - [Brand collaborations: TikTok creators drive big results](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/brand-collaborations-tiktok-creators-drive-big-results), TikTok Marketing Science, 10 Jan 2022. Platform (about 2,800 ad inventory pieces from about 220 brand lift studies in five European markets, plus 5,000 Mindlab interviews). Showing the product in use drove a positive emotional response (reported for beauty ads). **[TikTok]**
  - [Capturing Attention in Feed: The Science Behind Effective Video Creative](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/insights/capturing-attention-feed-video-creative), Facebook IQ, 20 Apr 2016. Platform (over 600 ads matched to over 200 Nielsen Brand Effect studies; the page also cites 850 and 965 ads, so the exact sample is unclear). People recall mobile feed content after 0.25 seconds; recommends putting the brand and product up front. **[IG/FB ads]**
  - [Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf), TikTok, one-pager (file titled May 2023). Platform. Cites TikTok's 2020 "Value of View" analysis: 90% of ad recall impact and 80% of awareness impact come within 6 seconds; early product placement improves memorability (Neuro-Insight study for TikTok, 2022). **[TikTok]**
  - [Tailored campaigns](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/automation/tailored-campaigns), Meta Business, undated. Platform guidance. Key message or branding within the first 3 seconds ("the 3-second rule"); use people to show the brand in action and explain products. **[IG/FB ads]**
  - Counterpoint: [Moment-to-Moment Optimal Branding in TV Commercials](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.1100.0567), Teixeira, Wedel and Pieters, Marketing Science, 2010. Peer-reviewed (eye tracking and zapping data, 31 commercials, nearly 2,000 participants). Brand shown in a central on-screen position increased ad avoidance; brand size did not; "pulsing" the brand reduced avoidance. **[TV]**
- **Strength:** moderate. Lab experiments and platform recall data point the same way, but none tests organic Reels or books, and ad recall is not sales.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** open on hands opening the printed book at the hero page; keep the LumeBook logo small and later.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `product_first_seen_s` at or under 2; first shot's subject is the product being handled (`demonstration` not "none" in the opening); `opening_image` is not a logo card, closed box or text card; logo size and position in the first 3 s.

#### S2. Show the proof that settles the buyer's main doubt, in one steady shot

- **Rule:** The Reel shows checkable proof of the claim that worries buyers most: for a personalised book, the child's real photo next to the illustrated page, and the child's name in the printed text, held still long enough to look (about 2 seconds or more).
- **Evidence:**
  - [On Product Uncertainty in Online Markets: Theory and Evidence](https://doi.org/10.2307/41703461), Dimoka, Hong and Pavlou, MIS Quarterly, 2012. Peer-reviewed (eBay Motors transactions matched with 331 bidders). Uncertainty about the product cut price premiums more than uncertainty about the seller; diagnostic product descriptions and third-party assurances reduced product uncertainty. **[e-com]**
  - Cheng, Shao and Zhang 2022 (S1): the usage video worked through perceived diagnosticity, that is, how well it lets a buyer judge the product. **[e-com]**
  - No study was found that tests side-by-side, before/after or "photo beside result" visuals in social video.
- **Strength:** weak to moderate. The mechanism (diagnostic information reduces uncertainty) is well supported in e-commerce; the specific format is untested.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** likeness is the doubt ("will it look like my child?"). The playbook found only 5 of 36 personalised-book Reels showed photo beside illustration (recounted 18 Oct); this is an open gap LumeBook can fill without a child's face by using a consenting family's photo or Yarin's own (concept 2).
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `likeness_proof` = "photo beside illustration" or "name visible on page"; a shot with both photo and page in frame; that shot's length from `shots[]`; `proof` field not "none".

#### S3. If the price appears, show the whole price at once

- **Rule:** When a price is in the video or caption, it is the full price in shekels, with delivery stated next to it (for example ₪119 and ₪30 home delivery, or free pickup), never a teaser price with fees appearing later.
- **Evidence:**
  - [חוק הגנת הצרכן, התשמ"א-1981](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הצרכן), Hebrew Wikisource text of Israel's Consumer Protection Law. Law (not legal advice). Section 17ד: a dealer may not publish or quote a price unless it is the total price, in shekels. Section 17א: the total price includes mandatory payments the consumer cannot practically waive. Section 14ג: in remote sales, the price that must be disclosed includes delivery charges. The law does not require every ad to show a price.
  - [Consumer Reactions to Drip Pricing](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2019.1207), Santana, Dallas and Morwitz, Marketing Science, 2020 (read in the [authors' PDF](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/26100/Santana%20Dallas%20Morwitz%20Marketing%20Science%20forthcoming.pdf)). Peer-reviewed (six studies). When surcharges were revealed later, buyers chose low-base, higher-total options, stuck with them after seeing the total, and were relatively dissatisfied. **[e-com]**
  - [The price does not include additional taxes, fees, and surcharges: A review of research on partitioned pricing](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2015.04.006), Greenleaf, Johnson, Morwitz and Shalev, Journal of Consumer Psychology, 2016 (online 2015). Peer-reviewed review. Consumers often do not fully process surcharges and underestimate totals, which changes what they buy; the authors flag it as a regulation concern. **[e-com]**
  - Counterpoint: [Price Salience and Product Choice](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2020.1261), Blake, Moshary, Sweeney and Tadelis, Marketing Science, 2021. Peer-reviewed (large field experiment on StubHub). Showing fees upfront reduced both the quantity and quality of purchases. **[e-com]**
  - Related, read only as a one-line abstract: [Lifting the Veil: The Benefits of Cost Transparency](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2019.1200), Mohan, Buell and John, Marketing Science, 2020, studies when disclosing a firm's costs can raise purchase interest. Not used as evidence.
  - No peer-reviewed study was found on showing a price versus hiding it ("price in DM") in social posts or Reels.
- **Strength:** strong for the legal rule (if shown, the total); contested for whether showing a price raises sales.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** open pricing is a gap in the category (5 of 173 Reels showed a price). Product facts: ₪119, home delivery ₪30, free pickup in Jerusalem. Because free pickup exists, ₪119 may count as the total, but say the delivery cost in the same breath to avoid a drip-pricing surprise; ask a lawyer before relying on "₪119" alone.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `price_on_screen`, `price_in_caption`; whether delivery or other fees appear in the same frame or line; "from" or "starting at" wording; price first appearing only inside a screen-recorded checkout (`screen_recording` = yes).

#### S4. Hands holding the product can carry the Reel; a face is optional

- **Rule:** A Reel where an adult's hands hold, open and point at the book (no face) is a first-choice format; add a face only when someone needs to talk.
- **Evidence:**
  - [The Power of Brand Selfies](https://doi.org/10.1177/00222437211037258), Hartmann, Heitmann, Schamp and Netzer, Journal of Marketing Research, 2021. Peer-reviewed (more than 250,000 brand image posts, 185 brands, Twitter and Instagram; plus display ads and a lab experiment). Selfies with faces got more likes and comments; "brand selfies" (an unseen person holding the product) got more brand engagement in the form of purchase intentions, and higher click-through in display ads. Self-reference explained the difference. **[photos, organic IG + ads]**
  - [Faces Engage Us](https://doi.org/10.1145/2556288.2557403), Bakhshi, Shamma and Gilbert, ACM CHI, 2014 (abstract via the DOI record; [Georgia Tech release](https://news.gatech.edu/news/2014/03/20/face-it-instagram-pictures-faces-are-more-popular), 20 Mar 2014). Peer-reviewed (about 1 million Instagram photos; the release says 1.1 million). Photos with faces were 38% more likely to get likes and 32% more likely to get comments; number, age and gender of faces had no effect, and photos of children were no more popular than adults'. **[photos, organic IG]**
  - [Is a Picture Worth a Thousand Words?](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022243719881113), Li and Xie, Journal of Marketing Research, 2020. Peer-reviewed (airline and SUV brand posts on Twitter and Instagram). A human face raised engagement on Twitter but not on Instagram. **[photos, organic IG]**
  - [Vicariously touching products through observing others' hand actions increases purchasing intention](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.23845), Liu, Zang, Chen, Assumpção and Li, Human Brain Mapping, 2018 (online 2017). Peer-reviewed (fMRI lab study with video clips). Seeing hands touch a product, versus not touching it, raised purchase intention. **[lab video]**
  - [Creative best practices for performance ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices), TikTok Business Help Center, updated June 2025. Platform. Feature people (creators, employees, customers) to capture attention. **[TikTok]**
- **Strength:** moderate. Faces lift likes in one large study and not in another; hands-holding-product images are linked to purchase intent in one large study. All on photos, none on Reels.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** approaches B and C and concepts 1 and 5 are well grounded; judge them by buying questions in comments and DMs, not likes.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `face_on_camera` = "no face (hands only)"; shots where a hand touches the product; ratio of buying questions to total comments (separate from likes).

#### S5. No identifiable child's face in LumeBook's own Reels; consent and no identifiers when a creator's child appears

- **Rule:** LumeBook's own Reels show no identifiable child's face; a creator Reel with a child has the parent's written consent, never combines the child's face with name, gan, school or location, and shows nothing embarrassing.
- **Evidence:**
  - [חשיפת ושיתוף מידע על ילדים ברשת על-ידי הוריהם (Sharenting)](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/sharenting_for_parents1/he/SharintingPost13Access.pdf), Israel Privacy Protection Authority, 25 Nov 2025 (updated after Amendment 13). Gov. Recommends hiding a child's face when posting publicly and widely, especially young children ("מומלץ כי בעת פרסום תמונות תמנעו מחשיפת פניו של ילדכם"), for example with blur or an emoji; prefer closed groups; when children appear in commercial marketing, take extra care to have the child's consent where age allows; reminds that section 2(6) of the Privacy Protection Law makes using a person's name, image or voice for profit without consent a privacy violation. Also names parents who use their children in online marketing as sharing for economic motives.
  - [Calcalist report on the Authority's first guidance](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/rjufahgryx), 18 Dec 2024. Practitioner (news). Earlier version of the same recommendations, including avoiding a young child's face in public posts.
  - [Expanding Teen Account Protections and Child Safety Features](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/07/expanding-teen-account-protections-child-safety-features/), Meta, 23 Jul 2025 (updated 23 Oct 2025). Platform. Adult-managed accounts that mainly feature children get the strictest message settings and Hidden Words, are not recommended to potentially suspicious adults, and such adults' comments are hidden. **[organic IG]**
  - [העסקת ילד או נער בהופעה או בפרסומת](https://protocol.co.il/child-work-show-commercial/), Protocol (legal information site), undated. Practitioner summary of the Youth Employment Law 1953 and its 1999 regulations: employing a child under 15 in advertising or advertising photography needs a time-limited Ministry of Labor permit, with conditions including a parent present. The page says nothing about influencers or parents filming their own children.
  - [Sharenting, parent blogging, and the boundaries of the digital self](https://doi.org/10.1080/15405702.2016.1223300), Blum-Ross and Livingstone, Popular Communication, 2017. Peer-reviewed (interviews with 17 parent bloggers). Parents face real ethical dilemmas: telling their own story exposes their children to risks they are responsible for preventing.
  - [#familygoals: Family Influencers, Calibrated Amateurism, and Justifying Young Digital Labor](https://doi.org/10.1177/2056305117707191), Abidin, Social Media + Society, 2017. Peer-reviewed (ethnographic content analysis). Some followers raise concern for children's well-being, though most responses to family influencers are supportive.
  - [Adolescents' Perspectives on Sharenting](https://library.iated.org/view/PELEG2024ADO), Peleg and Hadar (Yezreel Valley College), EDULEARN24 Proceedings, 2024. Peer-reviewed conference paper (qualitative, 31 Israeli Arab and Jewish adolescents). Ambivalence about parents' posts, some conflicts, a wish to be asked first; Arab adolescents more accepting.
  - [Bar-Ilan University: FoMO and sharenting](https://www.biu.ac.il/en/article/584384), Gazit, 22 Mar 2026. University news on research with 299 Israeli parents (journal not named). Oversharing was linked to fear of missing out and social comparison.
  - Bakhshi et al. 2014 (S4): photos of children were no more popular than photos of adults.
- **Strength:** strong as a legal and policy risk rule (official Israeli guidance, Israeli privacy law, Meta policy). No study was found on whether child-free product Reels sell less.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** supports the playbook's no-child design for Yarin. For barter creators (29 of 36 personalised-book Reels in the sample show a child), add written consent, no identifiers, and the option of a hidden face; any customer's child's photo or illustration used in LumeBook marketing needs the parents' written consent (section 2(6)); ask a lawyer whether the Youth Employment permit applies to barter content with under-15s.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `child_on_camera`; child's face visible and identifiable (yes/no); child's name, age, gan or town in video, caption or hashtags together with the face; embarrassing moments (bath, potty, crying).

#### S6. Let real people vouch: creators, customers and visible customer proof

- **Rule:** The Reel carries a real person's first-hand use or verdict (a parent or grandparent who owns the book), or visible real-customer proof (messages, reviews, counts), rather than brand adjectives.
- **Evidence:**
  - TikTok Marketing Science, 10 Jan 2022 (S1 link). Platform (about 2,800 ad inventory pieces, about 220 brand lift studies, UK, France, Germany, Spain, Italy, Oct 2020 to May 2021). Creator-made TikTok-style ads: 2-second view rate up 65%, 6-second view rate up 91%, engagement up 83%, against 12%, 8% and 12% for TikTok-style ads without creators. The post itself warns that platform-style ads without creators are not enough. Secondary coverage: [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/370205/tiktok-says-creators-drive-social-ad-campaign-succ.html), 12 Jan 2022. **[TikTok]**
  - [Reels ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads), Meta Business, undated (Hebrew page). Platform (meta-analysis of 15 A/B tests, May 2022 to Apr 2023, five countries). Adding a partner's Reels creative gave 5% lower cost per result and 11% higher conversion rate. **[IG/FB ads]**
  - [Social Media Brand Community and Consumer Behavior](https://doi.org/10.1287/isre.1120.0469), Goh, Heng and Lin, Information Systems Research, 2013. Peer-reviewed (Facebook brand page content matched to individual purchase records). User-generated content had a stronger effect on purchase spending than marketer-generated content. **[organic FB]**
  - [The Effect of Electronic Word of Mouth on Sales: A Meta-Analytic Review](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.14.0380), Babić Rosario, Sotgiu, De Valck and Bijmolt, Journal of Marketing Research, 2016 (online 2015). Peer-reviewed meta-analysis (1,532 effect sizes, 96 studies, 40 platforms). Word of mouth correlates positively with sales (average .091); on social media it works better when receivers can judge how similar they are to the sender; volume matters more than valence.
  - [Influencer Marketing: How Message Value and Credibility Affect Consumer Trust of Branded Content on Social Media](https://doi.org/10.1080/15252019.2018.1533501), Lou and Yuan, Journal of Interactive Advertising, 2019 (online 2018). Peer-reviewed (online survey of people who follow an influencer). Informative value, trustworthiness, attractiveness and similarity raised trust in branded posts, which raised awareness and purchase intention.
  - [Intention to Purchase through Parent Blogs: Evidence from Poland](https://doi.org/10.1080/15332861.2023.2234121), Canduela et al., Journal of Internet Commerce, 2023. Peer-reviewed (survey, n = 1,050 parenting-blog readers). For baby products, trust and reputation mattered more than parasocial influence; parasocial influence mattered more for readers under 23; longer readers trusted more.
  - [Mommy Influencers' Impact on the Purchase Intention of Followers in Türkiye](https://doi.org/10.30798/makuiibf.1588314), Paç Çelik and Kılıçer, 2026. Peer-reviewed (survey, 346 mothers of children 0 to 3 who follow a mom influencer). Trustworthiness, likability, similarity and argument quality shaped attitudes to the information, which led to purchase intention.
  - [How Online Reviews Influence Sales](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/how-online-reviews-influence-sales/), Spiegel Research Center, Northwestern University, report June 2017. Industry (data source not stated on the page). Purchase likelihood for a product with five reviews was 270% higher than with none; the lift was larger for higher-priced items; ratings of 4.0 to 4.7 converted best. **[e-com]**
  - [Global Trust in Advertising 2015](https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2015/global-trust-in-advertising-2015/), Nielsen, Sep 2015. Industry (method not on the page). 83% trust recommendations from friends and family, 66% trust consumer opinions posted online.
- **Strength:** moderate. Direction is consistent across platform data, purchase data, a meta-analysis and surveys; the platform numbers are self-reported paid-ad results, and the surveys measure intention, not buying.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** pick creators who look like the buyer (Israeli parents of 1 to 8 year olds, grandparents) over bigger reach; in LumeBook's own Reels, show real customer messages or photos (with consent) instead of adjectives like "אמיץ, חכם, מיוחד".
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `creator_type`; `relationship`; the speaker owns and uses the book (first-person past tense in the transcript); on-screen customer message or review screenshot; `selling_language` dominated by brand adjectives versus first-hand detail.

#### S7. Disclose the partnership in words, at the start

- **Rule:** A creator Reel says it is a partnership in words in the first caption line or on screen in the first seconds (for example "בשיתוף לומיבוק"), plus Instagram's paid-partnership label when money or goods changed hands.
- **Evidence:**
  - [חוק פרסום סמוי חל גם על אינסטגרם? מנדלבליט המליץ בחיוב](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/3413075), Walla, 21 Jan 2021. Practitioner (news) on the Attorney General's position in two class actions: the covert-advertising ban applies to Instagram; tags, hashtags or naming the brand are not enough; the post must say clearly at its start, without extra clicks, that it is paid.
  - Consumer Protection Law (S3 link), section 7. Law. An ad presented as editorial content without clearly saying it is an ad is treated as misleading.
  - [פרסום סמוי באינסטגרם: תביעה ב-30 מיליון ש'](https://www.bizportal.co.il/advertismentandmarketing/news/article/742517), Bizportal, 9 May 2018. Practitioner (news). A class action against seven advertisers over undisclosed Instagram influencer posts; shows the legal risk is live in Israel.
  - [A Meta-Analysis of the Effects of Disclosing Sponsored Content](https://doi.org/10.1080/00913367.2020.1765909), Eisend, van Reijmersdal, Boerman and Tarrahi, Journal of Advertising, 2020. Peer-reviewed meta-analysis (61 papers, 57 data sets). Disclosure lowered brand attitudes, credibility and source evaluation, and raised recognition, persuasion knowledge and resistance; timing and wording change the size of the effects.
  - ["This Post is Sponsored"](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intmar.2016.12.002), Boerman, Willemsen and Van Der Aa, Journal of Interactive Marketing, 2017. Peer-reviewed (2 x 2 experiment). A "Sponsored" label on a celebrity's Facebook post triggered ad recognition, distrust and lower intention to share; it had no such effect on a brand's own post.
  - [Credible Influencers](https://doi.org/10.1027/1864-1105/a000310), Johnson et al., Journal of Media Psychology, 2021 ([UF summary](https://jou.ufl.edu/2021/08/29/benjamin-johnson-cjc-alumna-and-students-author-article-on-social-media-influencer-credibility)). Peer-reviewed registered report (2 x 2 x 2 experiment, YouTube videos). Sponsorship disclosure changed only perceived sponsorship, not trust.
  - [Influencer Marketing on Instagram: Empirical Research on Social Media Engagement with Sponsored Posts](https://doi.org/10.1080/15252019.2022.2123724), Gross and von Wangenheim, Journal of Interactive Advertising, 2022. Peer-reviewed (64,438 sponsored Instagram posts). Users engaged more with sponsored posts than with the same influencers' non-sponsored posts, especially for micro-influencers. **[organic IG]**
- **Strength:** strong as an Israeli legal requirement; contested on the trust cost (experiments find a modest cost, field data finds sponsored posts still engage).
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** the sample showed loose disclosure (70 of 173 with words, 4 with the label). Put the disclosure words in the creator brief as the first caption line.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `disclosure_visible`; where it sits (first caption line, end of caption, on screen in 0 to 3 s, hashtag only); paid-partnership label present.

#### S8. Show the personalising and the child as the hero

- **Rule:** The Reel shows the act of personalising (one photo, the name typed, the free preview) and its payoff (the child's own name and likeness inside the story), so a parent can picture their own child.
- **Evidence:**
  - [The "I Designed It Myself" Effect in Mass Customization](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1090.1077), Franke, Schreier and Kaiser, Management Science, 2010 (online 2009). Peer-reviewed (five studies). Self-designed products earned higher willingness to pay beyond preference fit, through feelings of accomplishment and the person's sense of their own contribution. **[e-com]**
  - [The IKEA effect: When labor leads to love](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2011.08.002), Norton, Mochon and Ariely, Journal of Consumer Psychology, 2012. Peer-reviewed (four studies). Effort raised valuation only when the task was completed successfully.
  - Hartmann et al. 2021 (S4): a lab experiment showed self-reference drives the response to images of an unseen person holding a product.
  - [Personalised print books and family literacy outcomes: A literature review](https://cdn.literacytrust.org.uk/media/documents/2017-11-21_Personalised_books_and_family_literacy_outcomes_-_National_Literacy_Trust.pdf), Picton, National Literacy Trust, Oct 2017. Industry (literature review). Personalised books may help parents bridge the child's world and the book's; a 2014 study of 35 preschool children found they spoke more, and for longer, with personalised texts; the review calls the evidence small-scale.
- **Strength:** moderate that customising raises the customer's own valuation (lab); weak that watching someone else personalise does the same (untested).
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** concept 2 (the making-of) and a short shot of the free preview carry this; keep the ordering step brief and end on the printed page.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `screen_recording` = yes and shows upload or name entry; `likeness_proof` includes the name on a page; voice or text says the book was made for a named child.

### What is filmed

#### F1. Shoot vertical 9:16, full screen

- **Rule:** Filmed upright at 9:16 and filling the screen; no letterboxed landscape clips, squares or borders.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Instagram Reels video ad specs (Awareness)](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-reels), Meta Ads Guide, current page. Platform spec. 9:16 full-screen vertical; recommended 1440 x 2560. **[IG/FB ads]**
  - Meta Reels ads page (S6 link). Platform (meta-analysis of 15 Reels-only A/B tests). Campaigns using 9:16 video with sound and safe zones had 34.5% lower cost per result than image ads. **[IG/FB ads]**
  - [Unlock the Power of Reels Ads](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2024/11/07/unlock-the-power-of-reel-ads/), Meta for Developers, 7 Nov 2024. Platform. Build for vertical; campaigns following the creative essentials got 2x delivery to Reels (trend analysis of 4 million campaigns, Mar to May 2024). **[IG/FB ads]**
  - [Want to Make Better Video Ads for Mobile?](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/want-to-better-video-ads-for-mobile-well-show-you-how), Meta Business, no date shown. Platform. Phones are held upright 98% of the time; in tests with ten advertisers, vertical video raised brand lift for seven, up to 9 points of ad recall (footnote text not on the page). **[IG/FB ads]**
  - [Instagram Ranking Explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained), Adam Mosseri, Instagram, 31 May 2023. Platform. Reels with borders are made less visible. **[organic IG]**
  - TikTok best practices (S4 link) and Creative Codes (S1 link). Platform. Vertical 9:16, fill the screen. **[TikTok]**
- **Strength:** strong. All platforms agree and Instagram ties borders to lower visibility; the performance numbers are Meta paid-ad data.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** phone upright, native 9:16, no reused landscape footage.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** width and height from `media_info` (9:16); black bars or borders in any frame; letterboxed inserts.

#### F2. Keep text, faces and the product inside the safe zone

- **Rule:** Nothing important in the top 14%, the bottom 35% or the outer 6% of each side of the frame.
- **Evidence:**
  - Meta Ads Guide Reels specs (F1 link). Platform spec. Keep the top 14%, bottom 35% and 6% on each side free of text, logos and key elements so the profile icon, caption and buttons do not cover them. **[IG/FB ads]**
  - Meta for Developers, 7 Nov 2024 (F1 link). Platform. Put the hook and the call to action inside the safe zone. **[IG/FB ads]**
  - TikTok best practices (S4 link) and Creative Codes (S1 link). Platform. Keep key content in the UI safe zone; fill the screen without overlapping the UI. **[TikTok]**
  - Meta does not publish separate safe-zone percentages for organic Reels that I could find.
- **Strength:** strong as a platform spec for ads; organic overlap is similar in kind but its exact size is not published.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** the playbook's "subtitles in the lower third" (section 2A, section 3) and "nothing important in the bottom fifth" (section 6) sit inside Meta's 35% band. Move subtitles to just above the 35% line and keep hook text below the top 14%; check in the app before posting.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** position of text boxes, faces and the product against a 14 / 35 / 6 percent grid on the contact sheets; text or product cut by the caption or buttons.

#### F3. Original, sharp footage: no watermarks, borders, reposts or text-only slides

- **Rule:** Footage shot for this Reel, sharp (1080 x 1920 or better, never below 720p), no other app's watermark, not mostly text, not already posted on Instagram, under 3 minutes.
- **Evidence:**
  - Mosseri, 31 May 2023 (F1 link). Platform. Instagram aims to make Reels less visible when they are low-resolution, watermarked, muted, bordered, mostly text, or already posted on Instagram. **[organic IG]**
  - [Tips for Improving Your Reach](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/tips-for-improving-your-reach), Instagram Creators, 31 May 2023. Platform. Reposts, noticeable watermarks and accounts that collect others' content are less likely to be recommended; Reels must be 3 minutes or less to be recommended. **[organic IG]**
  - [Finding success on reels in 2025](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/the-latest-with-instagram), Instagram Creators, 21 Jan 2025. Platform. Post original content consistently; the Edits app exports without a watermark; Reels up to 3 minutes can be recommended. **[organic IG]**
  - [Instagram has a new penalty for unoriginal content aggregators](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator), Tubefilter, 30 Apr 2026. Practitioner (news; primary Instagram post not linked). The aggregation penalty was extended from Reels to photos and carousels; light edits like speed changes do not count as original.
  - TikTok best practices (S4 link): at least 720p. Creative Codes (S1 link): shoot hi-res, avoid pixelated visuals or grainy sounds. **[TikTok]**
- **Strength:** strong. Instagram states these rules directly.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** export from Edits or the native editor, no CapCut or TikTok watermark; share creator Reels as Collab posts, not reuploads.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** watermark visible; borders; resolution in `media_info`; share of the runtime that is text cards; the same clip already posted elsewhere on Instagram.

#### F4. Fill the frame with what matters, close enough to read on a phone

- **Rule:** In key shots the book page, the name or the speaker fills most of the frame, and any text on the product is readable at phone size; avoid wide shots where the book is small.
- **Evidence:**
  - Meta "Want to Make Better Video Ads for Mobile?" (F1 link). Platform. Keep the product front and center and highlight key elements within the small frame. **[IG/FB ads]**
  - Creative Codes (S1 link). Platform. Try to fill the screen. **[TikTok]**
  - [The Power of Creative Elements in TikTok ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/Power_Creative_Elements/pc/en), TikTok Creative Center, 28 Nov 2022. Platform (Kantar coding of about 4,000 ads from 2021 plus other studies). Close-up talking heads drive attention (no figure); but the same page reports, with no source, that a face filling under 20% of the screen gave 31% more consideration than one filling over 20%. **[TikTok]**
  - Teixeira et al. 2010 (S1): central brand placement raised avoidance, brand size did not. **[TV]**
- **Strength:** weak. Platform advice only; no controlled test of product framing size found, and the only number on face size is unsourced and points the other way.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** go close enough that the child's name on the page is readable on a phone; for talking to camera, chest-up framing is enough.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `shots[].framing` = close-up for product shots; share of shots where the product fills more than half the frame; whether on-page text is legible on the contact sheet at phone size.

#### F5. Hands touching the product, from the viewer's point of view

- **Rule:** Main product shots are POV or top-down, with a hand opening, turning or pointing at the book, the book facing the viewer the way they would hold it.
- **Evidence:**
  - Liu et al. 2018 (S4). Peer-reviewed (fMRI, first- and third-person clips). Seeing hands touch products raised purchase intention; the abstract reports perspective effects in brain areas but no clear winner between first and third person. **[lab video]**
  - [In search of a surrogate for touch: The effect of haptic imagery on perceived ownership](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2012.09.001), Peck, Barger and Webb, Journal of Consumer Psychology, 2013 (online 2012). Peer-reviewed. Imagining touching an object raised perceived ownership like real touch did; more vivid imagery, more ownership.
  - [The "Visual Depiction Effect" in Advertising](https://doi.org/10.1086/661531), Elder and Krishna, Journal of Consumer Research, 2012. Peer-reviewed (four studies). Orienting a product toward the viewer's dominant hand helped mental simulation and raised purchase intentions (reversed for negatively valued products). **[photos, ads]**
  - Hartmann et al. 2021 (S4): images of an unseen person holding the product drew purchase intentions, driven by self-reference. **[photos]**
  - [Tablets, touchscreens, and touchpads](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2013.10.003), Brasel and Gips, Journal of Consumer Psychology, 2014 (online 2013). Peer-reviewed (two lab studies). Touchscreens raised perceived ownership and valuation, more so for products where touch matters. Reels viewers are on touchscreens. **[e-com]**
- **Strength:** moderate. Several peer-reviewed lab studies agree on the mechanism; mostly stills or lab clips, none on Reels.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** film from behind the book, right hand turning pages, the book facing the lens as if the viewer holds it; this is concept 1 and approach C.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `shots[].framing` = POV or top-down; hand in contact with the product (yes/no); perspective first-person or third-person.

#### F6. When someone talks, they talk to the viewer

- **Rule:** Talking segments have the speaker facing the camera, eyes on the lens, speaking to "you".
- **Evidence:**
  - [Horton and Wohl Revisited: Exploring Viewers' Experience of Parasocial Interaction](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1460-2466.2011.01595.x), Hartmann and Goldhoorn, Journal of Communication, 2011. Peer-reviewed (2 x 2 experiment, N = 198). Being addressed by a performer bodily and verbally intensified parasocial experience, which raised enjoyment. **[TV]**
  - TikTok Creative Center, 28 Nov 2022 (F4 link). Platform. Creators or talent appear in 41% of about 4,000 coded ads, often addressing viewers directly. **[TikTok]**
  - Meta tailored campaigns (S1 link). Platform. Use people to explain products or share customer reviews. **[IG/FB ads]**
  - Johnson et al. 2021 (S7). Peer-reviewed. A vulnerable personal story unexpectedly lowered the influencer's trustworthiness.
  - Canduela et al. 2023 (S6). Peer-reviewed. For baby products, trust outweighed parasocial closeness.
- **Strength:** moderate for the mechanism (direct address builds a felt connection; tested on TV), weak for sales.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** concept 4 (Yarin with facts, to camera) fits; facts build trust better than confessions.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** `audio.speech` = direct-to-camera or both; gaze at the lens in talking shots; second-person address in the transcript ("אתם", "את", "אתה").

#### F7. Look homemade, but sharp, lit and steady

- **Rule:** Home setting, phone-shot, creator-style editing, with the subject in focus, evenly lit (window light), steady, and clean sound.
- **Evidence:**
  - Creative Codes (S1 link). Platform. "Go lo-fi (avoid glossiness)" sits beside "shoot hi-res" and "avoid pixelated visuals or grainy sounds". **[TikTok]**
  - TikTok best practices (S4 link). Platform. Use a DIY or less polished look that fits user content. **[TikTok]**
  - Abidin 2017 (S5). Peer-reviewed. "Calibrated amateurism": family influencers work to craft a raw, amateur look.
  - Li and Xie 2020 (S4). Peer-reviewed. High-quality, professionally shot pictures raised engagement on Instagram and Twitter. **[photos, organic IG]**
  - Johnson et al. 2021 (S7). Peer-reviewed. Production edits raised perceived expertise but not trust, and raised both when the video was unsponsored.
  - Meta Reels ads page (S6 link). Platform. Use the Reels communication style. **[IG/FB ads]**
  - No study was found on natural versus artificial light as such.
- **Strength:** contested. Platforms want a native, unglossy style; research finds technical quality helps; the two fit if "lo-fi" means style, not blur.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** window light, a tidy real home corner, phone braced or on a stand, no studio look; fix dim, blurry top-down shots (the weakness noted in Ddrev0jjauL).
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** setting (home, studio, store); focus and exposure on the contact sheets; shake or motion blur; studio lighting or heavy graphics.

#### F8. Use motion: live video of the product, not stills or text cards

- **Rule:** Most of the runtime is live video of the product moving (pages turning, hands, a person); stills, slideshows and text-only cards are brief.
- **Evidence:**
  - Roggeveen et al. 2015 (S1). Peer-reviewed. Video rather than still presentation raised preference and willingness to pay for hedonic options. **[e-com]**
  - Meta Reels ads page (S6 link). Platform. 9:16 video with sound had 34.5% lower cost per result than image ads. **[IG/FB ads]**
  - Mosseri, 31 May 2023 (F1 link). Platform. Mostly-text Reels are made less visible. **[organic IG]**
  - Creative Codes (S1 link). Platform. Movement catches the eye and fast scene changes draw users in early (Neuro-Insight study for TikTok, 2022). **[TikTok]**
- **Strength:** moderate.
- **Transfer to LumeBook:** show the digital preview by filming the phone being scrolled or a short screen recording, never a Reel built from still JPEGs.
- **How to spot it in a Reel:** share of runtime that is stills or text cards; motion in the first shot; `n_shots_estimate` and `editing_rhythm`.

## 3. Myths and contested claims

1. **"Content with faces gets 38% more engagement."** Traced to Bakhshi, Shamma and Gilbert (CHI 2014): Instagram photos, not videos; likes (38%) and comments (32%); about 1 million images. Li and Xie (JMR 2020) found no face effect on Instagram for brand posts, and Hartmann et al. (JMR 2021) show likes are not purchase intent. Fine as "faces attract likes on photos", wrong as "faces sell".
2. **"85% of Facebook video is watched without sound."** Traced to [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/), 17 May 2016: self-reports from two publishers (LittleThings, Mic), PopSugar at 50 to 80%, an agency at 85 to 90% for branded clients; no method. Meta's later pages say about 70% of mobile video watching is sound on (no date shown) and over 75% of Instagram Reels views are sound on (Nov 2024, no source given); TikTok says 93% (no source given). Captions still help muted viewers, but "most people watch muted" is outdated for Reels.
3. **"Shoppers are 64 to 85% more likely to buy after watching a product video."** The 64% traces to a [Ragan](https://www.ragan.com/consumers-are-64-more-likely-to-buy-after-watching-a-video/) article (18 Feb 2014) citing an infographic by a video production company, with no study named. The 85% is attributed online to Internet Retailer; the [Digital Commerce 360 article](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/?p=11139) (27 Mar 2014) is behind a registration wall and could not be checked. Even if real, these compare people who chose to watch with people who did not (self-selection). Do not cite.
4. **"92% of consumers trust recommendations from people they know."** Real but narrow: [Nielsen](https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2012/consumer-trust-in-online-social-and-mobile-advertising-grows/), Apr 2012, online survey of 28,000+ internet users in 56 countries, about friends and family, not influencers, self-reported. Nielsen's 2015 figure was 83%. Not evidence that creator content is trusted.
5. **"Partnership ads get 53% higher CTR and 19% lower CPA."** Meta's own [Advantage+ catalog ads page](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/meta-advantage-plus/catalog-ads) states a 19% lower cost per action when partnership ads are combined with catalog ads, but the footnote text is not visible. Secondary sources quote the CTR lift as 53% or as 13 to 15%. No Meta method document could be opened. Treat as a Meta sales claim for paid ads.
6. **"Lo-fi ads beat polished ads."** Contested (F7). A "33% stronger consideration for lo-fi" figure credited to a TikTok playbook appeared in a search summary but was not in the [TikTok playbook](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf) text that could be extracted (most of it is images). Untraced.
7. **"You have 3 seconds."** Windows differ: Meta says key message in 3 seconds; TikTok says 90% of ad recall impact comes within 6 seconds (2020) and the first 2 to 2.5 seconds matter most for recall and awareness (2022). All are paid-ad recall measures from the platforms themselves; none measures organic Reel sales.
8. **"Get the face close."** TikTok's Creative Center page both says close-up talking heads drive attention and reports, without a source, that faces filling under 20% of the screen drove 31% more consideration. Unsourced and contradictory; no peer-reviewed test found.
9. **"Kids in the video boost engagement."** Bakhshi et al. found photos of children were no more popular than photos of adults. No Reels or video study found.
10. **"Disclosing sponsorship kills performance."** Contested. The 61-paper meta-analysis finds modest drops in credibility and brand attitude; a 64,438-post Instagram study finds sponsored posts get more engagement than the same creators' other posts; one experiment finds disclosure changes only perceived sponsorship. In Israel, disclosure is required regardless.
11. **"Showing the full price upfront always helps."** Untested for social video. Drip pricing leaves buyers dissatisfied, but upfront fees cut purchases in a StubHub field experiment. Israeli law makes "total price if a price is shown" the floor.
