## Method

**Collection date:** 09 Nov 2026, from Yarin's signed-in Instagram session in the Claude desktop app's built-in browser (read only: no follows, likes, comments, DMs or form submissions).

**Inventory.** The Reels tab's own query returned 2,050 items over 171 pages: 1,553 Reels (`product_type` clips, 25 Feb 2021 to 08 Nov 2026) and 497 older feed and IGTV videos (01 Nov 2018 to 2022). The profile header said 1,984 posts; the Reels tab holds more than the grid, so this is the full Reels history the tab exposes, not a sample of the first screen. Items were deduplicated by media id. Feed and IGTV videos carry a different count (`view_count`, at most 32,753) and are kept apart; none would have entered the top 100 (the 100th Reel has 122,531 views).

**The metric.** "Views" here is the number the Reels grid shows under each tile. It equals the API field `play_count` (checked on 09 Nov 2026: the pinned Reel DfCiXqEoqWg shows "104K" on the grid; `play_count` 104,438, Instagram-only `ig_play_count` 103,563). For Reels cross-posted to Facebook it includes Facebook plays (DNWIV26oabB: 1,157,090 = 871,616 on Instagram + 285,474 on Facebook). It is not reach and not unique viewers. Likes and comments are Instagram-side counts, so the interaction ratios below divide them by `ig_play_count`. Shares are the number under the paper-plane icon on the Reel page (DNWIV26oabB's page showed 1,973, the API's `reshare_count`); Reels posted before Nov 2024 show 0 to 5 shares even at millions of views, so shares are treated as not comparable before 01 Nov 2024 (an inference from the data, not documented). Saves are not public and are marked unavailable. Ratios are "visible interaction ratios", not engagement rates: the denominator mixes followers and non-followers, repeat plays and Facebook plays, and Instagram's view counting has changed over the years.

**Top 100.** The 100 Reels with the highest views among the 1,553 (lowest: 122,531). Distribution: 2 from 2022, 15 from 2023, 32 from 2024, 32 from 2025, 19 from 2026. 15 are collaborations (8 owned by the other account, with @matan.nistor as co-author). Pinned on 09 Nov 2026: Dd9X6TeIjld (#73) on both the Reels tab and the profile grid, DGqhYmrIjVv (#47, a collab) on the profile grid; the other two pinned Reels (DdJR5sOIbNx, DfCiXqEoqWg) rank 116 and 139. Pinning keeps a Reel in view and can add views. 57 of the 100 were cross-posted to Facebook (Facebook plays inside the count). None carries Instagram's paid-partnership label. Promotion is not public: a Reel without a label can still have been boosted, and some top Reels have unusually low like ratios (C_tCtPvoT3x: 2.4 likes per 1,000 Instagram views, against a 2024 to 2026 account median of 9.9), which fits reach outside the follower base, paid or not.

**Comparisons.** 20 lower performers chosen before watching: Reels he owns, no collab, posted in the same or neighbouring month as a top Reel, with views at or below that month's 25th percentile (pool of 129), paired by caption topic and similar length. After watching, the coders rated each pair's match (topic, format, duration within 30%). Their median is 0.62 times the median of their month, against 4.7 times for the top 100.

**Account-wide check.** Full metadata (duration, caption, audio type, Facebook plays, shares) for every Reel posted since 01 Jan 2024 (764) made it possible to test some patterns over 753 Reels at least 14 days old, using views divided by the median of the Reels posted the same month (`src/analyze_account.py`). That controls roughly for the account's growth; it does not control for topic demand, promotion, collaborations or the follower count on the day.

**Evidence per Reel.** All 120 videos were downloaded with audio (116 with yt-dlp signed out; 4 that Instagram hides from signed-out viewers through the link the signed-in page plays). For each Reel: 20 frames over the first 5 seconds (4 per second), one frame per second for the whole Reel (1.5 s steps above 90 s), ffmpeg scene cuts (threshold 0.28), loudness per half second, the cover image, and a local Hebrew Whisper transcript (turbo model, first 120 s). "Watched" in this report means those frames and that transcript were read: nobody listened to the audio. Music is known only where Instagram attributes a licensed song or the transcript prints lyrics; sound effects were not determinable.

**Comments.** For each of the 120 Reels the first three pages of top-level comments (up to about 42, 4,184 in all) in the order Instagram's web endpoint returns them to a signed-in viewer (not sorted by likes or date), plus replies under up to three threads (405 replies, 220 by @matan.nistor). Commenter names were not kept. A few dozen comments per Reel do not represent the audience; they show what kind of reply a Reel draws.

**Coding.** Rubric v0 was piloted by hand on five varied Reels (`src/PILOT.md`) and refined to v1 (`src/CODING_BRIEF.md`): caption-based guesses were wrong for 2 of 5, so topic and format are coded from the video; fields were added for the first sentence, numbers in the first 3 seconds, b-roll source, end cards, counters and rights. Six Sonnet coders coded 20 Reels each, every coder with a mix of top and comparison Reels so that coder and group are not confounded. Reels coded before their transcript existed were coded from the burned-in captions and then checked against the transcript in a text-only pass (`src/TRANSCRIPT_CHECK.md`, log in `src/transcript_check_log.jsonl`). I checked a sample of coded records against the frames myself (listed in `coverage.md`).

## Limits

- Correlation only. The top 100 differ from the rest in topic, period, collaborations, pinning and possibly paid reach, not only in craft. Every pattern below is a lead to test, not a cause.
- No retention, completion, watch time, rewatch, reach, follower gain, saves, DMs, link clicks or sales: none is public. Where the report talks about retention it names a possible mechanism, never a measured effect.
- The follower count at the time of each post is unknown; only today's (205K) is.
- The comparison set is small (20) and was matched on captions before watching; read pair results as examples.
- Views include Facebook plays for cross-posted Reels and replays; very short Reels (3 to 12 s) may collect views from automatic replay.
- Audio was not heard. Transcripts mishear Hebrew words; quotes on screen were read from frames, which sample one frame per second after the first 5 seconds.
- Several top Reels use third-party film and TV clips or show identifiable bystanders; those executions are described, not recommended.
