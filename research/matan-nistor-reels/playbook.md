# Creative playbook: ten Reel structures from @matan.nistor

Ten structures that recur in his top 100 (read 09 / 11 / 2026), written so they can be filmed by someone else. Each says what it is, how to build it beat by beat, what it is for, when it fits and when it may fail, how strong the evidence is, and how LumeBook could use it. The evidence is correlation in one creator's account (`report.md`); a structure that worked for a provocative business coach can fail for a children's book brand. Copy the mechanism, never his wording, persona, money stunts or third-party footage.

Evidence labels: **stronger** = a pattern in the top-vs-comparison data or the 753-Reel account check; **medium** = a group of several top Reels with a consistent device; **thin** = one or two Reels.

---

## 1. Rising score: repeated encounters with a counter

**What it is.** The same small test repeated with different people, each with a quick yes or no, while a number on screen keeps climbing.

**His examples.** [DYJdtg5ovNb](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DYJdtg5ovNb/) (8.2M; counter "סך כסף שחולק" from 0:08 to 1:18), [C8j_5daiAdv](https://www.instagram.com/reel/C8j_5daiAdv/) (3.3M; the prize doubles, "X2" at 0:15), [C94DFKMon1V](https://www.instagram.com/reel/C94DFKMon1V/) (1.9M; one payment per platform followed).

**Beats.** 0 to 1 s: the stake in one picture (cash at 0:00.25). 1 to 3 s: the rule in one sentence. Then 6 to 12 encounters of 4 to 8 s each, every one ending on a result; the counter changes on each result; one failure in the middle (a red X at 0:43) for variety; the last encounter is the biggest; close on the ask to camera.

**For.** Reach. **Fits** a challenge that can be judged in seconds. **May fail** when the stake is small or the test is slow; it also hands the viewer's attention to the prize, not the brand (cash Reels: 4.1 likes per 1,000 views, comments are giveaway entries).

**Evidence.** Medium (3 Reels, the biggest numbers in the account; a giveaway is not craft).

**For LumeBook.** No cash and no prize promotion. The mechanism that transfers is the visible running score: "how many grandmothers recognise the grandchild from the cover alone" counted on screen.

## 2. Narrated montage: one voice, a new picture for every line

**What it is.** A single voiceover argument while the footage changes every 1 to 2 seconds, each shot acting out the line being said.

**His examples.** [DCgV2ujI0cw](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DCgV2ujI0cw/) (2.0M; a command per line, film clip per command from 0:01), [DNWIV26oabB](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DNWIV26oabB/) (1.2M; a real queue, then the fast-track lane from 0:07), [C5DTQqpom4f](https://www.instagram.com/reel/C5DTQqpom4f/) (760K; one clip of his own life per line from 0:07), [DKwNYG7IRlN](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DKwNYG7IRlN/) (703K; a book passage over film clips).

**Beats.** 0 s: the claim or the question on the first frame. Then one sentence per shot, picture matching the noun in the sentence; captions as phrases, the key word coloured; 20 to 60 s; end on the line that sums it up, with no new information after it.

**For.** Reach and shares. **Fits** a short argument made of concrete steps. **May fail** when the footage is generic stock or does not match the words (his comparisons with stock b-roll sit low), and when it relies on film clips you have no right to use.

**Evidence.** Medium (6 top Reels at a median 13.5 times their month; none among the comparisons).

**For LumeBook.** Use only footage LumeBook owns: the photo, the preview screen, the printed pages, the dedication, the box (brief 2).

## 3. The number reveal: ask, wait, show

**What it is.** A question whose answer is a single number or image, held back for a few seconds and then shown big.

**His examples.** [C0ZSOdsth3G](https://www.instagram.com/reel/C0ZSOdsth3G/) (782K; "כמה אתם משלמים פה שכירות?" then "22,500₪" at 0:05), [DaQeQSlIS8b](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DaQeQSlIS8b/) (395K; the passenger's reaction at 0:11, the fare at 0:12), [C3SRBGrolrY](https://www.instagram.com/reel/C3SRBGrolrY/) (248K; "guess where I flew", never answered in the video; the spoken ask at 0:18 offers 1,000 shekels to one right guess, which explains part of its 5.5 comments per 1,000 views).

**Beats.** 0 to 2 s: the question in words and on screen. 2 to 10 s: build the expectation (the place, a face). The reveal as the only overlay, large. Then 3 to 10 s of reaction or context.

**For.** Reach and comments. **Fits** anything with a surprising concrete answer. **May fail** when the answer is not surprising, or when the shown proof contradicts the headline ([C9cogjXo7ow](https://www.instagram.com/reel/C9cogjXo7ow/): "23,000₪" headline, a receipt for 5,900 at 0:09).

**Evidence.** Medium.

**For LumeBook.** The reveal is never a price (reels don't mention it). It can be a page, a name, a likeness, or a number that is a fact (24 pages, 10 pictures inside).

## 4. A small act and the reaction

**What it is.** A POV where the creator does one kind or surprising thing for someone and the camera stays for their reaction.

**His examples.** [DYzLsUgoosB](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DYzLsUgoosB/) (434K; coffee for window cleaners on a platform, 35.8 likes per 1,000 views, the highest in the top 100), [C94DFKMon1V](https://www.instagram.com/reel/C94DFKMon1V/) (the cash as the act).

**Beats.** 0 to 1 s: the unusual situation (the platform at 0:00.75) under a "Pov:" headline. The offer in captions word by word. The preparation in quick shots (the kitchen, 0:18 to 0:41). The hand-off, then the reaction, held (1:02 to 1:18).

**For.** Likes and warmth. **Fits** a gift moment. **May fail** when the act looks staged or the person on camera has not agreed to it.

**Evidence.** Thin (1 strong example), but the highest like rate in the set.

**For LumeBook.** A grandmother giving the book is this structure (brief 1); the reaction is the payoff and must be real.

## 5. A real disagreement between two people

**What it is.** Two people who disagree about a decision, filmed as it happens, ending with one of them conceding or a reveal that settles it.

**His examples.** [DZw0OnMu-rQ](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DZw0OnMu-rQ/) (650K, 224 s; a hotel upgrade argued at reception from about 1:47, settled with the suite 3:08 to 3:42), [DaNwAg0KME0](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DaNwAg0KME0/) (133K, 140 s; packing, from "יש לך פה שני ג'ינסים" at 0:20 to the peak at 1:48 to 2:00), [C0ZSOdsth3G](https://www.instagram.com/reel/C0ZSOdsth3G/) and [C-kyvekNWBI](https://www.instagram.com/reel/C-kyvekNWBI/) (home tours with his partner).

**Beats.** The disagreement stated in the first 5 s. Each side gets a line, captions coloured per speaker. Escalate with concrete objects (the room, the jeans). Resolve on screen. Long Reels work only if each 10 to 15 s adds a new step.

**For.** Comments (people take sides) and watch time. **Fits** two real people with a real stake. **May fail** when comments turn on the people instead of the question (his couple Reels draw mocking comments).

**Evidence.** Medium (4 to 5 top Reels).

**For LumeBook.** Two grandparents who disagree about the gift, if real; never scripted conflict over children.

## 6. Answer a real question, in one take

**What it is.** A question someone actually sent (a story sticker, a comment) pinned on screen, answered to camera in one unbroken take.

**His examples.** [DeB4C26RjFU](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeB4C26RjFU/) (167K, 2,776 shares; the sticker from 0:00, one take for 35 s), [DGqCUZHogdZ](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DGqCUZHogdZ/) (240K, 4,844 shares, 12 s), [DI84dYIo_nH](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DI84dYIo_nH/) (comparison, 50K; viewers' story replies as the prompt).

**Beats.** 0 s: the question sticker, readable. The answer's first sentence within 1 s. No cuts or few. One quotable line in the middle. Stop when the answer ends.

**For.** Shares. **Fits** questions that many people have. **May fail** without captions (DeB4C26RjFU has none, so it needs sound), and when the answer insults part of the audience (its top comments correct him).

**Evidence.** Medium (the top share rates in the set).

**For LumeBook.** Only real questions from DMs or comments, quoted exactly; no invented ones.

## 7. The claim, then your own proof

**What it is.** A specific claim in the first sentence, followed within a second by footage that proves it, then the reasoning.

**His examples.** [C_tCtPvoT3x](https://www.instagram.com/reel/C_tCtPvoT3x/) (1.5M; "100,000₪ בחודש" and a helicopter by 0:01.25) against its pair [C-VS6pZxd9p](https://www.instagram.com/reel/C-VS6pZxd9p/) (51K; the same claim, opened mid-thought, stock footage at 0:16), [DNbJwFGose6](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DNbJwFGose6/) (261K; "משכיר" to "למיליונר" photos at 0:00.75).

**Beats.** 0 to 1 s: the claim as a complete sentence. 1 to 2 s: the first proof shot. Reasons with a proof shot each. Close on the rule in one line.

**For.** Credibility and reach. **Fits** a claim you can show. **May fail** when the proof is stock, or when the claim needs the viewer to already know the context.

**Evidence.** Stronger for the opening (72 of 100 top Reels open with a self-contained sentence, 8 of 20 comparisons), medium for the proof footage.

**For LumeBook.** Claims about the product that can be shown in the next shot (the name on the cover, the picture inside, the handwritten dedication).

## 8. Earn the keyword with a story

**What it is.** A short story with a stake, and only at the end one word to comment for the full thing.

**His examples.** [DW6J5jPEee2](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DW6J5jPEee2/) (172K; a cancelled flight at 0:01, ₪43,642 at 0:03.3, "יפן" at 0:36) against [DXwPDBDI5yp](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DXwPDBDI5yp/) (26K; a food montage with no stake, "אוכל" at 0:13.5), [DUvqlKuCKTl](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DUvqlKuCKTl/) (142K, 4,119 comments; the club is closing, "חדש" at 0:55).

**Beats.** The news or the problem in the first second. A stake with a number or a date. Two or three beats of what happened. The keyword alone on screen for the last 2 to 3 s, also in the caption, nothing after it.

**For.** Leads (DMs), not reach: across 124 of his keyword Reels since 2024, 2.0 comments per 1,000 views against 0.58, and 1.6% in his top tenth against 12.0%. **Fits** content made to start a conversation that ends in a link. **May fail** when used on a Reel meant to travel, or judged by comment count.

**Evidence.** Stronger (account-wide).

**For LumeBook.** This is the לומי Reel. Judge it by DMs and books started from the link (`reels.py link`), not by comments (brief 4).

## 9. The one-look card

**What it is.** A single still text card readable in one look: a list or a "swap this for that", on screen from the first frame, 3 to 15 seconds.

**His examples.** [C8CDQJvI18j](https://www.instagram.com/reel/C8CDQJvI18j/) (1.2M, 6.5 s, "תחליף את זה בעד זה"), [DANj05nITho](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DANj05nITho/) (128K, 3 s, the caption asks viewers to add to the list), [C_xChcHIs9-](https://www.instagram.com/reel/C_xChcHIs9-/) (197K, 3 s).

**Beats.** Everything on the first frame. 3 to 6 short lines. One line that someone will want to argue with or add to. Caption carries the question.

**For.** Reach and comments for almost no production. **Fits** a list people agree or disagree with. **May fail** as a one-off: five of his six cards sit at 2.2 to 3.6 times their month, only one at 27 times; views on very short Reels may include automatic replays.

**Evidence.** Thin to medium (account-wide, Reels under 15 s sit at 1.74 times their month, 51 Reels).

**For LumeBook.** A 10-second gift card for grandparents (brief 5), kept inside the 10 to 30 s reel rule.

## 10. The labelled scene

**What it is.** A familiar scene (a slap, a phone call, a parent and child) where on-screen labels turn each moment into commentary.

**His examples.** [DFsVHkjohR4](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DFsVHkjohR4/) (137K; each slap labelled with a choice he mocks, "*נרשמה לתואר במנהל עסקים*" at 0:13), [DLUSKkbITlt](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DLUSKkbITlt/) (215K; "אי שם בשנת 2050", a son asks what a lawyer is, the "נכחד" stamps from 0:59), [Cu3tIv3Iz79](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Cu3tIv3Iz79/) (185K; a call to a college with an "מד רגשות" label from 0:00.25).

**Beats.** The premise as a label on the first frame. The scene plays; every beat gets a short label. The last label is the punchline.

**For.** Shares and comments through humour (34 of the top 100 use humour, 1 of 20 comparisons). **Fits** a light point most viewers already half agree with. **May fail** when the humour mocks the people you want to sell to, or the footage is someone else's (DFsVHkjohR4 uses a TV scene).

**Evidence.** Medium.

**For LumeBook.** Own footage and gentle labels only: for example the "מד" label idea on a grandparent's face as she reads the dedication.

---

## Across all ten

- Something happens or is shown in the first second; the first sentence makes sense alone or is a deliberate cliffhanger.
- Edits carry information (a counter, a number, a proof shot); more cuts and more effects did not separate his hits (the comparisons cut faster and used more effects).
- Burned-in text on nearly every Reel (98 of 100), phrase captions as the default, word-by-word with speaker colours when two people talk.
- Keep reach Reels and keyword Reels apart, and measure them differently.
