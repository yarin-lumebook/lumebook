**What carries it (interpretation):**
- The crash and the red "אתה מת." card (rows 1 and 2) give a shock plus a cover in under 3 s, with a mid-sentence "ואז -" that asks the viewer to continue.
- The fixed TV frame and a new clip about every 1 to 3 s (rows 3 to 9) mean the eye always has new information while the voice stays on one idea.
- Red for dark words and lime for hopeful words turn the text into a second track of emotion.
- Shares are high (about 2.7 per 1,000 views): the lines are quotable and universal.

**Friction (interpretation):**
- The black screen at 1.7 to 3 s may lose viewers who stayed for the crash.
- The dark subject divides people; see the comments below.
- Nothing on screen says the text comes from a book.

**Engagement and next step (observed):** There is no spoken or on-screen prompt. The only pointer is in the caption: the passage is from the last part of "לשרוף את הספינות" and the audiobook is free on his YouTube channel (named, no link). A commenter asks where the quote is from, so the source was not clear from the video. In the 45-comment sample (not representative) most are praise ("שמת לי סטירה לפנים"); a few criticise: the crash upsets motorcyclists, the last question sounds suicidal to one person, and one comment (18 likes, the most liked in the sample) says constant thoughts of death mean being broken. The creator replied 3 times in the sample, with emojis and one answer, "מהספר שלי". Comments are low (0.28 per 1,000 views) and there are no keyword entries.

**For LumeBook:** The principle that transfers is a read-aloud excerpt of your own text (a book page, a story line) cut with one image per phrase, coloured emphasis words and a hook card in the first 3 s. Do not copy: the third-party crash clip and about 40 film or TV clips (use only owned or licensed footage, such as the book's own illustrations), the dark mortality theme, or the missing pointer; for a children's book, put the book's name and where to find it on screen, not only in the caption.
