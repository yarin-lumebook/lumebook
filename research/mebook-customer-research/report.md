Researched 14 / 11 / 2026. Language of customers: Hebrew. Report in English. Translations are mine and marked as such.

**Read this first.** This is evidence about people who bought from or reacted to MeBook. It is not proof about LumeBook customers. Every hypothesis at the end needs testing with our own buyers.

## A. Research summary

**Strongest findings**

1. **The payoff customers describe is the child recognising themself.** 12 statements describe the child finding, pointing at or asking about themself in the book ("זה באמת אני? מתי הייתי שם?"). This is the most repeated post-purchase story in the sample, and it matches what MeBook's own ads lead with (brand claim, Ad Library ids 2159538358320935, 1403700474485399).
2. **Many buyers are not the parent.** 10 grandparent statements and 9 aunt, uncle, sibling or friend statements, plus 5 about kindergarten group orders. Grandparents mention the making itself as part of the gift ("עצם הכנת הספר הייתה עבורי, כסבתא, חוויה מהנה"). Counter-evidence: a creator reel of a grandma reading to her grandson drew 71 Facebook comments and none about the book.
3. **Value is proven by repeated reading, not by the unboxing.** 10 statements describe nightly or repeated reading weeks later ("שבוע וחצי כבר כל לילה", "לא מוכן ללכת לישון לפני שמקריאים לו"). This is the clearest answer in the sample to the one explicit "is it a gimmick?" doubt.
4. **Likeness is the quality bar, and it sometimes fails.** 6 buyers praise how alike the character looks, 3 buyers report it did not look like the child or got the gender wrong (the only 1-star review). Observers in comments also notice ("הצ׳ט עשה אותך רזה").
5. **Pre-purchase questions are about price, timing and how it works.** Social comments ask price (4), delivery time (3), how to order, a link or how to reach them (6), whether parents must write the story (1), whether any story can be chosen (1), whether one book can hold twins (2, one of whom tried and failed) and whether there is a potty-training book (1). One parent asks in a book group whether it is worth the money or "סתם גימיקי".
6. **Gift occasions:** birthdays (5), transitions such as a new sibling, potty training, first grade (5 buyer and prospect statements, plus 4 sponsored creator captions), end of the kindergarten year (3).

**What remains unknown**

- What alternatives buyers weighed. Apart from the gimmick question and the brand's own comparisons (toys, clothes, shelf books, other personalised-book sites), the sample says almost nothing.
- Why buyers did not order. No abandoned-cart or non-buyer voices; the sample is people who bought and liked it, or friends of creators.
- Real complaint rate. Public negative reviews are rare (1 of 67 on Google), but MeBook itself posted on 16 / 10 / 2026 that it "received quite a few complaints from customers who confused MeBook with other sites" about soft covers and booklets (brand claim, https://www.instagram.com/p/DejyWEUgavW/). That says the category has a quality-disappointment problem the reviews do not show.
- Price sensitivity. Questions ask the price, but nobody in the sample says what price would stop them.
- Who buys by gender or age. Reviewer names suggest mostly women, but names do not establish this.

## E. Six content hypotheses for LumeBook

Nothing below claims a LumeBook feature as fact. Where a hypothesis leans on a LumeBook feature, I name the source and what must be rechecked. Our product brief of 01 / 10 / 2026 (`docs/lumebook-product-brief.md`) records a free preview before payment, a digital copy by email, and a **22×22 cm soft cover** with back pages that recommend other books or offers. Recheck the live product before using any of it.

### H1. Gift buyers outside the parents
"We hypothesize that **grandparents, aunts and uncles** will be more likely to buy if we show **a relative giving the book and the child finding themself in it, with the relative's own words**, because **19 of the statements in this sample come from non-parent buyers, and they describe the gift as strengthening their own bond with the child**."
- Evidence: #4, #5, #7 to #13, #46; 10 grandparent and 9 aunt, uncle, sibling statements. Against: the grandma reel on Facebook, 71 comments and none about the book.
- Hook (Hebrew, for the audience): "סבתא הכינה לו ספר. הוא מצא את עצמו בעמוד הראשון" (gloss: "Grandma made him a book. He found himself on page one").
- Footage or proof needed: a real grandparent or aunt filmed giving a LumeBook book (consent from the family), the child's first look, the relative's unscripted line. No actors presented as customers.
- Test: two Reels with the same book and CTA, one framed for parents and one for grandparents and aunts; compare preview starts and orders by UTM (utm_content per version). Optionally a paid split by age band.
- Assumption to validate: that a meaningful share of LumeBook buyers are not parents, and that a relative at the centre of a video brings orders, not only laughs. Our sales data does not say who the buyer is, and MeBook's grandma reel drew comments with no talk of the book.

### H2. The recognition moment
"We hypothesize that **parents of 2 to 7 year olds** will be more likely to buy if we show **the unedited moment a child recognises themself in the book, next to the photo it was made from**, because **12 statements describe that moment as the main payoff, and likeness is the quality bar buyers judge (6 for, 3 against)**."
- Evidence: #1, #2, #4, #8, #21, #33; contradicting: #22 to #24.
- Hook: "שאל אותי: זה באמת אני?" or open on the child's face, no text for the first second.
- Footage or proof needed: real first-open reactions from LumeBook customers, the source photo beside the illustrated page (with permission), no retouching of the book pages.
- Test: Reel A (reaction first) vs Reel B (product walkthrough first), same caption and CTA; metric: preview starts per 1,000 reach, then orders.
- Assumption to validate: that LumeBook's likeness holds up on camera across ages and skin tones. A weak match filmed close up would work against us, as #22 shows.

### H3. "Every night" as the answer to "gimmick"
"We hypothesize that **price-hesitant parents** will be more likely to buy if we show **the same child asking for the book again on night 1, night 7 and night 20**, because **10 statements describe repeated nightly reading, and the one explicit doubt in the sample is whether the book is a gimmick that costs too much**."
- Evidence: #10, #11, #14 to #17, #20, #26, #37.
- Hook: "עשרים ערבים. אותו ספר" (gloss: "Twenty evenings. The same book").
- Footage or proof needed: a family filming short clips over three weeks, or written diaries from 3 to 5 real customers, with dates.
- Test: carousel or Reel built on the repeat-reading story vs our current best carousel format; metric: saves, preview starts, orders by UTM.
- Assumption to validate: that LumeBook books get re-read. Ask in the customer questions (F5) before filming.

### H4. See it before you pay
"We hypothesize that **first-time visitors who worry about likeness or mistakes** will be more likely to buy if we show **a screen recording of making the preview and fixing a page before paying**, because **buyers here report wrong gender, weak likeness, upload trouble and editing confusion (8 statements), and prospects ask whether they must write the story themselves**."
- Evidence: #22 to #25, #31, #32, #36, #42.
- Hook: "לפני שמשלמים, רואים את כל הספר" (only if true on the live site).
- Footage or proof needed: a real screen recording on the live LumeBook site, start to preview, with a real child's photo used with permission.
- Test: add a 10 second preview recording to an existing Reel vs the same Reel without it; metric: preview completion rate, then orders.
- Assumption to validate: that the free preview and "pay only if you love it" still exist (brief of 01 / 10 / 2026, and seen in a LumeBook Google ad on 14 / 11 / 2026), and that the preview matches the printed book. MeBook reviewer #25 says the print did not match the edit.

### H5. Answer price, timing and "how" inside the content
"We hypothesize that **people commenting on our posts** will be more likely to order if **each post answers price, delivery time and the 3 steps in the caption or a pinned comment**, because **15 MeBook comments in this sample ask exactly these questions, and several get no visible answer**."
- Evidence: #28 to #32, #44, plus 5 "how do I order / link" comments.
- Hook: not a hook; a pinned comment and a last slide ("כמה זה עולה, מתי זה מגיע, איך עושים").
- Footage or proof needed: current price, real delivery window, and the steps, confirmed with the store owner before publishing. Reels never mention price (our rule since 05 / 11 / 2026), so for Reels the answer goes in the pinned comment or caption only if Yarin agrees; otherwise test this on carousels.
- Test: alternate posts with and without the pinned answer for two weeks; metric: comment questions, link clicks, orders by UTM.
- Assumption to validate: that unanswered questions lose LumeBook orders, rather than people clicking through anyway.

### H6. Transition stories as preparation
"We hypothesize that **parents facing a new sibling, potty training or starting kindergarten or first grade** will be more likely to buy if we show **the book used to prepare the child before the change**, because **buyers here describe using the book as preparation (4 statements, plus 4 sponsored creator posts and a formula-brand partnership MeBook ran for new siblings)**."
- Evidence: #7, #8, #13, #14; Ad Library id 2137767587097061 (brand partnership, brand claim).
- Hook: "הוא עוד לא יודע שיהיה לו אח. הספר כבר יודע" (gloss: "He doesn't know he's getting a brother yet. The book does").
- Footage or proof needed: a real family using a LumeBook story before a transition. First check whether LumeBook has a transition title for that moment.
- Test: one transition story vs one generic adventure story, same format; metric: preview starts, orders.
- Assumption to validate: that LumeBook's catalogue has matching stories, and that this angle beats generic gift framing. This is the weakest-supported hypothesis.

**Two risks the research raises for LumeBook specifically**
- MeBook is publicly attacking soft-cover competitors ("כריכה רכה וזולה", "חוברת זולה", https://www.instagram.com/p/DejyWEUgavW/). Our brief records a soft cover. Do not claim hardcover; if we show the physical book, show it honestly and lead with what is true (preview, delivery, digital copy if still offered).
- A MeBook buyer says an ad page at the end makes the book "פחות אישי" (#15). Our brief records back pages that recommend other books or offers. Worth checking with the store owner.

## F. Five questions for LumeBook customers

Neutral wording; ask in this order; record answers word for word.

1. "Who was the book for, and what was going on at the time you decided to order it?"
2. "Before ordering, what else, if anything, did you think about giving or buying instead?"
3. "Was there anything that made you hesitate before you ordered? If so, what was it?"
4. "Tell me about the first time the child saw the book. What happened?"
5. "Since it arrived, how has the book been used, if at all?"

## Recommended first three experiments

1. **H2, the recognition moment.** The strongest evidence (12 statements across two source types) and the cheapest to test if we have one real first-open video. Guard: film only books where the likeness is clearly good.
2. **H1, gift buyers outside the parents.** 19 statements, and it is a framing change on content we already make (same book, different giver and caption). Easy to split by UTM.
3. **H5, answer price, timing and "how" in the post.** Moderate evidence but almost free: a pinned comment template and a last carousel slide. Fast to read through comment questions and link clicks.

H3 (repeat reading) has strong evidence too but needs three weeks of footage; start collecting it now so it can be the fourth test.
