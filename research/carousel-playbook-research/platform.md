# Instagram carousels: platform facts, official statements and data studies

Research for the LumeBook carousel playbook. Checked 2026-10-06.

**Method.** I used WebSearch (about 43 queries) and WebFetch. help.instagram.com pages are rendered with JavaScript, and plain fetches returned an empty "Help Center" shell. I therefore opened those pages, plus several Meta developer and business pages, in a real browser and read the rendered text. Facts marked "rendered 2026-10-06" were read directly from the live page.

**Evidence tags**
- **[official]**: Meta or Instagram documentation, or a first-hand statement by Instagram staff (or a direct quote of one in a news report).
- **[platform data study]**: a study by an analytics vendor using its own customer data.
- **[practitioner opinion]**: blogs, agencies, guides.
- **[unverified]**: I could not open or confirm the source.
- **[first-hand observation]**: something I observed myself in the product.

**Where the studies disagree**
- **Reach.** Buffer says Reels out-reach carousels by 36% (2022 to 2024 data). Socialinsider says carousels have the highest reach rate (4.50% vs 4.10%, 2025 to 2026 data). Socialinsider also reports more average views for carousels than Reels at every account size. Differences in period, panel and denominator likely explain this. Carousel "views" may also be inflated by the re-show behaviour (2.4). That last point is my inference: Meta does not document how carousel views are counted.
- **Engagement.** All studies rank carousels at or near the top on engagement. Hootsuite's citation of Metricool (Reels 6%) disagrees with Metricool's own press release (Reels have 4x the interactions of single images). The bases are probably different (ER vs absolute interactions).
- **Slide count.** No 2025 or 2026 study with a disclosed method compares 10 vs 20 slides or any slide count. The only quantified slide-count data is from 2020.

## 7. Hebrew / RTL swipe direction

- **Documentation:** I found no Meta or Instagram page, and no reliable report, describing carousel swipe direction in the Hebrew app. I ran two searches (English and Hebrew). Only generic RTL design guidance came up, nothing specific to Instagram. **Unverified for the iOS and Android apps.**
- **First-hand observation, web only, 2026-10-06** [first-hand observation]: I opened instagram.com in a desktop browser at 1024 px wide. The test post was a carousel from the @creators account: https://www.instagram.com/p/DXhNFjCkTyX/
  - With `?hl=he`, the page is set to right-to-left (`dir=rtl`) and the whole layout is mirrored: media on the right, comments on the left. The "הבא" (Next) arrow sits on the **left** edge of the image, and slide 2 is laid out to the **left** of slide 1. So in Hebrew the next slide comes in from the left.
  - With `?hl=en`, the page is left-to-right and the Next arrow is on the **right** edge.
  - Slide images themselves are not mirrored.
- **Implication:** the web UI suggests Instagram mirrors carousel navigation for Hebrew. Before committing to a Hebrew-only "swipe left" design (for example, panoramas that run across slides or "swipe ->" cues), check on a phone with the app set to Hebrew. This test is not done.

## Most decision-relevant facts (10 lines)
1. Carousels hold up to 20 slides in the app. Via the API and schedulers it is only 10 slides, at 4:5 to 1.91:1, so 3:4 or 11+ slides must be posted by hand (1.1, 1.3, 1.6).
2. 1080x1440 (3:4) is the tallest format the app keeps and best fills the vertical grid. Use 4:5 if you need a scheduler (1.4, 1.5, 1.8).
3. Mosseri: a carousel that is not swiped is often re-shown starting on slide 2. Slide 2 is a second hook, so it should make sense on its own (2.4).
4. Mosseri's top signals are watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. Sends matter more for reaching non-followers, so build slides worth sending to a co-parent or friend (2.2).
5. There is no per-slide or swipe metric anywhere, app or API. Measure at post level: shares, saves, profile activity, and the bio-link-click breakdown in the API (3.4, 3.5).
6. Caption links do not work (except a Meta Verified test). Conversion paths: up to 5 bio links, story link stickers, and comment-to-DM. Meta Business Suite has a native "Comment to message" keyword automation (4.1, 4.2, 4.4).
7. DM rules: one private reply per comment within 7 days; then a 24-hour window after the user replies; promotions allowed inside the window (4.5, 4.6).
8. Tag every link with UTMs (utm_source=instagram, utm_medium=social) so GA4 counts it as Organic Social instead of losing it to Direct through the in-app browser (4.8, 4.9).
9. Studies agree carousels lead on engagement and saves, but measure only engagement, not conversion, and use different denominators. Small accounts average about 1 save per post (5.1 to 5.5).
10. Israel: paid or gifted creator carousels need a clear Hebrew "פרסומת" or "ממומן" label at the very start, visible without a click; #ad or tags are not enough. Get recorded parental consent before showing any child's photo (8.1, 8.3).
