Single continuous shot, locked-off tripod camera: no camera movement, no zoom, no cuts. Nobody speaks.

For the first second nothing moves except the giant plastic toy dump truck, which rolls backward a little further into the room through the open doorway with a light toy wobble and stops. Then its yellow tipper bed tilts up steeply, hinged at the rear, and the whole load of strawberries slides out in one big avalanche: a wave of red strawberries pours onto the wooden floor and spreads across the room, over the rug and around the table legs toward the camera, bouncing and rolling, until the floor is covered. The last few strawberries tumble out of the raised, empty bed.

The toddler boy beside the open door calmly watches the strawberries pour past his legs, then looks down and picks one up. Lume, the furry mascot hiding under the table, watches in horror as the strawberries pile up around him to his chin; one strawberry lands on top of his head.

The truck stays a chunky plastic toy with all its wheels on the floor. The picture book and both microphones on the table do not move, and the book cover stays exactly as in the first frame. The room, chairs, shelves, plants, door and lighting stay unchanged. The boy stays a photorealistic real child and Lume stays a stylized animated character.

No speech, no music.
