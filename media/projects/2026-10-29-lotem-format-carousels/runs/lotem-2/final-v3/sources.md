- [Stopping use of a dummy and/or bottle](https://derbyshirefamilyhealthservice.nhs.uk/our-services/0-5-years/toddlers-1-3-years/feeding-and-nutrition/ditch-dummy-and-bottle) · Derbyshire Family Health Service (NHS), Derbyshire Community Health Services NHS Foundation Trust · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Teach new ways to soothe with a favourite toy; point out older children without a dummy; give the dummies to someone important; stories about children who give theirs away; cuddles and distraction first; praise dummy free times and keep it out of sight; the same rules for every carer including the nursery. An NHS service webpage, general advice.
  - quote: "Help them learn new ways to soothe themselves, such as using a favourite blanket or toy instead of the dummy. Point out older girls and boys who don't use dummies to your child. Pre-schoolers love being more grown-up. Encourage your child to give all their dummies away to a person who is important to them, such as a grandparent or nursery key worker. ... Reading story books or making up a story about children that give away their dummy can help. ... Try cuddles and distraction first, remove the dummy when they are calm. ... Praise times without the dummy, keep it out of sight and reach, set clear rules and stick to them. ... all parents and carers (including childcare settings) should follow the same steps so the message is consistent and the child is not confused."
- [Weaning off dummies and bottles](https://library.sheffieldchildrens.nhs.uk/weaning-off-dummies-and-bottles/) · Sheffield Children's NHS Foundation Trust, Resource Library · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Dummy only for naps and bedtime first, a favourite toy for comfort, a dummy free date, lots of praise, and keep it out of the child's sight. An NHS trust leaflet, general advice.
  - quote: "Start by just letting your child use their dummy for nap times and bed time. Try to swap it out for their favourite toy or teddy to hold onto for comfort instead. ... Once they are using their dummy less, set a date for being ‘dummy free’. Help them by giving them lots of praise, and give them lots of positive attention. ... Make sure you and other caregivers remove any bottles and dummies from your child’s sight."
- [Pacifier Weaning: When Is It the Right Time?](https://www.healthline.com/health/baby/pacifier-weaning) · Healthline, medically reviewed · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Tell the child in advance and repeat the message the next day; stretch the time between asking and getting it; offer other comfort; praise loudly; show empathy but do not give in. A medically reviewed consumer page, not a clinical guideline.
  - quote: "Explain to your toddler that in 3 days’ time, you’ll be taking away their paci because they’re already big enough to manage without one. Repeat your message the following day. ... Show your child that they can manage without their paci by gently stretching out the time from when they ask for their paci and when you hand it over. Offer them other comfort measures instead. When they successfully go without the paci, praise them loudly. ... Brace yourself against the tantrums and the crying, show the empathy that you surely feel (no one said it would be easy), but do not give in."
- [Dummies](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/dummies) · Better Health Channel, Victorian Department of Health · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Stop gradually, not suddenly; limit the dummy to sleep, drop it after daytime naps; talk about it for a couple of days beforehand; a chosen cuddly toy in its place; give it to the dummy fairy or a younger child's parent; encouragement; gently but firmly; the first days are hardest; it may take a few attempts. An Australian state health page, general advice.
  - quote: "Most children stop sucking habits between the ages of two and four, but you can start the process from around one year of age. ... Avoid trying to stop dummy use suddenly, as it can lead to other oral habits such as thumb or finger sucking. ... Limit the use of the dummy to sleep or nighttime. Stopping dummy use after daytime naps have finished can help reduce the amount the dummy is required. Talk about stopping the dummy for a couple of days to prepare your child. Consider replacing it with a toy, maybe a cuddly toy they have chosen and can hold when they would be using the dummy. ... Encourage your child to give their dummy away to the dummy fairy, or to another parent of a younger child. Give plenty of encouragement and keep it positive. Persist gently but firmly. The first few days without a dummy are likely to be the most difficult. Be patient – it may take a few attempts."
- [Baby Pacifiers & Thumb Sucking: What Parents Need to Know](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/crying-colic/Pages/Pacifiers-and-Thumb-Sucking.aspx) · HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics (last updated 20 Jan 2026) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Praise when the child goes without the pacifier, explain the method to the child, and stop if it makes the child afraid or tense. AAP page about pacifiers and thumb sucking.
  - quote: "Praise and reward your child when they do not suck their thumb or use the pacifier. ... No matter what method you try, be sure to explain it to your child. If it makes your child afraid or tense, stop right away."
