# Sources · lotem-3 · 2026-10-29

- [Toilet Training (Potty Training)](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/toilet-teaching.html) · Nemours KidsHealth (reviewed March 2019) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Look for readiness signs, never sit a child on the toilet against their will, a routine (for example after waking or after drinking, a few minutes), praise every attempt, no punishment for accidents.
  - quote: "Instead of using age, look for signs that your child may be ready to start heading for the potty. Don't make your child sit on the toilet against their will. Establish a routine. Praise all attempts to use the toilet, even if nothing happens. It's important not to punish potty-training children or show disappointment when they wet or soil themselves or the bed."
- [Potty Training: 12 Tips to Keep the Process Positive](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Praise-and-Reward-Your-Childs-Success.aspx) · HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics (last updated 6 Nov 2023) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Potty chair that fits, role model, words, morning routine, big-kid underwear, no power struggle, understand fear and let the child flush.
  - quote: "Pick a potty-chair. Children's feet should be able to reach the floor. Be a role model. Let your children see you use the toilet and wash your hands afterward. Choose positive words your family will use to describe body parts, urine and bowel movements. Make a habit of seating your children onto the potty-chair first thing in the morning. When toilet training starts, switch to big-kid underwear. Avoid a power struggle. Remember that children control when and where they pee and poop. Understand their fear. To give your children a feeling of control, let them flush the toilet."
- [Create a Potty Training Plan for Your Child](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/creating-a-toilet-training-plan.aspx) · HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics (last updated 25 May 2022) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: A consistent routine, praise for progress, and a calm response to an accident (ask the child to help clean up).
  - quote: "Keep their bathroom routine as consistent as possible, with their potty in the same place every day. When they progress, give them a hug, some praise, and perhaps even a small tangible reward. Tell them you're sure they'll do better next time and ask them to help you clean up."
