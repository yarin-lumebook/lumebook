- [Restraint collapse: Why kids fall apart after school](https://www.understood.org/en/articles/restraint-collapse-why-kids-fall-apart-after-school) · Understood · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Holding it together at school and letting go at home where it feels safe, decompression time (food, rest, connection) before demands, hugs, calming music and quiet space, a snack, not asking about problems straight away, not raising your voice, an empathy sentence, open arms and talking once calm. The page is mostly about kids with ADHD or autism and says it can happen to any child; used as general advice for school-age children.
  - quote: "After-school restraint collapse can happen to any child. ... Kids face a lot of demands at school beyond academics. They may be able to keep their stress and emotions in check while they're at school with their teachers and peers. When kids get home, they lose that self-control and experience after-school restraint collapse. Usually, restraint collapse happens where kids feel safest. And that's often at home, with people who support and understand them. ... "The best way to manage afterschool outbursts is to plan for decompression time right when kids get home. You want to address hunger, tiredness, and the need to share or connect about their day before you make any demands like homework or chores." ... Provide hugs, blanket wraps, calming music, and quiet space ... A snack can help your child get back on an even keel. ... If your child is upset or moody, it's natural to ask about problems at school. But doing that immediately may actually cause your child to focus on the negatives. "Try talking about what was good or best today, or what was challenging" ... Raising your voice will only increase the emotions your child is struggling with. Show empathy and say, "I can see you're upset. Take some time to calm down and we'll talk about it." ... If your child's form of emotional release is crying, be there with open arms. ... Wait until your child is calm. Talk in a neutral way that won't stir up more emotions."
