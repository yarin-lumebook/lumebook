  - supports: Children can learn to handle disappointment with support; empathic words, time and space to cry and a hug; calm from the parent and the talk later; three questions (how did it feel, what did you wish would happen, what can be done differently next time); young children hear might as will, so a list of hopes, possibilities and sure things; calming skills such as rainbow breathing and drawing. A PBS KIDS for Parents page.
  - quote: "With proper support and frequent discussions about coping with disappointment and overcoming obstacles, young children can learn how to handle life’s disappointments, big and small. ... Use empathic responses like, “I understand that this is difficult. I know you feel disappointed right now.” Give your child the time and space to cry, feel sad and soak up a hug from mom or dad. Connection helps kids recover from adversity. Meet heated responses with calm ones to model healthy coping strategies and save the discussion for a later, calmer moment. ... try asking a few questions that empower him to solve the problem: How did it feel when that happened? What did you wish would happen? What can be done differently the next time? ... Young children have a tendency to engage in all-or-nothing thinking. When parents say, “A stop at a theme park might be fun,” young children hear: “We’re going to a park and it will be great!” ... Make a list to indicate hopes, possibilities and sure things. ... Breathe the rainbow — practice deep breathing while thinking about favorite things for each color of the rainbow ... Use art to work through feelings — coloring, drawing and molding clay can all be relaxing"
