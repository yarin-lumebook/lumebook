            Messages and voice

            Hebrew grammar rules the messages follow
            • ה (the) only for something specific and known: הילד, הספר, העמוד. Not for general statements: ספרי ילדים, ילדים אוהבים.
            • ל, ב, כ swallow the ה: לילד, בגן, לתוכן (never להילד, בהגן). מ, עם, של, על, את keep it: מהגן, עם הספר, של הילד.
            • Construct chains (סמיכות): the article goes on the last noun only: מנהל הסושיאל, ספר הילדים.
            • את only before a definite object: אהבתי את התוכן.
            • Adjectives agree and take ה too: הילד הקטן. Names and LumeBook never take it.
            • Your own typed edits are never changed. The rules apply to the suggested messages and to what the voice learning writes.

            Method & limits

            How this was built

            Search methodology
            • Web search (6 parallel agents, ~120 searches). Hebrew and Arabic queries plus site:instagram.com / site:tiktok.com, and Hebrew listicles (mako, ynet, 9instyle, Walla). Yield was low: about 120 leads, many of them shops, inactive or not Israeli.
            • Instagram similar-accounts graph (14 seeds). Profiles of a kindergarten teacher, SLP, OT, dad, Montessori, books, homeschool, potty-training and gan-consultant creators. Most of the final list came from here.
            • Instagram account search (58 keywords). Hebrew, Arabic, Russian and English terms. This filled the gaps web search couldn't: Arabic and Russian SLPs, twins moms, grandparents, home daycares, dads, homeschoolers.
            • Second pass (same day). I crawled Instagram's similar-accounts data from 76 verified Hebrew creators (about 2,000 accounts), filtered them by name and bio line, then added more seeds and keyword searches for dads, grandparents, gan and book creators. About 190 more were checked one by one, and 134 passed.
            • Rule since 12 Oct 2026: 5,000 followers or more. Creators under 5,000 who had not been contacted were removed from the map (61, including 28 of the third pass). Under-5K creators who were already messaged stay, with their conversations.
            • Third pass (11 Oct 2026). I took Instagram's similar-accounts lists from 117 creators already in the map, then from 74 of the new finds, and ran 48 Hebrew keyword searches: about 3,800 accounts in all. I opened the 446 most relevant ones, read followers, bio and the latest reels, and kept 100. Most of the ones I dropped were baby-sleep, birth, nutrition or shop accounts, or outside 1K to 50K followers. About 80 of the 100 are a clear fit; about 20 are borderline and say so in their risk note.
            • Hebrew only. At your request, Arabic- and Russian-language creators were removed.
            • Verification. Every listed creator was opened read-only in a logged-in browser, on 3 Oct 2026 for the first two passes, on 11 Oct 2026 for the third and on 18 Oct 2026 for the fourth. I read follower count, bio and category, plus each of the last 12 reels' views and its post date (decoded from the reel ID). TikTok was read with yt-dlp (latest 15 videos). I also checked mebook.ai's Instagram “tagged” tab for competitor overlap.
            Query set

            Scoring

            Ten 1-5 ratings, weighted: audience fit 14, creator-brand fit 14, parent trust 12, UGC potential 12, authenticity 10, content quality 8, professional credibility 8, commercial usability 8, affordability 8, on-camera 6. Score = weighted sum ÷ 5, so 100 is the maximum. Follower count is not an input. Views feed content quality and UGC potential only as a signal.

            What was not verified
            • Comments and engagement quality were not read. That needs a manual look at 2-3 posts per creator before outreach.
            • On-camera ability and content quality are inferred from reel-first accounts, bios and view levels. I did not watch the videos. Treat those two ratings as estimates.
            • Sponsorship history covers bio signals plus MeBook's tagged tab only. Only @mor_knoll appeared there.
            • Collaboration practicality is a category estimate. No rates were sought or invented.
            • Scoring method differs by pass. The 70 first-pass creators and 3 second-pass creators were rated by hand. The other 131 second-pass creators and all 100 third-pass creators get stricter rule-based ratings from archetype, views-to-followers ratio, reel recency and size. Use the per-creator notes rather than small score gaps.
            • Language is inferred from Hebrew names and bios; I didn't listen to audio. A few creators write bios in English.
            • Audience demographics (share of parents, cities) were not measured.
            • Contacts are only ones each creator publishes themselves (Instagram bio or bio links, or their own Linktree or website). 81 of 243 have an email or phone; the rest are Instagram DM only. Instagram's web version doesn't show a business account's contact buttons, so some creators may have more. I didn't check which numbers are personal and which are business lines, so confirm before messaging, and send one short, personal message each.
