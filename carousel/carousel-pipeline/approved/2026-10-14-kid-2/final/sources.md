# Sources · kid-2 · 2026-10-14

- [Communication Skills Start at Home](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Components-of-Good-Communication.aspx) · American Academy of Pediatrics, HealthyChildren.org (adapted from HealthyChildren Magazine, Winter 2022; updated 16 Mar 2023) · read 2026-10-13
  - supports: Children are often taught to say sorry without naming what happened; a full apology names the specific wrong; parents can model full apologies.
  - quote: "We often teach children to say "I'm sorry" without teaching them to acknowledge the issue. ... Instead, try "I am sorry I yelled at you. Mommy felt frustrated with the mess." ... Model full apologies ("I am sorry for...")."
- [How to Help Your Child Develop Empathy](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/how-to-help-your-child-develop-empathy/) · Zero To Three (page dated 1 Feb 2016) · read 2026-10-13
  - supports: Insisting on 'sorry' does not necessarily build empathy in toddlers; help children focus on the other person's feelings by naming them; empathy takes time; reading stories about feelings is recommended. Written for toddlers; we apply it to ages 3 to 5 as an analogy.
  - quote: "We often insist that our toddlers say "I'm sorry" as a way for them to take responsibility for their actions. ... it doesn't necessarily help toddlers learn empathy. ... help children focus on the other person's feelings. ... Developing empathy takes time. ... Kayla is feeling sad because you took her toy car. ... Read stories about feelings."
- [Toddlers and Challenging Behavior: Why They Do It and How to Respond](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/toddlers-and-challenging-behavior-why-they-do-it-and-how-to-respond/) · Zero To Three (page dated 29 Feb 2020) · read 2026-10-13
  - supports: Asking a toddler to say sorry without understanding what they did does not teach from the action; parents modelling apologies helps. Written for toddlers; applied to ages 3 to 5 as an analogy.
  - quote: "Toddlers who are asked to say “sorry” without fully understanding what they’ve done wrong, do not learn from their actions. ... It helps when parents model how to apologize and take responsibility for their actions too."
- [Forcing kids to say 'I'm sorry' doesn't fool anybody](https://www.futurity.org/forced-apologies-children-1920802-2/) · Futurity, reporting a University of Michigan study (Craig Smith et al., Merrill-Palmer Quarterly, children aged 4 to 9), 30 Nov 2018 · read 2026-10-13
  - supports: In a study of children, coerced apologies were seen as less effective (especially by ages 7 to 9); the researcher advises waiting until the child is calm and helping them see how the other person feels; preschoolers value amends made with action. The study measured children's judgments, not long-term outcomes.
  - quote: "Coercing your child to apologize is going to backfire. ... But the coerced apologies weren't seen as effective, especially by the 7-to-9-year-olds, Smith says. ... "When your child is calm, help them see how the other person is feeling, and why," Smith says. "Research shows that even preschoolers value it when a wrongdoer makes amends with action.""
- [Taming Tempers](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/temper.html) · Nemours KidsHealth (reviewed June 2022, Margaux J. Barnes, PhD) · read 2026-10-13
  - supports: After an outburst wait until the child calms down before talking; they may need time to reflect before being ready to talk. The page is about tempers, applied here to the moment after a conflict.
  - quote: "Once your child calms down, ask what got them so upset. ... But don't push too hard for your child to talk right then. ... They may need some time to reflect before being ready to talk."
