# Sources · kid-1 · 2026-12-03

- [Parenting Strategies for a Child Who is Easily Frustrated](https://zerotothree.org/resource/parenting-strategies-for-a-child-who-is-easily-frustrated) · ZERO TO THREE (page dated 22 Feb 2010) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Acknowledge how hard it is, coach instead of doing it for the child, suggest or show a strategy, break the challenge into parts, offer a break with a hug and return with new energy.
  - quote: "When your child falls apart, let her know that you appreciate how hard it can be. ... Then become her coach—help your child think through solutions without doing the work for her. ... Suggest or demonstrate strategies for solving whatever problem she is facing. ... Break the challenge into manageable parts. ... Offer time away from the frustrating task for a hug or a cozy snuggle with a book. ... Then return to the challenge with new energy."
- [How to Help Kids Learn to Fail](https://childmind.org/article/how-to-help-kids-learn-to-fail/) · Child Mind Institute (last reviewed or updated 6 May 2025) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Name the frustration first, give time to feel it, and when the child is ready talk about what to do differently next time.
  - quote: "When your kid does fail at something, let them know you see how frustrated and disappointed they are. ... Skipping right to bouncing back doesn't give kids the time they need to feel their feelings. ... When your kid is ready, talk about what went wrong and how they might do things differently next time. ... You and your child can try to come up with what they could do the next time for a better chance at success."
- [How to Support Young Kids Who Are Struggling in School](https://childmind.org/article/how-to-support-young-children-who-are-struggling-in-school/) · Child Mind Institute (last reviewed or updated 7 Nov 2025, Rae Jacobson) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Short bursts of difficult work build resilience, and praising effort rather than results makes trying feel safer. The page is about school; applied here to any hard task at home.
  - quote: "Help kids build their resilience by doing small bursts of difficult work ... Praising your child’s efforts instead of focusing on results will help them feel more comfortable trying things ... Managing frustration without blowing up, or giving up, is an essential skill for kids (and grownups)"
- [Recommended Reading: Books to Teach Kids About Feelings & Resilience](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/recommended-reading-books-to-teach-kids-about-feelings-and-resilience.aspx) · HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics (last updated 24 Apr 2025) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Reading together about feelings helps children recognise their own and understand others'. Used only as support for reading stories about a hard moment; no promise of a result.
  - quote: "Reading books together about common feelings can help children recognize their own emotions. It can also help them develop empathy, understanding how others may be feeling."
