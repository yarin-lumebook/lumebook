- [8 ways to make school morning routines easier](https://www.understood.org/articles/school-morning-routines-tips) · Understood · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Night-before preparation, a predictable routine, designated spots, clocks and countdowns, a double-check, a gentle wake-up, the adult starting early and a game if time is left. An American page that also covers neurodivergent kids, used as general advice.
  - quote: "Ask your child to pick out an outfit for the next day. ... Check that all books, homework, and changes of clothing are packed up and by the door. ... If you have breakfast as a family, set the breakfast table. ... Wake up a little earlier than your child. ... Try not to be racing around to get yourself ready at the same time. This will lower the stress level for both of you. ... Set your child's alarm to play a favorite song. Try waking your child up with lots of snuggles or a favorite breakfast. ... Make every day as predictable as you can. Follow the same routine before and after school. ... Wake up, wash face, eat breakfast, get dressed, brush teeth, go over the day's schedule, leave for school. A picture schedule can be extra helpful for kids who struggle with reading. ... Designate spots for school supplies, sports gear, coats, and jackets. ... Make sure there are clocks "in your face" around the house. ... Give manageable countdowns, like "At 7:25, it'll be time to put your shoes on." ... Have your child check and re-check to make sure everything's packed before leaving the house. Younger kids can come up with a silly phrase or song to help remember all the books and materials they need each morning. ... if your child gets ready for school and still has time to spare before it's time to leave, play a quick game together or read a book."
- [Experts weigh in: How can I get my young child less anxious about starting school?](https://www.understood.org/en/articles/experts-weigh-in-how-can-i-get-my-young-child-less-anxious-about-starting-school) · Understood (experts including Janine Domingues, Child Mind Institute) · read 2026-10-29

- [Back to School](https://kidshealth.org/NemoursEMR/en/parents/back-school.html) · Nemours KidsHealth · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Talking about worries, breakfast, setting out backpack and clothes the night before, and that a few kids get stomachaches at the start of school.
  - quote: "It's also important to talk to kids about any worries and offer support. ... Eat a healthy breakfast. They're more alert and do better in school if they eat a good breakfast every day. ... Organize and set out what they need the night before. Homework and books should be put in their backpacks by the door and clothes should be laid out in their bedrooms. ... But a few kids develop real physical symptoms, such as headaches or stomachaches, at the start of school."
- [Sleep and Your School-Aged Child](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/sleep-school-age.html) · Nemours KidsHealth (medically reviewed by Mary L. Gavin, MD, January 2021) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Sleep needs of school-age children, screens off an hour before bed, counting back from the wake-up time and keeping weekend times close to weekdays.
  - quote: "School-age kids need 9–12 hours of sleep a night. ... Turn off all screens at least 1 hour before bedtime. ... Set regular bedtimes and wake-up times. To be sure your child gets enough sleep, decide when your child needs to get up for school in the morning and count back 9–12 hours from there. On weekends, try to stick to the sleep schedule as close as you can. Aim for sleep or wake-up times no more than an hour later than on weekdays."
