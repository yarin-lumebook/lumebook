# Sources · kid-2 · 2026-10-11

- [Why Kids Behave Differently at Home vs. School](https://childmind.org/article/kids-different-home-school/) · Child Mind Institute (Beth Arky; clinical experts Stephanie A. Lee, PsyD and Jerry Bubrick, PhD; last reviewed or updated 19 May 2026) · read 2026-10-10
  - supports: Some children hold it together at school and fall apart at home; hunger after a hard day makes it worse; home is where they feel safe enough to let go; a snack, rest and lower demands help.
  - quote: "There are also kids who work so hard to keep it together at school that they melt down the second they get home. ... Coming home starving, after struggling all day to keep it together, is a good recipe for an afternoon meltdown. ... They feel safe to be "their worst selves" at home, secure in the knowledge that their parents will still love and support them. ... advises parents to get kids back on track by giving them a snack and having them rest. ... It's absolutely OK to have less stringent demands during that period of time for your child if they need a break."
- [Restraint collapse: Why kids fall apart after school](https://www.understood.org/en/articles/why-some-kids-fall-when-they-get-home-from-school) · Understood (Gail Belsky; reviewed by Kristin J. Carothers, PhD; published 9 Feb 2026, last modified 5 Aug 2026) · read 2026-10-10
  - supports: School asks a lot of self-control; many children let go when they get home; plan decompression time right at arrival; a snack helps; asking about the day immediately can pull the child toward the negatives; hugs, a blanket, calm music and quiet space help.
  - quote: "Kids face a lot of demands at school beyond academics. ... When kids get home, they lose that self-control and experience after-school restraint collapse. ... After-school restraint collapse can happen to any child. ... A snack can help your child get back on an even keel. ... The best way to manage afterschool outbursts is to plan for decompression time right when kids get home, ... doing that immediately may actually cause your child to focus on the negatives. ... Provide hugs, blanket wraps, calming music, and quiet space"
- [5 Ways Books Can Help Your Anxious Child](https://www.pbs.org/parents/thrive/5-ways-books-can-help-your-anxious-child) · PBS KIDS for Parents (Stephanie Murray, 18 Jun 2020) · read 2026-10-10
  - supports: A shared story can help a child talk about it and connect it to their own feelings. (The article is about anxious children; we apply it to the feelings of a long school day.)
  - quote: "You might find your little one is able to talk about the story, and, in doing so, make connections to their own emotions."
