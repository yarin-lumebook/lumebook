# Sources · expert-1 · 2026-10-20

- [Potty Training Regression](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Regression.aspx) · HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics (last updated 25 May 2022; adapted from the AAP Guide to Toilet Training, 2nd Edition) · read 2026-10-16
  - supports: Toilet training regression: see the pediatrician first to rule out physical causes such as constipation or a urinary tract infection; otherwise it is usually a response to a change or stress (new child care, new sibling, a move); it usually lasts days or weeks. Tips on the same page: ask the child why, fix what can be fixed (familiar objects around the potty, going with the child to the child-care bathroom), ask the child for ideas so they own the solution, be clear and confident about expectations with hugs, praise and stickers, and consider a break only if regression stretches on for a month or more.
  - quote: "Your first response when this happens should be to have them examined by their pediatrician to be sure the cause is not physical. Regression sometimes signals an infection or other disorder that requires medical treatment. If medical causes have been ruled out, however, your child is probably just responding in the only way they know how to a recent change in their environment or some other source of stress in their life. ... Change in the child-care routine ... The mother's pregnancy or the birth of a new sibling ... An upcoming or recent move to a new home ... Constipation/painful bowel movements ... Urinary tract infection ... Upsetting as regression can be to parents, it usually doesn't last very long. In many cases, the child picks up where she left off in toilet training after a few days or weeks."
- [Emotional Issues and Potty Training Problems](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Emotional-Issues-and-Bathroom-Problems.aspx) · HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics (last updated 25 May 2022; adapted from the AAP Guide to Toilet Training, 2nd Edition) · read 2026-10-16
  - supports: From about age 3 children can feel shame after an accident and may hide it; no criticism or punishment; say gently that it is okay and they will do better next time, then ask them to help clean up; constantly asking whether they need to go can backfire; fear of the toilet is possible; avoid going back to diapers, offer training pants, the potty in their room or going with them to the preschool bathroom.
  - quote: "At about three years of age, children start developing the capacity to experience discomfort, or shame, when they have done something they know is wrong. ... A child who longs for their parents' approval may suddenly start to feel embarrassed or ashamed after a bathroom accident, no matter how accepting their parents are actually likely to be. ... There is no cause to criticize or punish a child who behaves in this way. ... The best response when you find your child hiding their accidents from you is to gently tell them that you know they had an accident, that it's okay, and that you know they will do better next time. Then ask them to help you clean up ... If they feel you have been too controlling about bathroom use—constantly asking if they need to go instead of letting them direct their own behavior—they may resist going until it's too late and they have an accident instead. A more active imagination and a tendency toward magical thinking may cause them to fear the toilet and start to avoid it. ... try to avoid major concessions such as returning to diapers. Instead, consider offering to put them in training pants under their underwear for a while, put the potty in their room, accompany them to the preschool bathroom before the school day begins, or otherwise compromise until they feel more secure."
- [Potty Training](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/toilet-teaching.html) · Nemours KidsHealth (medically reviewed by KidsHealth Medical Experts, date reviewed March 2019) · read 2026-10-16
  - supports: Do not punish or show disappointment after an accident; tell the child it was an accident and offer support; praise attempts; stickers; clothes the child can take off alone; all caregivers use the same routine; stress such as a new sibling can bring accidents back.
  - quote: "Avoid clothes that are hard to take off, such as overalls and shirts that snap in the crotch. Kids who are potty training need to be able to undress themselves. ... Offer your child small rewards, such as stickers or time reading, every time your child goes in the potty. ... Make sure all caregivers — including babysitters, grandparents, and daycare workers — follow the same routine ... Praise all attempts to use the toilet, even if nothing happens. And remember that accidents will happen. It's important not to punish potty-training children or show disappointment when they wet or soil themselves or the bed. Instead, tell your child that it was an accident and offer your support. ... Many kids who've been using the potty have some trouble during times of stress. For example, a 2- or 3-year-old dealing with a new sibling may start having accidents."
