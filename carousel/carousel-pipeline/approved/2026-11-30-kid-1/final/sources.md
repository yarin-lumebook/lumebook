# Sources · kid-1 · 2026-11-30

- [How Can We Help Kids With Transitions?](https://childmind.org/article/how-can-we-help-kids-with-transitions/) · Child Mind Institute (Martinelli), last updated 15 Sep 2025 · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Giving a time frame, a description of what will happen and countdowns helps a child get ready emotionally; drawings and visual cues help some children. The page is about transitions, applied here to waiting for a birthday.
  - quote: "give a time frame and description of what will happen along with countdowns ... allows them to emotionally get ready for an event ... Being able to point to a chart with drawings about what to expect from a particular transition ... Other kids may benefit from visual cues."
- [Create a Kid's Calendar in Two Ways](https://www.pbs.org/parents/crafts-and-experiments/create-a-kids-calendar) · PBS KIDS for Parents, 23 Jul 2018 · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: A big event such as a birthday is a chance for a child to learn to keep track of days and months on a calendar. The page does not describe a birthday countdown as such; the daily crossing off is our example.
  - quote: "Waiting for big events like birthdays, holidays, or the start of school are great opportunities"
- [Helping Kids Learn to Take Turns and Practice Waiting](https://www.pbs.org/parents/thrive/helping-kids-learn-to-take-turns-and-practice-waiting) · PBS KIDS for Parents (Samantha Gratton), 4 Aug 2023 · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Waiting is hard, a visual countdown timer helps, something else to do while waiting helps, and talking about what comes next comforts a child and says who has the next turn.
  - quote: "Waiting is hard (even for grown-ups)! ... You can also use a timer that shows a countdown for taking turns! ... gave each person something else to do while they waited! ... Talking about what comes next can comfort your child and remind them who gets the next turn."
- [How to help your preschooler manage overexcitement](https://www.understood.org/en/articles/how-to-help-your-preschooler-manage-overexcitement) · Understood, 5 Aug 2019 · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Prepare the child ahead and give a five-minute notice when a preschooler gets louder and too excited. The page is about overexcitement in general, applied to the days before a birthday.
  - quote: "Prep your child ... Plan on giving a five-minute notice when you see they start to get louder and too excited."
