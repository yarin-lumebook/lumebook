  - quote: "The fall time change, meanwhile, can cause younger kids to crash before bedtime and wake up earlier than usual in the morning. ... For younger children, start nudging naps and bedtime in the direction the clocks will be changing a few minutes each day. Ideally, start this process about a week before the time change. ... Having set nighttime routines. This lets your child's body know that it's time to unwind and go to sleep. For younger kids, having a brush, book bed routine, cuddling, or listening to quiet music can help. ... Have your child put all screens away at least an hour before bedtime ... Spending some time outside and being exposed to natural light during the day can help reset your child's internal clock after a time change. ... Getting enough exercise during the day can help kids sleep better, too. Just avoid too much physical activity close to bedtime ... Examples include a warm bath with Epsom salts, reading a book that's not on a screen, meditation, soft music ... While time changes can cause some drowsy days, kids usually adjust within about a week. If your child continues to have problems sleeping, talk with your pediatrician."
- [Helping Kids Adjust to the Time Change](https://www.nationwidechildrens.org/family-resources-education/700childrens/2025/10/helping-kids-adjust-to-the-time-change) · Nationwide Children's Hospital, Columbus, Ohio (October 2025) · read 2026-10-16
  - supports: The body clock does not reset at once and it takes a few days, like jet lag; shift the schedule 10 to 15 minutes a night in the week before; keep the bedtime steps; blackout curtains; evening light; adjust naps gradually; consistency in meals and playtime; cool, dark, quiet bedroom. Written for the US fall change; applied to Israel's.
  - quote: "Kids don’t automatically adjust to new schedules, and the shift can lead to crankiness, early waking, and bedtime battles. ... their internal “body clock” (also known as the circadian rhythm) doesn’t reset immediately. ... Just like jet lag, it takes a few days for the body to catch up. ... In the week leading up to the time change, try shifting your child’s schedule by 10–15 minutes every night. ... Stick with the familiar steps—bathing, brushing teeth, reading, cuddling ... Use blackout curtains if your child tends to wake with the sun. ... Evening light helps reset the body clock and supports the shift. ... Adjust naps gradually in conjunction with bedtime and try not to let naps run too long ... It may take a few days (sometimes up to a week) for kids to fully adjust. Consistency with meals, playtime, and bedtime helps the new rhythm “stick.” ... Keep bedrooms cool, dark, and quiet."
- [How to Prepare Kids for the End of Daylight Saving Time This November](https://www.connecticutchildrens.org/sleep/daylight-savings-time-ways-to-prepare) · Connecticut Children's (16 Sept 2021, Lynelle Schneeberg, PsyD, pediatric sleep psychologist) · read 2026-10-16
  - supports: Gradual week-before method (bedtime shifted in 15 minute steps), the weekend-of method (change clocks Saturday night after the child is asleep, get up at Monday's time, 30 to 60 minutes of sunlight, breakfast at the new time), about a week to adjust, the same wake time every day. Written for the US change; applied to Israel's.
  - quote: "Adjust little by little in the week before the change. If your child goes to bed at 8 pm, put them to bed at 8:15pm, 8:30pm, 8:45pm and so on ... Saturday night: After your child goes to bed, change the clocks in your home. Sunday morning: Get your child up at the time they’ll need to rise on Monday morning. Right away, get them out and about in sunlight for 30 to 60 minutes. Have breakfast at the new time, too. ... It should take your child about a week to adjust. ... Always get your child up around the same time each day, whether it’s a weekday or a weekend."
