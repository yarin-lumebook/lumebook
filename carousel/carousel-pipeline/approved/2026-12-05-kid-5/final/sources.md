# Sources · kid-5 · 2026-12-05

- [There's a reason your child wants to read the same book over and over again](https://www.uow.edu.au/media/2018/theres-a-reason-your-child-wants-to-read-the-same-book-over-and-over-again.php) · University of Wollongong (J. Herbert and E. Duursma, first published in The Conversation), 2 Nov 2018 · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Young children often prefer the familiar, and hearing the same story again helps them learn and remember; the authors also note that repeated reading lets children notice other details and talk about the story. A university research summary, used as general advice.
  - quote: "A preference for familiarity, rather than novelty, is commonly reported at young ages ... Repetition aids learning complex information by increasing opportunities for the information to be encoded ... Repeated exposure helps children encode and remember from these sources"
- [How to Deal With Reading the Same Book 100 Times](https://www.scholastic.com/parents/books-and-reading/raise-a-reader-blog/how-to-deal-reading-same-book-100-times.html) · Scholastic, Raise a Reader Blog (Allison McDonald), 26 Feb 2015 · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Young children thrive on repetition; practical ideas: funny voices, books with the same character, two books at bedtime, swapping roles, and the favourite book as a security object. A publisher's parenting blog, used as general advice.
  - quote: "Young kids thrive on repetition. ... Read in funny accents. ... Find books with the same character or by the same author and illustrator. ... Read two books at bedtime. ... You read a new book and have them read you their old favorite. ... That book is a security object for them, and clearly you are reading to them a lot."
- [How to Help Your Child Develop Empathy](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/how-to-help-your-child-develop-empathy/) · ZERO TO THREE, 1 Feb 2016 · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Reading stories about feelings is one of the ways Zero To Three suggests to help a child with big feelings. The page is about empathy, not about losing games; used only as support for reading stories about a hard moment.
  - quote: "Read stories about feelings."
