# Sources · kid-1 · 2026-12-01

- [Preparing Your Child for a New Sibling](https://childmind.org/article/preparing-child-new-sibling/) · Child Mind Institute (last reviewed or updated 2 Jan 2026) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: One on one time with each parent, regression is normal, praising mature behaviour, picture books about new babies in the bedtime ritual, holding the baby with adult supervision, and keeping the routines the child counts on.
  - quote: "Telling kids about a new sibling early and maintaining one-on-one time with each parent helps reduce anxiety and jealousy. ... Regression behaviors like wanting bottles are normal ways for children to seek attention and will pass with time. ... It's also good to praise the positive mature behaviors the child exhibits. ... Board books and picture books about new babies are a great thing to add to the usual bedtime ritual. ... with adult supervision, learn how to hold and gently touch the baby. ... If there are any important routines that your child counts on, it's a good idea to share that information ahead of time."
- [Preparing Your Child for a New Baby](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/sibling-prep.html) · KidsHealth from Nemours (medically reviewed by KidsHealth Medical Experts, no date shown) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Jealousy toward the newborn is common, daily undivided time with one parent, letting the child help, talking about feelings, feelings are fine but need appropriate ways to be expressed, and looking at the child's baby pictures.
  - quote: "It's common for them to feel jealousy toward the newborn and to act out. ... set aside time each day for older kids to get one parent's undivided attention ... Many kids want to help take care of a new baby. ... Encourage older kids to talk about their feelings about the new baby. ... although his or her feelings are important, they have to be expressed in appropriate ways ... going through your child's baby pictures"
