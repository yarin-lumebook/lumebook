# Sources · expert-1 · 2026-10-31

- [Shyness in Kids](https://www.chop.edu/news/health-tip/shyness-kids) · Children's Hospital of Philadelphia (CHOP) health tip, published 22 Jul 2021, updated 15 Dec 2025 · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Some children need more time to warm up to new people and this is a natural part of development; avoid the label shy and say the child is slow to warm, letting them answer when comfortable; help with preparation and practice; tell them about a new playground before you go; follow their lead and walk around and observe first; start a conversation with the other parent and model introducing yourself and your child; practise with children of friends or family; prompt a first conversation on a shared interest; a quick compliment can break the ice; praise efforts such as playing near another child; offer a positive look back; ask what they need; ask an open question about a quiet afternoon. A US children's hospital page about shyness, not about any one age.
  - quote: "A CHOP psychologist explains that it's normal to have attachments to primary caregivers and a suspicion of strangers. It's a natural part of child development and some kids may need more time to adapt ... "We have to be very careful about labeling kids or allowing others to label our kids as 'shy' or 'stand-offish'. Instead, we encourage parents to say their child is slow to warm and encourage children themselves to respond to questions when they feel comfortable." ... you can help your slow-to-warm child with preparation, practice, positive feedback, validation and distraction. ... if you're headed to a new park or playground, tell your child about it before you get there ... When you get there, follow your child's lead. Slow-to-warm children may prefer to walk around the park with a parent first and observe. Encourage your child to talk about what they're seeing ... When your child is young, you may need to start a conversation with another parent before introducing your kids. ... You can help by modeling behavior, like introducing yourself and your child, to others. ... consider practicing at home or with children of your adult friends or close family members. If you know your child and the new friend share a common interest, let them know and prompt an initial conversation such as: "I heard you both like superheroes. What superpower would you like to have?" ... A quick compliment or comment can break the ice. ... When you see your kid trying to step out of their comfort zone, praise their efforts ... Maybe instead of playing alone, they played nearby another child, laughed at their jokes or asked a question. ... offering your child a positive "look back" after a recent experience. ... Ask your child what they need from you ... "I noticed you got really quiet after the park today, how were your feeling?""
- [Children with Shy or Slow to Warm Up Temperaments](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/children-with-shy-or-slow-to-warm-up-temperaments/) · ZERO TO THREE (page dated 4 May 2025) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Slow to warm children need time in a new setting, may want the parent close, often prefer one or two friends to a group; avoid labels; give notice about new people and places; practise with family and friends at home; read books about friendships. Written for toddlers (18 to 36 months); we apply it to ages 4 and 5 as an analogy.
  - quote: "uneasy or cautious in new situations or with unfamiliar people ... Need some time to get comfortable in a new setting, such as a friend's house or new playground ... Prefer to play with you, or have you close while she plays with others. ... they prefer to play with just one or two other good friends, rather than with a large group. ... Avoid labels. Telling someone who is slow to warm up to "try not to be so shy" is like saying, "Try not to be yourself." ... Give notice about new people, events, and places. ... Provide regular opportunities for social interaction in your home. Getting together with family and friends gives children an opportunity to practice social skills in a familiar, safe setting. ... Read books about friendships."
- [What Parents Can Do to Support Friendships](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/what-parents-can-do-to-support-friendships.aspx) · HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics (last updated 7 Apr 2022) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: From the preschool years a parent can be present during play and help warm up first interactions for a very shy child.
  - quote: "Starting from the preschool years, you can be present and available when your children are playing with others. ... These early play times allow you to see how your child interacts with other children and highlights your child's internal social strengths and challenges. If your child is very shy, you will be able to help warm up initial interactions"
- [Tips for Parents: Supporting your child to make friends](https://gov.wales/sites/default/files/inline-documents/2024-09/advice-sheet-on-supporting-a-child-to-make-friends.pdf) · Welsh Government, Give it time parenting advice sheet (Crown copyright 2024, WG50353) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Play dates at home where the child is most relaxed, after which they may feel more relaxed with that friend at nursery; the parent saying hello to the other child and parent models the first step; reading books about friendships helps children understand friendships. Government advice sheet, no promise of a result.
  - quote: "While you can't make friends for them, you can help your child to practice how to be a good friend. ... Some children are naturally shy and making friends can be difficult. It can be really helpful to arrange play dates at your house where your child feels most relaxed. They should then feel more relaxed in nursery or school playing with that friend and in turn will begin playing with others. ... If they are playing next to another child, you could say "hello" to the other child and their parent so that your child learns a simple but effective skill to make the first step. ... Read books about friendships with your child ... This is a really simple but very effective way of helping children to understand friendships and what to do if the friends have conflict or fall out."
