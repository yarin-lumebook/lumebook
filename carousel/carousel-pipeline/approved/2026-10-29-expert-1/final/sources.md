  - quote: "Advance planning can prevent struggles, but it won't eliminate every meltdown. ... You can help build a bridge back to reality by entering the room or sitting down near your child, quietly observing what they're doing. When you see signs that they're willing to engage, offer a warm smile of encouragement. ... This isn't the time to go over the rules, since a child in meltdown mode can't think logically. Simply hold your ground, using as few words as possible: "I know it's tough, but it's time to put your tablet away." ... Kids can benefit from having a spot where they can go to calm down. ... If your child starts to rage when screen time ends, ask them to go to their quiet spot until they feel better. If necessary, gently pick up a younger child or lead them by the hand. Tell them you'll be glad to see them again when they're ready to reconnect. ... Try it with younger kids, too: offer a beloved book, cuddle time with the family pet or a small chore that makes them feel part of the group."
