# Sources · kid-1 · 2026-12-05

- [How to Help Your Kids Handle Disappointment](https://childmind.org/article/how-to-help-your-kids-handle-disappointment/) · Child Mind Institute (Christina Frank, with clinical expert Carey Werley, LCSW), last reviewed 6 Nov 2023 · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Acknowledge the feeling without judging it, and remind a child of earlier letdowns he or she got through, including losing a big game. A general page on disappointment, used as general advice for losing a game.
  - quote: "listen to and acknowledge children's frustration and disappointment. ... It's also important to avoid judging their reactions. ... Remind your kids of things they've tolerated before, she says, whether it was not being cast in the school play, getting a lower-than-expected grade, or losing a big game. ... Let them know that even though this situation is different, they can use some of the same skills to get through it."
- [Sports for Kids With Learning and Emotional Challenges](https://childmind.org/article/making-sports-work-for-kids-with-learning-and-mental-health-challenges/) · Child Mind Institute (Rachel Ehmke), last reviewed 7 Aug 2025 · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Practising losing on purpose in small, low stakes ways, and planning what the body does when a child loses. The page is about sports for children with learning and emotional challenges; used as general advice for losing a game.
  - quote: "We do a lot of practice with losing on purpose ... not getting the ball, not getting the position that you wanted and sitting with that a little bit. ... What should they be doing with themselves and their body if they're feeling frustrated or overwhelmed? ... How do you keep your body calm when you lose?"
- [Teaching Your Child to Be a Good Sport](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/sportsmanship.html) · Nemours KidsHealth (medically reviewed by Margaux J. Barnes, PhD), date reviewed October 2023 · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Accepting a loss and looking at what to improve, asking what went well instead of who won, saying a kind word to others, and that adults are the example. A page about sports and sportsmanship, used as general advice for games at home.
  - quote: "Accept a loss and focus on what they can do to improve rather than getting upset or blaming others. ... After a competition, don't focus on who won or lost. Instead, try asking, "What did you do well during the game?" or "Was there something you wish you could have done better?" ... Support teammates by saying things like, "Good shot" or "Good try." ... Kids learn how to be a good sport from the adults in their lives, especially their parents and coaches."
- [How to Help Your Child Develop Empathy](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/how-to-help-your-child-develop-empathy/) · ZERO TO THREE, 1 Feb 2016 · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Reading stories about feelings is one of the ways Zero To Three suggests to help a child with big feelings. The page is about empathy, not about losing games; used only as support for reading stories about a hard moment.
  - quote: "Read stories about feelings."
