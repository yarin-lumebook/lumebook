  - supports: Regression is expected, one-on-one time, inviting the child to help, feeling included, praise for gentleness, reassurance of love, and reading children's books about siblings. AAP parent page, general advice.
  - quote: "Expect your child to regress a little. ... your toilet-trained child might suddenly start having 'accidents,' or they might want to take a bottle. ... Plan a one-on-one date with your child. ... Set aside special time for your child. ... Find ways to invite your child to help. ... You want to make sure your child feels included and not excluded. ... Praise them when they are gentle and loving toward the baby. ... Reassure your child that you will love them just as much after the baby is born, as you do now. ... Read children's books about newborns and siblings with your child."
- ["When Is He Going Back in Your Belly?" How to Help Older Siblings Adjust to the New Baby](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/when-is-he-going-back-in-your-belly-how-to-help-older-siblings-adjust-to-the-new-baby/) · ZERO TO THREE (April 10, 2017) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Regression is often a sign of stress, do not demand 'big kid' behaviour, invite help without forcing it, and keep some focus on the older child.
  - quote: "Taking steps backwards in development is often a sign of stress. ... Encouraging or demanding that older children act 'like a big boy or girl' often backfires. ... Encourage your older child to help with the new baby, but don't force it. ... Don't make everything about the new baby."
