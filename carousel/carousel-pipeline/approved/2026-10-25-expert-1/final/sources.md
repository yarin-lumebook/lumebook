# Sources · expert-1 · 2026-10-25

- [Childhood Fears and Worries](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/anxiety.html) · Nemours KidsHealth (medically reviewed by Zachary Radcliff, PhD, September 2023) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Fear of loud noises like thunder is common in young children (the page names ages 4 to 6); comfort with 'you're safe, I'm here', hugs, staying calm, putting the feeling into words, facing fears slowly and praising bravery help.
  - quote: "It's normal for children to feel afraid or worried at times. ... Things that are new, big, loud, or different can seem scary at first. ... Young kids may also be afraid of loud noises, like thunder or fireworks. ... Comfort your infant, toddler, or very young child by saying, "It's OK, you're safe, I'm here." Let your child know you're there to protect them. Give hugs and soothing words to help your child feel safe. ... Be calm and soothing. Help your child put feelings into words. ... Help your child slowly face fears. ... Help your child feel brave, and praise them when they confront fears. ... Stay calm when your child is fearful."
- [how can I help my child's anxiety about storms and bad weather?](https://childrensdayton.org/the-hub/how-can-i-help-my-childs-anxiety-about-storms-and-bad-weather/) · Dayton Children's Hospital (last reviewed 21 February 2023) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Storm fears are common; lightning, booming thunder, wind and rain together can overwhelm some children; a comfortable spot away from windows, reading a book together there, a storm kit used only in storms, a flashlight, understanding what causes storms and calm parents (children take cues from them) can ease the fear. Written for US storms including tornadoes; we use only the thunder and rain parts.
  - quote: "Fears about thunderstorms, tornadoes or other inclement weather are common in children. Storms can be very overwhelming for some children, with flashes of lightning, booming thunder, high winds and pounding rains resulting in sensory overload. ... Parents can also identify a more comfortable, fun place in the home where children can go during nonemergent storms. This place might be away from windows and the sights and sounds of the storm. Having some relaxing time with a parent or a sibling in this space (e.g., reading a book together) can help ease children's anxiety. ... a "storm kit" that the children will only use during storms. Such a kit might include fun activities like puzzles, markers, fidgets and battery powered games. ... A fun flashlight or lantern with batteries can also help ease anxiety in case the power goes out. ... understanding what causes storms can ease some children's anxiety ... Because children clearly take cues from their parents, they are more likely to feel at ease during a storm if their parents remain calm during a storm."
- [How to Help Kids who are Afraid of Storms](https://www.boystownhospital.org/knowledge-center/help-kids-scared-thunderstorms) · Boys Town National Research Hospital (reviewed by Mara P. Paradis, M.D.; no date on the page) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Staying calm helps children feel at ease; explain how thunder and lightning work; make the storm time special with forts, a talent contest or a job like finding the flashlights; a hug from a parent or a stuffed animal helps them feel secure; answer honestly.
  - quote: "Often kids grow out of this fear as they age ... Keep your cool. Kids tend to feed off of their parents' moods so if you remain calm, they will feel more at ease as well. If your child is interested in nature, turn the storm into a science lesson. Explain how the thunder and lightning works. ... make the time during the storm special. Have a family talent contest, build forts or assign your child a special job like finding all of the flashlights in the house. Hug it out. Whether it's you or a favorite stuffed animal, sometimes children just need someone or something to hug to feel more secure. Be honest. If your child asks a question, don't lie."
- [Is your child scared of storms? Read this](https://www.ahchealthenews.com/2025/07/07/tips-for-children-scared-of-storms/) · health enews, Advocate Children's Hospital (pediatrician Emma Olivera, 7 July 2025) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: A young child's storm fear may come from loud noises; talk about what they like and dislike about storms without minimizing; role play a thunderstorm with phone app noises before a storm; explain what is happening; stay calm; say when the storm is over or easing; feeling prepared helps.
  - quote: "Depending on their age, a child's fear or anxiety around storms may come from loud noises. ... Discuss what things about storms your child likes and dislikes and avoid minimizing their fears. Active listening is important. ... Role play with your child by replicating a thunderstorm, using noises from a phone app or materials at home, and act out your safety plan. ... Explain what is happening during the storm. If your child understands what is going on around them, they may have decreased anxiety. Stay calm and reassure your child. ... Reassure your child the storm is over or lightening up based on weather information. ... Feeling prepared, whether you are a child or an adult, can go a long way when it comes to managing feelings of anxiety or fear."
- [Understanding Lightning: Thunder](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-thunder) · NOAA National Weather Service · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Thunder is the sound lightning makes when it heats the air; counting the seconds from the flash to the thunder tells how far the lightning is (more seconds, farther away), counted from a safe place indoors.
  - quote: "Thunder is the sound caused by a nearby flash of lightning ... Thunder is created when lightning passes through the air. The lightning discharge heats the air rapidly and causes it to expand. ... If you count the number of seconds between the flash of lightning and the sound of thunder, and then divide by 5, you'll get the distance in miles to the lightning: 5 seconds = 1 mile, 15 seconds = 3 miles, 0 seconds = very close. Keep in mind that you should be in a safe place while counting."
