# Sources · kid-4 · 2026-12-03

- [Social Development in Preschoolers: Learning How to Share & Cooperate](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Social-Development-in-Preschoolers.aspx) · HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics (last updated 5 Dec 2024) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Help the child see the other child's side, ask for an apology only once they understand, explain why they apologize, give it time, and accept their feelings.
  - quote: "Help your child see the situation from the other child's point of view ... Once they understand what they've done wrong—but not before—ask them to apologize to the other child. ... simply saying "I'm sorry" may not help your child correct their behavior ... they also need to know why they're apologizing ... They may not understand right away, but give it time. ... Let them know you understand and accept their feelings."
- [Communication Skills Start at Home](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Components-of-Good-Communication.aspx) · HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics (last updated 16 Mar 2023) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Teach the child to name what happened, model a full apology that says what you are sorry for, and talk about the behavior, not the child.
  - quote: "We often teach children to say "I'm sorry" without teaching them to acknowledge the issue. ... I am sorry I yelled at you. Mommy felt frustrated with the mess. ... Model full apologies ("I am sorry for..."). ... Focus on the behavior, not the child."
- [How to Help Kids Learn to Fail](https://childmind.org/article/how-to-help-kids-learn-to-fail/) · Child Mind Institute (last reviewed or updated 6 May 2025) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Name the frustration first, give time to feel it, and when the child is ready talk about what to do differently next time.
  - quote: "When your kid does fail at something, let them know you see how frustrated and disappointed they are. ... Skipping right to bouncing back doesn't give kids the time they need to feel their feelings. ... When your kid is ready, talk about what went wrong and how they might do things differently next time. ... You and your child can try to come up with what they could do the next time for a better chance at success."
- [Recommended Reading: Books to Teach Kids About Feelings & Resilience](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/recommended-reading-books-to-teach-kids-about-feelings-and-resilience.aspx) · HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics (last updated 24 Apr 2025) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Reading together about feelings helps children recognise their own and understand others'. Used only as support for reading stories about a hard moment; no promise of a result.
  - quote: "Reading books together about common feelings can help children recognize their own emotions. It can also help them develop empathy, understanding how others may be feeling."
