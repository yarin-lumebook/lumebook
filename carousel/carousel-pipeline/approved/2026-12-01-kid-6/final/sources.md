# Sources · kid-6 · 2026-12-01

- [Important Milestones: Your Child By 18 Months](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/18-months.html) · CDC, Learn the Signs. Act Early (last reviewed 16 May 2026) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Face the child at eye level, name body parts, add to the words the child says, ask simple questions, read books and talk about the pictures with simple words. Around 18 months a child tries to say three or more words besides mama and dada.
  - quote: "Tries to say three or more words besides “mama” or “dada” ... Talk with your child by facing her and getting down to her eye level when possible. ... Start to teach your child the names for body parts by pointing them out ... Teach your child more words by adding to the words she says. ... Ask simple questions to help your child think about what's around her. ... Read books and talk about the pictures using simple words."
- [Language Development: 1 Year Olds](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Language-Development-1-Year-Olds.aspx) · HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics (from Caring for Your Baby and Young Child, 2009; page dated 8/1/2009) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: First words are often familiar people, favourite things and body parts; speak slowly and clearly with simple words, name objects and body parts, answer with the correct pronunciation and never ridicule mistakes. An older page (2009), used as general advice.
  - quote: "his first few words probably will include the names of familiar people, his favorite possessions, and parts of his body. ... try speaking slowly and clearly, using simple words and short sentences. ... Teach him the correct names of objects and body parts ... answer with a correct pronunciation of the word ... By all means, don’t ridicule his language mistakes."
