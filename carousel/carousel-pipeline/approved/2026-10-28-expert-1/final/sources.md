# Sources · expert-1 · 2026-10-28

- [Fussy eaters](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/weaning-and-feeding/fussy-eaters/) · NHS (page last reviewed 13 November 2023) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: A child will sometimes eat for someone else, such as a grandparent, without any fuss; children learn new foods by copying, so eat with them; make mealtimes enjoyable and chat about other things; invite children who are good eaters without talking about how good they are; do not leave meals until the child is too hungry or tired. NHS advice for parents of fussy eaters (baby and toddler section); applied to ages 3 to 4.
  - quote: "Ask an adult that your child likes and looks up to to eat with you. Sometimes a child will eat for someone else, such as a grandparent, without any fuss. ... The best way for your child to learn to eat and enjoy new foods is to copy you. Try to eat with them as often as you can. ... Make mealtimes enjoyable and not just about eating. Sit down and chat about other things. ... If you know any other children of the same age who are good eaters, ask them round for tea. But do not talk too much about how good the other children are. ... Do not leave meals until your child is too hungry or tired to eat."
- [10 Tips for Parents of Picky Eaters](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/nutrition/Pages/Picky-Eaters.aspx) · HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics (source: AAP Committee on Nutrition, last updated 4/26/2018) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Share family meals with no TV or phones; serve one meal for the whole family with at least one food the child likes; toddlers tend to enjoy food with a dip. Written for toddlers; applied to ages 3 to 4 as the same stage.
  - quote: "Share a meal together as a family as often as you can. This means no media distractions like TV or cell phones at mealtime. Use this time to model healthy eating. ... Serve one meal for the whole family and resist the urge to make another meal if your child refuses what you've served. This only encourages picky eating. Try to include at least one food your child likes with each meal and continue to provide a balanced meal, whether she eats it or not. ... Kids this age also tend to enjoy any food involving a dip."
- [Peas, please! Food familiarization though picture books helps parents introduce vegetables into preschoolers' diets](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/72923/) · Owen L.H., Kennedy O.B., Hill C., Houston-Price C., Appetite 2018;128:32-43 (peer reviewed, University of Reading repository) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Picture books that make a vegetable familiar raised toddlers' interest in tasting it and their liking of it. A randomised study with 127 toddlers aged 21 to 24 months; picture books about one target vegetable, read daily for two weeks before taste exposure. It does not test personalised books.
  - quote: "Repeated taste exposure is an established means of increasing children’s liking and intake of fruit and vegetables. However, parents find it difficult to offer children disliked foods repeatedly, often giving up after a few attempts. Studies show that familiarizing children to fruit and vegetables through picture books can increase their interest in tasting targeted foods. ... compared to the control group who experienced only taste exposure, looking at vegetable books enhanced children’s liking of their target vegetable post-intervention (p<.001) and at follow-up (p<.05), and increased consumption of the vegetable at follow-up (p<.01)."
