# Sources · expert-1 · 2026-10-28

- [Risk Factors and Consequences of Food Neophobia and Pickiness in Children and Adolescents: A Systematic Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11720204/) · del Campo C. et al., Foods 2024;14(1):69 (systematic review, PMC) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Wariness of new food is a useful mechanism in early childhood and a natural stage that usually resolves; texture is the main reason children reject or accept foods. A systematic review on food neophobia and pickiness; the quoted lines are its background statements.
  - quote: "Currently, neophobia is a useful mechanism in early childhood linked to the development of a sense of taste; the attitude of distrust toward novelty protects the child from the danger of eating something potentially dangerous to health ... Sensory characteristics have been reported as the most influential determinants of eating behavior, and among these, textures are the main reason for food rejection or acceptance in children ... Food neophobia is a natural development stage that usually resolves spontaneously"
- [10 Tips for Parents of Picky Eaters](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/nutrition/Pages/Picky-Eaters.aspx) · HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics (source: AAP Committee on Nutrition, last updated 4/26/2018) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Include one liked food at each meal; food bridges to foods of similar color, flavor and texture; 10 or more tastes; wait a week or two before offering the same food again; pair bitter vegetables with familiar salty foods such as broccoli with grated cheese; no treats as bribes; the parent provides and the child decides whether to eat. Written for toddlers; applied to ages 3 to 4 as the same stage.
  - quote: "Serve one meal for the whole family and resist the urge to make another meal if your child refuses what you've served. This only encourages picky eating. Try to include at least one food your child likes with each meal and continue to provide a balanced meal, whether she eats it or not. ... Once a food is accepted, use what nutritionists call "food bridges" to introduce others with similar color, flavor and texture to help expand variety in what your child will eat. ... Just because a child refuses a food once, don't give up. Keep offering new foods and those your child didn't like before. It can take as many as 10 or more times tasting a food before a toddler’s taste buds accept it. Scheduled meals and limiting snacks can help ensure your child is hungry when a new food is introduced. ... To minimize waste, offer new foods in small amounts and wait at least a week or two before reintroducing the same food. ... Try serving unfamiliar foods, or flavors young children tend to dislike at first (sour and bitter), with familiar foods toddlers naturally prefer (sweet and salty). Pairing broccoli (bitter) with grated cheese (salty), for example, is a great combination for toddler taste buds. ... Tempting as it may be, try not to bribe your children with treats for eating other foods. This can make the "prize" food even more exciting, and the food you want them to try an unpleasant chore. It also can lead to nightly battles at the dinner table. ... It's a parent's responsibility to provide food, and the child’s decision to eat it. Pressuring kids to eat, or punishing them if they don't, can make them actively dislike foods they may otherwise like."
- [Toddlers at the Table: Avoiding Power Struggles](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/toddler-meals.html) · Nemours KidsHealth (medically reviewed by Mary L. Gavin, MD, January 2021) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: The parent chooses what goes on the plate and need not serve the same favorite daily; most food jags don't last long; small portions of new foods. Written about toddlers; applied to ages 3 to 4 as the same stage.
  - quote: "You choose the foods on your toddler's plate — and you don't have to serve macaroni and cheese daily. If you do, you miss a chance to introduce new foods and increase the number of foods your child is willing to eat. Most of these "food jags" won't last long if parents don't give in to them. ... Especially with foods that aren't yet favorites, a couple of tablespoons is plenty to start with. Small portions are less overwhelming"
- [Peas, please! Food familiarization though picture books helps parents introduce vegetables into preschoolers' diets](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/72923/) · Owen L.H., Kennedy O.B., Hill C., Houston-Price C., Appetite 2018;128:32-43 (peer reviewed, University of Reading repository) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Picture books that make a vegetable familiar raised toddlers' interest in tasting it and their liking of it. A randomised study with 127 toddlers aged 21 to 24 months; picture books about one target vegetable, read daily for two weeks before taste exposure. It does not test personalised books.
  - quote: "Repeated taste exposure is an established means of increasing children’s liking and intake of fruit and vegetables. However, parents find it difficult to offer children disliked foods repeatedly, often giving up after a few attempts. Studies show that familiarizing children to fruit and vegetables through picture books can increase their interest in tasting targeted foods. ... compared to the control group who experienced only taste exposure, looking at vegetable books enhanced children’s liking of their target vegetable post-intervention (p<.001) and at follow-up (p<.05), and increased consumption of the vegetable at follow-up (p<.01)."
