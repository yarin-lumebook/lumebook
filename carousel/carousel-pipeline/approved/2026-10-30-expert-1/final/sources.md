# Sources · expert-1 · 2026-10-30

- [Nightmares](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/nightmare.html) · Nemours KidsHealth (medically reviewed by Amy W. Anzilotti, MD, June 2023) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: After a nightmare the images feel real; a cozy bed with a favorite toy or nightlight helps; check the closet and under the bed with the child; a nightlight, hall light or bedside flashlight helps the child feel safe going back to sleep; a stuffed animal or soft music; a kiss to hold in the palm while tiptoeing out; reading is part of a calming bedtime routine.
  - quote: "When kids wake up from a nightmare, its images are still fresh and can seem real. So it's natural for them to feel afraid and upset and to call out to a parent for comfort. ... have a bed that's a cozy, peaceful place to quiet down. A favorite toy, stuffed animal, nightlight, or dream catcher can help. ... Go ahead and check the closet and under the bed, reassuring your child that all's clear. ... A nightlight or a hall light can help kids feel safe in a dark room as they get ready to go back to sleep. A bedside flashlight can be a good nightmare-chaser. ... offer a favorite stuffed animal to hold, a blanket, pillow, nightlight, or dreamcatcher, or play soft music. ... And maybe seal it by giving your child a kiss to hold — in the palm of their hand — as you tiptoe out of the room. ... This might include a bath, a snuggle from you, reading, or some quiet talk about the pleasant events of the day."
- [Nightmares, Night Terrors & Sleepwalking in Children: How Parents Can Help](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Nightmares-and-Night-Terrors.aspx) · HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics (last updated 21 Sep 2023) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Nightmares often come in the second half of the night; children may have trouble falling back to sleep; comfort them; go to the child; allow a light; check what is scaring them.
  - quote: "Nightmares are scary dreams that often happen during the second half of the night when dreaming is most intense. ... Children may wake up crying or feeling afraid and may have trouble falling back to sleep. If your child has a bad dream, the best response is to comfort them. ... Go to your child as quickly as you can. ... Allow them to keep a light on if it makes them feel better. ... See if there is something that is scaring your child, like shadows. If so, make sure they are gone."
- [Nightmares](https://sleepeducation.org/sleep-disorders/nightmares/) · AASM Sleep Education, American Academy of Sleep Medicine (October 2020, reviewed by Reeba Mathew, MD and Anne M. Morse, DO) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: A special blanket or stuffed animal and a dim nightlight help the bedroom feel safe.
  - quote: "Let your child sleep with a special blanket or stuffed animal for security. Plug in a dim nightlight so your child's bedroom feels safe."
