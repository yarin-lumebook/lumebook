# Sources · kid-1 · 2026-10-27

- [8 Ways to Help Your Child When They're Being Excluded](https://bannerhealth.com/healthcareblog/teach-me/what-to-do-when-your-child-feels-left-out) · Banner Health (Regan Olsson, with Adeola Adelayo, MD, psychiatrist at Banner Behavioral Health Hospital), 6 June 2021 · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: When a child is left out: listen without interrupting or diminishing, then validate the feeling; show the child they are loved at home. Written mostly about school-age children; applied to a 4 year old as a related situation.
  - quote: "try not to react too quickly ... Encourage them to talk and then let them talk ... don't interrupt, criticize or diminish what they're sharing with you. 2. Validate feelings When they're done sharing, validate their feelings and demonstrate you understand how they feel. ... Tell and show your child that they are unconditionally loved and valued by their family."
- [Social Development in Preschoolers: Learning How to Share & Cooperate](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Social-Development-in-Preschoolers.aspx) · HealthyChildren.org, American Academy of Pediatrics (last updated 5 Dec 2024) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Giving preschoolers words for their feelings and wishes.
  - quote: "Help your child with the appropriate words to describe their feelings and desires so that they don't feel frustrated."
- [Supporting your child to make friends (Tips for Parents)](https://gov.wales/sites/default/files/inline-documents/2024-09/advice-sheet-on-supporting-a-child-to-make-friends.pdf) · Welsh Government, 2024 (WG50353) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Children in nursery or school who feel left out: role play with toys at home, daily one-to-one time to talk (bedtime is a good time), and reading books about friendships.
  - quote: "Others might get upset if they feel left out ... You can help them at home by role playing with toys ... Read books about friendships with your child ... This is a really simple but very effective way of helping children to understand friendships and what to do if the friends have conflict or fall out. ... Set aside some one-to-one time everyday to ask how their day went and how they are feeling. Bedtime can be a good time for this."
- [Helping Kids Cope With Cliques](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/cliques.html) · Nemours KidsHealth (medically reviewed by Elenda T. Hessel, PhD, June 2023) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Who is in and who is out can change from day to day; help the child see that things change quickly. Written about cliques in older children; applied to a gan day as a related situation.
  - quote: "And some may be in today and out tomorrow. ... Help put rejection in perspective. Remind your child of times they've been angry with parents, friends, or siblings ... how quickly things can change."
