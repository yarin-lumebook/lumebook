# Sources · kid-1 · 2026-12-06

- [Dummies](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/dummies) · Better Health Channel, Victorian Department of Health · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Stopping gradually, limiting the dummy to sleep, preparing the child by talking for a couple of days, a chosen cuddly toy in its place and that the first few days are the hardest. An Australian state health service page, applied to Israeli families as general child health advice.
  - quote: "Give your child the chance to stop their dummy habit when they are ready. Most children stop sucking habits between the ages of two and four, but you can start the process from around one year of age. ... Avoid trying to stop dummy use suddenly, as it can lead to other oral habits such as thumb or finger sucking. ... Limit the use of the dummy to sleep or nighttime. ... Talk about stopping the dummy for a couple of days to prepare your child. ... Consider replacing it with a toy, maybe a cuddly toy they have chosen and can hold when they would be using the dummy. You can talk about the replacement when they are asking for the dummy. ... Persist gently but firmly. The first few days without a dummy are likely to be the most difficult."
- [Weaning off dummies and bottles](https://library.sheffieldchildrens.nhs.uk/weaning-off-dummies-and-bottles/) · Sheffield Children's NHS Foundation Trust, Resource Library · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Dummy only for naps and bedtime first, a favourite toy for comfort, a dummy free date once use is down, praise, keeping it out of sight and making the goodbye fun (a Dummy Fairy). An NHS trust leaflet for UK families, used as general advice.
  - quote: "Start by just letting your child use their dummy for nap times and bed time. Try to swap it out for their favourite toy or teddy to hold onto for comfort instead. ... Once they are using their dummy less, set a date for being ‘dummy free’. Help them by giving them lots of praise, and give them lots of positive attention. ... Make sure you and other caregivers remove any bottles and dummies from your child’s sight. ... Try creating some fun around leaving the dummy behind. For example use a ‘Dummy Fairy’ that takes it away during the night and leaves a small reward. Remember that this can be a difficult and challenging time for them."
- [How to Help Your Child Develop Empathy](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/how-to-help-your-child-develop-empathy/) · ZERO TO THREE (Rebecca Parlakian, 1 Feb 2016) · read 2026-10-29
  - supports: Reading stories about feelings is one of the ways Zero To Three suggests to help a child with feelings. The page is about empathy; used only as support for stories about a hard moment.
  - quote: "Read stories about feelings."
