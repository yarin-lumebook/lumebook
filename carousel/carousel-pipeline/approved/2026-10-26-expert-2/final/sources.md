# Sources · expert-2 · 2026-10-26

- [How to Help Kids Cope With Disappointment](https://www.pbs.org/parents/thrive/how-to-help-kids-cope-with-disappointment) · PBS KIDS for Parents, Katie Hurley, LCSW (16 Jan 2019) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: All or nothing thinking in young children; managing anticipation with hopes, possibilities and sure things; empathy, time to cry and a hug; meeting heat with calm; guide rather than fix; the talk later with questions; reading together as a calming idea.
  - quote: "It’s natural to build excitement for something like a family trip by talking about all of the wonderful things that might happen, but the word “might” is very important. Young children have a tendency to engage in all-or-nothing thinking. ... Make a list to indicate hopes, possibilities and sure things. ... Use empathic responses like, “I understand that this is difficult. I know you feel disappointed right now.” Give your child the time and space to cry, feel sad and soak up a hug from mom or dad. Connection helps kids recover from adversity. Meet heated responses with calm ones to model healthy coping strategies and save the discussion for a later, calmer moment. ... It’s important to act as a guide when it comes to managing setbacks instead of jumping in with the fix. ... How did it feel when that happened? What did you wish would happen? What can be done differently the next time? ... Read or ask a parent to read to you"
- [Age-by-Age Birthday Party Guide](https://www.pbs.org/parents/thrive/age-by-age-birthday-party-guide) · PBS KIDS for Parents (1 May 2024) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Involving a five year old in planning the party.
  - quote: "Five-year-olds know parties are very important and may enjoy the planning even more than the event itself. That's why it's especially important to involve your five-year-old in the entire process."
- [Temper Tantrums](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/tantrums.html) · Nemours KidsHealth (reviewed June 2022, Elizabeth M. Schilling, PhD) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: The parent's calm helps the child calm down.
  - quote: "Remind yourself that your job is helping your child learn to calm down. So you need to be calm too."
- [How to Help Your Kids Handle Disappointment](https://childmind.org/article/how-to-help-your-kids-handle-disappointment/) · Child Mind Institute, Christina Frank (reviewed 6 Nov 2023) · read 2026-10-24
  - supports: Acknowledge the disappointment instead of rushing to fix it. Written about cancelled events (including birthdays) during the pandemic; applied here to a birthday that goes differently.
  - quote: "What you can do is listen to and acknowledge children’s frustration and disappointment ... As parents, our first impulse is often to try to fix problems for our children. ... It’s also important to avoid judging their reactions."
