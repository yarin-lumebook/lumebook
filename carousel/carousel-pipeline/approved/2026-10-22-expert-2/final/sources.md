# Sources · expert-2 · 2026-10-22

- [Have an Aggressive Toddler? Here's How To Manage Their Behavior](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/toddler-hitting-and-aggressive-behavior) · Cleveland Clinic Health Essentials (25 June 2024, interview with pediatric psychologist Emily Mudd, PhD) · read 2026-10-16
  - supports: Aggression is expected at toddler age because language is not there yet; meeting big emotion with more emotion raises aggression; no physical force; redirect with keeping safe and a pillow; boundary then a choice; name the emotion; specific praise; say yes when it is not a safety issue; triggers and routines; see a pediatrician when daily functioning suffers. Written for toddlers, applied to ages 2 to 5 as an analogy.
  - quote: "We generally expect toddlers to experience some aggressive behaviors ... At this stage, kids tend to resort to physical expressions of their frustration, simply because they don’t yet have the language skills to express themselves. ... When a child is expressing a lot of emotion, and the parents meet that with more emotion, it can increase the child’s aggression ... Never respond with physical force such as spanking, hitting or restraining. ... I have to keep you safe. You can’t hit me, but you can hit the pillow. ... Set boundaries and then put the child in charge of the next choice ... help your child identify the emotion ... Reward good behavior, even when your child isn’t doing anything out of the ordinary. Make the praise specific. ... it’s OK to accommodate. ... Do tantrums happen every morning before school? Work on structuring your morning routine. ... Whatever the cause, if the behavior impacts your child’s day-to-day functioning, it’s time to seek professional treatment"
- [Temper Tantrums](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/tantrums.html) · Nemours KidsHealth (medically reviewed June 2022, Elizabeth M. Schilling, PhD, ABPP) · read 2026-10-16
  - supports: Tantrums (which include hitting) are a normal part of development; toddlers lack words for feelings; tiredness and hunger can set them off; a child who may hurt someone is taken to a quiet safe place; a calm down spot as a choice; a hug and reassurance once calm; small choices. Written for ages 1 to 3, applied here to ages 2 to 5 as an analogy.
  - quote: "Tantrums are a normal part of child development. ... Because toddlers can't always say what they want or need, and because words describing feelings are more complicated and develop later, a frustrating experience may cause a tantrum. ... Tantrums may happen when kids are tired, hungry, or uncomfortable. ... Kids who are in danger of hurting themselves or others during a tantrum should be taken to a quiet, safe place to calm down. ... Consider making a “chill out” or “calm down” spot in your home ... Now (when your child is calm) is the time for a hug and reassurance that your child is loved, no matter what. ... Offer minor choices such as"
- [Taming Tempers](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/temper.html) · Nemours KidsHealth (medically reviewed June 2022, Margaux J. Barnes, PhD) · read 2026-10-16
  - supports: Yelling at an outburst teaches yelling; speak calmly and firmly; wait until the child is calm before asking; do not push the child to talk right away; short house rules such as no hitting; enough sleep; letting the anger out safely; praising specifically; warnings before changes; a favorite book is one way to shift mood. The page is about tempers in general and applied here to hitting, biting and shouting.
  - quote: "Reacting to kids' meltdowns with yelling and outbursts of your own will only teach them to do the same ... Speak calmly, clearly, and firmly ... Once your child calms down, ask what got them so upset. ... But don't push too hard for your child to talk right then. They may need some time to reflect before being ready to talk. ... In this family, we don't hit, push, or shove. ... Make sure kids get enough sleep. ... Doing a bunch of jumping jacks, dancing around the bedroom, or going outside and doing cartwheels are all good choices. ... Point out specifically what you liked about the way they handled it ... if your child has trouble with changes, give warnings ahead of time ... reading a favorite book"
- [Teaching Your Child Self-Control](https://kidshealth.org/en/parents/self-control.html) · Nemours KidsHealth (medically reviewed June 2018, Lauren M. O'Donnell, PsyD) · read 2026-10-16
  - supports: Young children get frustrated by the gap between what they want and what they can do; for ages 3 to 5 end a break when the child calms down; praise staying in control; model self-control.
  - quote: "Infants and toddlers get frustrated by the large gap between the things they want to do and what they're able to do. ... end time-outs when your child calms down ... praise your child for not losing control in frustrating or difficult situations ... model good self-control yourself"
