Sitting Still Is Not Required Trying to read to a toddler who won't sit still can be frustrating. Be patient and keep trying. Find a book or a few pages that are interesting. If that doesn't work, don't force the reading but be sure to try again later. Remember that toddlers love repetition — if your child doesn't seem interested in books, you may need to find a favorite and read it over and over again. Some busy toddlers like to stand up while you read to them. Others like to look at a page or two before moving on to something else. Keep the book out — kids might want to return to it later, which you should encourage. It's OK if your child can't sit still for an entire book — toddlers' attention spans will get longer soon.

Dummies · Better Health Channel, Victorian Department of Health (reviewed 12 Jun 2026) · נקרא 2026-10-29

Avoid trying to stop dummy use suddenly, as it can lead to other oral habits such as thumb or finger sucking. Tips for removing the dummy Limit the use of the dummy to sleep or nighttime. ... Talk about stopping the dummy for a couple of days to prepare your child. Consider replacing it with a toy, maybe a cuddly toy they have chosen and can hold when they would be using the dummy. ... Encourage your child to give their dummy away to the dummy fairy, or to another parent of a younger child. Give plenty of encouragement and keep it positive. Persist gently but firmly. The first few days without a dummy are likely to be the most difficult.

When Should Kids Stop Using Pacifiers? · Cleveland Clinic (pediatrician Jason Sherman, DO, 1 Jan 2026) · נקרא 2026-10-29

“You may have to deal with a few days and nights of fussing,” Dr. Sherman concedes. “But stand firm.” ... Start removing your child’s pacifier when they’re at home, putting it somewhere they can’t see or find it. ... Children suck on pacifiers to self-soothe — and self-soothing is an important skill that helps them sleep. If your child is 1 or older, you can put them to bed with a light blanket or stuffed animal to help soothe them as you try to make the switch away from pacifiers. ... You could, for example, throw a goodbye party for their pacifiers or encourage your child to “trade in” their pacifiers for a new toy they get to pick out on their own. ... Kids are motivated by praise ... be sure to tell them what a great job they’re doing of not using their pacifier. ... If your child is accustomed to using their pacifier to sleep, you’ll want to have a plan in place to help them learn to sleep without it.
