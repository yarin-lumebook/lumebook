Give your child the chance to stop their dummy habit when they are ready. Most children stop sucking habits between the ages of two and four, but you can start the process from around one year of age. ... Avoid trying to stop dummy use suddenly, as it can lead to other oral habits such as thumb or finger sucking. ... Limit the use of the dummy to sleep or nighttime. ... Talk about stopping the dummy for a couple of days to prepare your child. ... Consider replacing it with a toy, maybe a cuddly toy they have chosen and can hold when they would be using the dummy. Weaning off dummies and bottles · Sheffield Children's NHS Foundation Trust, Resource Library · נקרא 2026-10-29

Start by just letting your child use their dummy for nap times and bed time. Try to swap it out for their favourite toy or teddy to hold onto for comfort instead. ... Once they are using their dummy less, set a date for being ‘dummy free’. Help them by giving them lots of praise, and give them lots of positive attention. ... Make sure you and other caregivers remove any bottles and dummies from your child’s sight. ... Try creating some fun around leaving the dummy behind. For example use a ‘Dummy Fairy’ that takes it away during the night and leaves a small reward. Remember that this can be a difficult and challenging time for them.
