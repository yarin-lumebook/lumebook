# Carousel hooks: platform facts and research mechanisms

Research notes for the LumeBook nightly carousel pipeline (slide 1 and slide 2 hooks, slide 7 CTA). Written 2026-10-08. This extends and re-checks `research/carousel-playbook-research/platform.md` (checked 2026-10-06) and `research/carousel-playbook-research/psych.md` (2026-10-06). It does not repeat what those files already cover. Where a mechanism is already there, I cite it as "psych.md section N".

Evidence labels used:
- **platform_supported**: current first-party Instagram or Meta guidance (or a direct quote of Instagram staff in a reputable outlet, flagged as such).
- **research_supported_mechanism**: peer-reviewed or large field-experiment research.
- **practitioner_claim** or **unverified**: anything else, with the reason given.

Nothing here is "proven". "Frequently observed", "high-performing" and "caused better performance" are kept apart. Where only an abstract was read, the row says so.

---

## 1. Method

**Searched.** About 12 WebSearch queries (Mosseri carousel second chance; Mosseri 2025 to 2026 ranking statements; Instagram originality 2026; Meta creative best practices; engagement bait on Instagram and Threads; @creators carousel tips; Banerjee and Urminsky findings; Kuiken et al. full text). Then direct reading:
- Meta and Instagram pages that render with JavaScript (transparency.meta.com, help.instagram.com, facebook.com/help, facebook.com/business/help, creators.instagram.com) were read as rendered text in the in-app browser, in one tab I opened and closed. I did not open instagram.com in the browser.
- Two Instagram post captions (Mosseri) were read with WebFetch only, which returns the caption and date but not the spoken video.
- Papers: DOIs confirmed and abstracts read through the OpenAlex and Crossref APIs. Full text read where an open copy existed: Gligorić et al. 2023 (Europe PMC full text), Kuiken et al. 2017 (University of Amsterdam repository PDF), and the January 2021 working-paper version of Banerjee and Urminsky (author's site PDF).

**Blocked or limited.**
- pubsonline.informs.org (Banerjee and Urminsky final paper) and tandfonline.com (Kuiken et al.) returned HTTP 403. I used the repository and working-paper copies instead, as noted per row.
- mashable.com blocked WebFetch, so I read it in the browser.
- theverge.com cannot be searched or fetched from this environment.
- The video content of Mosseri's Reels cannot be read. Only captions were read, plus direct quotes in news reports.
- I found no second reputable outlet, independent of Mashable, that quotes the slide-2 re-show. All other hits were marketing blogs repeating the same line (see P1 and section 4).

**Path note.** The brief's original paths were outdated. The existing notes are in `research/carousel-playbook-research/`, and this file is at `carousel/carousel-pipeline/research/notes/`, as the coordinator corrected.

## 4. Conflicts and gaps

**Conflicts**
1. **Question hooks.** Lai & Farbrot (M1) found question headlines win, especially self-referencing ones. Kuiken (M2: -17), the Banerjee & Urminsky working paper (M3: information-seeking cues negative) and Scacco & Muddiman (M5: summary headlines favoured) point the other way. Net: questions are not a reliable lift. A specific, self-referencing question is the only version with support.
2. **"You" address.** Packard & Berger (M13) and Sahni et al. (M14) are positive in songs and email. Gligorić (M4) found no effect for "you" in headlines, and the M3 working paper found headlines aimed at the reader "backfired". First-person "I" and third-person "she/he" (one person's story) were consistently positive in M4. That favours kid-voice "I" hooks and story hooks over "you" hooks.
3. **Quotes.** Kuiken (M2) found headlines that were entirely a quote did worse than headlines with no quote. A kid-voice slide 1 is, in effect, a full quote. M4 and M8 to M9 favour first-person voice. This is unresolved, and the only quote evidence is Dutch news. Worth an A/B in the pipeline: a child's line as a short quote plus a framing line, versus the quote alone.
4. **Negativity.** Negative words raise clicks (M4, M15, M2). Meta's clickbait definitions (P10), the Creators FAQ (P8), the brand rules and the guilt and shame research (M16 to M18) push against it. Clicks are not sends. Upworthy studied link clicks, while Instagram ranks sends and time spent (P2, P7).
5. **Headline across slides vs slide-2 re-show.** Instagram's Edits blog (P15) encourages headlines that span slides, while Mosseri (P1) says slide 2 is often shown first. Resolution: visual bleed is fine, split sentences are not.
6. **Comment-word CTA status.** The Creators FAQ says engagement bait is non-recommendable (P8). Meta's only definition is Facebook-scoped and exempts a "specific call to action" (P9). The current Instagram Recommendations Guidelines do not list engagement bait (P11). Meta itself offers a native keyword "Comment to message" automation (platform.md 4.4). **No first-party source says a comment-keyword-to-DM CTA is demoted on Instagram, and none says it is safe.** Mitigation: phrase it as a service ("כתבו 'ספר' ואשלח לכם קישור לתצוגה מקדימה"), never as "comment if you agree". Watch Account Status (P12).

**Gaps and things I could not verify**
- No second independent reputable report of the slide-2 re-show (P1). The Mosseri Reel's spoken content was not heard directly; only the caption and Mashable's quote were read.
- Banerjee & Urminsky final 2025 results (question, number and "you" directions) were read only in the 2021 preliminary working paper. The final article was behind a 403.
- Mosseri's 2025-12-31 20-slide text carousel on authenticity and "less polished" aesthetics was read only as a Social Media Today summary (2026-01-01, https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-outlines-the-challenges-of-ai-content/808674/) plus the caption. It is not quoted here as a fact.
- A widely cited Meta claim that "up to 47% of the value" of a video campaign comes in the first three seconds appeared only in a search snippet of an old Meta video-ads page. I did not open it, it is about video ads, and it is not cited.
- The 2017 Facebook engagement-bait announcement (https://about.fb.com/news/2017/12/news-feed-fyi-fighting-engagement-bait-on-facebook/, read via WebFetch) gives the definition and the exemption for "Posts that ask people for help, advice, or recommendations", but its list of bait types (vote, react, share, tag, comment) was not in the fetched text.
- The Explore tile's crop ratio is not documented (P6).
- I did not check whether Meta attaches an "AI info" label to LumeBook's generated images, or how that looks on slide 1.
- No Hebrew-language or Israeli headline study was found. Every headline study here is English or Dutch news, and measures clicks, not swipes, sends or saves. Gligorić's model picks winners only 54% of the time, and Kuiken found headline features alone cannot predict success: **topic and moment choice likely matter more than hook formula** (inference from these two).
- No study tests a child's "inner voice" addressed to the parent as a persuasion device. M8 to M12 are the nearest evidence.

## 5. Ten-line summary for writing slide 1 and slide 2 hooks

1. Slide 2 is a second cover: Instagram often re-shows an unswiped carousel from slide 2 (P1), so slide 2 needs its own self-contained hook line. Never split a sentence across slides 1 and 2 (P15).
2. Instagram predicts "swipe through the whole carousel" from swipes and cards viewed (P2). Slide 1's job is to earn the first swipe, not just a stop.
3. For non-followers, send rate matters most (P7) and an LLM rates how informative a post is (P5). Hooks should promise, and slides 2 to 3 should deliver, something a mum would send to her partner.
4. Clickbait and engagement bait are named by Instagram as non-recommendable (P8). Meta defines clickbait as withholding information or sensational phrasing (P10). Ban "you won't believe" style hooks, all caps and "!!!".
5. Open a specific gap and resolve it inside the carousel (M5, M6). Summary-style hooks with one open element are better supported than pure mystery.
6. Question hooks are not a reliable lift (M1 vs M2, M3, M5). If you use one, make it the parent's own specific question about her child.
7. First-person "I" and stories about one specific person were the most consistent positive wording cues in randomised headline tests (M4, M3). "You" was not (M4). This favours kid-voice "I" hooks and a named, aged child.
8. In kid-voice hooks, the child should notice or need, not accuse. If the mum is cast as the opponent, identification can be blocked (M11), and guilt or shame hooks get discounted or provoke anger (M16, M17; Coulter & Pinto in psych.md).
9. Keep slide 1 text short and centred. Text overlay is a modelled feature with unknown direction (P3), and Explore shows a cropped tile (P6).
10. The comment-word CTA on slide 7 has no first-party green light or red light on Instagram (P8, P9, P11). Phrase it as a service that delivers something specific, never "comment if you agree", and check Account Status weekly (P12).
